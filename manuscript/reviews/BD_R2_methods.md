These sentences generalise the forward finding to both directions. The decided reverse contrast is g_R = +1.69 [-0.72, 3.91] (b_r1_decide.json primary.g_R_ci95), which does not separate own-panel from other-panel slopes. ### 1. Summary of the submission

The paper merges a dataset-validity audit of KTVIC with a development-data study of training-set size. The audit covers single-writer references, a punctuation hazard that depends on the scoring pipeline, segmentation sensitivity, matcher versions and image overlap with UIT-OpenViIC. The training-set-size study (B-1, D0, B-R1) asks against which reference panel the gain from more training data registers. Its claims:
- B-1: +9.61 [7.18, 12.36] CIDEr-D per doubling on dev300, registered PASS.
- D0: on the 187 dev images that have UIT-OpenViIC captions, the slope is +10.59 against KTVIC references and +0.72 against the OpenViIC panel; gap +9.87 [6.71, 13.35], GO.
- B-R1: training on OpenViIC captions gives +2.98 on OpenViIC and +1.29 [-1.23, 3.75] on KTVIC; gap +1.69 [-0.72, 3.91], registered ASYMMETRIC.

The paper also releases a protocol card and an evaluation kit.

### 3. Assessment by criterion

**NOVELTY (3/5).** Scoring the same images against two separately written human panels, and comparing training-size slopes in both training directions, is a useful design. I found no exact precedent in a limited search; OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar were rate-limited and the arXiv API returned nothing. Bromonschenkel et al. (2026) is the closest captioning work and is cited and distinguished correctly. Conceptually, however, the study is an in-distribution vs out-of-distribution scaling contrast, and the 'accuracy on the line' and effective-robustness literatures are not cited. The audit parts are careful but incremental: single-writer adjacency, the punctuation/PTB hazard and segmentation sensitivity. Three 'first to report' statements about Tran et al. are priority claims that have not been verified.

**CORRECTNESS (3/5).** The bookkeeping is unusually good: hash-pinned rules, one-shot scripts, reproduction gates and a complete manifest. The interpretation of the reverse direction is not correct as written in the headline places (M1). The registered contrast g_R does not separate the two reverse slopes. ASYMMETRIC is registered as an equivalence reading. The 'not distinguishable from zero' result on KTVIC references weakens without the length penalty (+2.47 [-0.19, 5.19]). The conclusion's 'no measurable gain' converts absence of evidence into evidence of absence. Further issues: the CIs leave out training-seed variance (M4); the D0 GO threshold was nearly guaranteed, and the ASYMMETRIC margin depends on the data (M5); and the strength of the dev-only headline is not matched by its evidence tier (M3).

**CONTRIBUTION (3/5).** For LRE, the most durable contributions are the protocol card, the punctuation and segmentation audit, the overlap disclosure and the kit. The forward two-panel result is a practically important caution for Vietnamese captioning: a KTVIC training-size gain is largely agreement with KTVIC's own references. The evidence is dev-only, on 187 images, with one backbone and a 2-seed reverse pilot. The contribution is a well-documented hypothesis-generating result, not an established finding about what training-data gains measure in general.

**CLARITY (2/5).** The prose is precise sentence by sentence, but the merged paper's structure works against the reader. The title leads with training-data gains, while the introduction and contributions lead with the card and kit. The headline appears late, in §5.4–5.5, as dense prose with roughly 40 CIs and no summary table. Internal labels leak into the text (D0, P1 SESOI, O5b, b_O^R). Cross-references and citation years are inconsistent.
