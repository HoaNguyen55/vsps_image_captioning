## 0. Bottom line

1. One idea survives: "What is a second reference worth in images?" It is an analysis contribution, not a new
   method: a budget-matched, metric-dependent references-vs-images exchange rate for SFT of a pretrained VLM on
   partial human references.
   - Part (a): the number of references fused into a target sets the recall operating point.
   - Two independent pilots support (a):
     - the trained models inherit their targets' offline P/R ratios;
     - a frozen-feature surrogate reproduces the trained fuse2-detailed vs P1 deltas almost exactly
       (dP -10.4 vs -10.4, dR +19.7 vs +18.5).
   - Part (b): the open quantity is the 7B's image elasticity of precision.
     - In the surrogate (an upper bound) precision is data-limited: +2.75 +/- 0.5 points per doubling.
     - A within-model proxy puts the 7B at about +0.8 (P1) to +2.2 (fuse2).
     - P1's false mentions sit in classes with at least 63 training mentions (77 % [72, 83]).
   - In the surrogate, at an equal caption budget, references win recall by a lot: P1 would need 5-34x more images.
     Images win precision. For threshold-free ranking quality the two are roughly interchangeable (a second random
     reference is worth about 0.8 images).
   - The decisive grid costs 9.25 GPU-h. Of that, 1.5 GPU-h only evaluates the fuse2 half-data adapters that the
     cross-fit E5 jobs already train; their data already sits at /root/ncs-data/xfit/gold_fuse2_{A,B}.

2. Q2, adapter variants (rank, targets, DoRA, PiSSA, rsLoRA, LoRA+): a well-founded negative. Do not run them.
   - The current setup is all LM linear layers, r=16, lr 1e-4, with about 0.06-0.2 M target tokens. That is
     LoRA's "low-regret" regime:
     - QLoRA, App. A.1: rank is irrelevant when LoRA sits on all layers.
     - Zhang et al., ICLR 2024: scaling PET parameters is ineffective.
     - LoRA Without Regret, 2025: LoRA matches full fine-tuning at this scale.
   - Published variant gains on vision-language tasks are 0.7-1.9 points (DoRA, r=128) and 1-2 % (LoRA+).
     "Which LoRA?" (2026) finds no significant advantage in multilingual tuning.
   - Our single-seed minimum detectable effect (MDE) is 2.5-3.8 points, so any sweep would be uninformative.

3. Q2, epochs and vision tokens: two inference-only checks, 1.65 GPU-h in total. Both are good engineering, not
   novelty.
   - The free epoch-1 checkpoint of fuse2_s42 (942 steps, the same count as P1).
   - fuse2_s42 with the vision cap at 1,024 tokens. KTVIC images are near native size at 512, so this changes
     only 271 of 545 test images (mean 417 -> 593 tokens).

4. Q3, curriculum (single-reference rows first, then fused rows): a well-founded negative. Do not run it.
   - Sequential multi-skill SFT risks forgetting; mixing is the safer default (Dong et al., ACL 2024).
   - Captioning curricula gave about 1-2 CIDEr to from-scratch captioners (Dual-GCN, ACM MM 2021; arXiv
     2212.07075). That is far below our MDE.
   - It would also need a custom sampler in the HF trainer.

5. The power analysis changes how A6-style comparisons must be read.
   - On 558 test images, a single-seed paired comparison detects only effects of 2.5-3.8 points or more in
     object P/R/F1/CHAIR_i.
   - CIDEr is seed-dominated. With P1 at three seeds:
     - Corpus CIDEr is 149.8 / 143.9 / 143.7 (mean 145.8, sd 3.5).
     - Object F1 is 54.1 / 54.5 / 55.2 (sd 0.56).
     - Two of the three same-recipe seed pairs differ "significantly" in CIDEr under the repo's paired image
       bootstrap: s42->s43 -5.9 [-11.1, -1.0], z -2.29; s42->s44 -6.1 [-10.7, -1.4], z -2.52.
     - Their object P/R differ by 1.0 point or less (|z| <= 1.6).
     - The P1-only seed s.d. of CIDEr is about 3.0. This puts the CIDEr MDE at about 16 for a single-seed
       recipe comparison and about 13 with 3 seeds per arm.
   - The published fuse2-vs-P1 gap of -5.9 CIDEr is therefore seed noise. fuse2_s42 (143.9) falls inside P1's
     seed range; it is -1.9 against the 3-seed P1 mean.
   - CIDEr cannot be a decision metric for recipe comparisons in this paper. Object P/R can.


## 1. Evidence already in the repo and logs (CPU only)

### 1.1 Power of a single-seed comparison on KTVIC test (pilot 1: power.py -> power.json, power.out)

Method.
- Paired image bootstrap (B=2000) over the 558 test images, counting per image exactly as object_prf.py and
  CHAIR-vi do.
- The image bootstrap cannot see seed-level variance. We estimate it from 9 same-recipe seed pairs:
  - P1: seeds 42/43/44.
  - VSPS-main: seeds 42/43/44.
  - VSPS-SFT: off4090sft / seed43 / seed44.
- Under the null hypothesis, z = d / SE_boot ~ N(0,1). Hence 2*sigma_u^2 = mean(d^2) - mean(SE^2).
- MDE (alpha .05 two-sided, power .8) = 2.80 * sqrt(SE_boot^2 + 2*sigma_u^2/k), with k seeds per arm.
- Two cases are reported:
  - "similar": SE_boot of a P1 seed pair, e.g. P1-50 % vs P1-100 %.
  - "different": SE_boot of P1 vs fuse2, e.g. fuse2-50 % vs P1-100 %.

Short mode:

| metric | mean z^2 (all 9 pairs / P1's 3 pairs) | sigma_u (P1) | SE_boot similar / different | MDE k=1 similar / different | MDE k=3 different |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| object P | 0.68 / 0.54 | 0.0 | 0.97 / 1.36 | 2.7 / 3.8 | 3.8 |
| object R | 2.22 / 1.31 | 0.25 (0.54 over all families) | 0.66 / 0.99 | 2.8 / 3.5 | 3.0 |
| object F1 | 1.83 / 1.31 | 0.25 | 0.69 / 1.05 | 3.0 / 3.7 | 3.2 |
| CHAIR_i | 0.94 / 1.18 | 0.31 | 0.95 / 1.33 | 2.7 / 3.7 | 3.7 |
| CIDEr-D | 1.40 / 3.86 | 2.98 | 2.49 / 3.85 | 13.9 / 16.0 (P1 sigma_u) | 12.7 |

Detailed mode:
- Object P / R / F1: 2.5 / 3.4 / 1.9-3.2.
- CHAIR_i: 3.5 / 4.6. P1 has sigma_u 1.3 here: s42->s43 z -3.07, s43->s44 z +2.56.

Reading the table.
- Pooling two replicate models per arm lowers SE_P (different recipes) only from 1.37 to 1.15-1.26, because
  image sampling dominates. The levers are more test images (SE ~ 1/sqrt(n)) and, for CIDEr, more seeds.
- The paper's fuse2-vs-P1 short-mode deltas, against these MDEs:
  - dR +5.7: 1.6 x MDE, robust.
  - dF1 +4.0: 1.05 x MDE.
  - dP -2.2: 0.6 x MDE.
  - dCIDEr -5.9: 0.4 x MDE.
- The two P1 seeds write the identical short caption for only 234 of 558 images. Which template the model locks
  onto varies with the seed, and CIDEr rewards template agreement with the references.

### 1.2 Training logs (pilot 2: n3 runs/fuse2_s42/metrics.jsonl and fuse2_s43/checkpoint-943/trainer_state.json; copies in n3logs/)

Cost.
- fuse2_s42: 13,798 s for 1,884 steps (7.32 s/step) = 3.83 GPU-h.
- LR decays linearly from 1e-4 with no warm-up; it is 5.0e-5 at checkpoint-943.

Prequential (fresh-data) learning curve. In epoch 1 every row is new, so the training loss is a held-out loss.
- Loss is about 0.638 nats/token at 1,884 rows, 0.58-0.59 at 3,768 and 0.53-0.54 at 7,536.
- That is about -0.05 per doubling, in both s42 and s43 (mean curve difference 0.005).
- Style is acquired in the first 100-200 steps: loss falls from 1.18 to 0.67.
- LR decays over the same window, so this is an upper bound on the pure data effect.

Memorisation.
- Across the epoch boundary the loss drops 0.543 -> 0.469, i.e. -0.074 nats/token.
- That is larger than the gain from a doubling of fresh data, so epoch 2 mostly memorises. This fits the 21.5 %
  verbatim-copy rate of P1 that the round-1 reviewers measured.

### 1.3 Offline target statistics vs data fraction (pilot 3: offline_stats.py -> offline_stats.json, offline_stats.out)

At an equal human-caption budget of 3,769 captions:

| arm | images | trained rows (steps at 2 ep) | captions that must exist | object mentions in targets | classes with >=5 mentions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1-100 % | 3,769 | 3,769 (942) | 3,769 | 6,099 | 72 |
| pair2-50 % (budget-honest) | 1,884 | 3,768 (942) | 3,768 | 7,729 | 66 / 69 |
| fuse2-50 % (reads 5, trains on 2) | 1,884 | 3,768 (942) | 9,420 | 9,610 | 67 / 72 |

- At the same budget, fusion carries 27 % (pair2) to 58 % (fuse2) more object supervision.
- It loses only 3-6 of the classes that have at least 5 mentions.

Leakage-free target quality (build from 3 captions, score against the other 2, 3,769 train images; the
human_parity.py "offline" protocol):

| target | P | R | F1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| p1 | 71.8 | 52.4 | 60.6 |
| cons | 71.0 | 56.5 | 62.9 |
| pair2, short row (richer of 2) | 68.1 | 58.8 | 63.1 |
| pair2, detailed row (2 joined) | 65.2 | 66.6 | 65.9 |
| fuse2 | 62.9 | 73.5 | 67.7 |

Two captions per image reach 97 % of fuse2's F1 without reading five.

KTVIC caption positions are not exchangeable. On train:
- Adjacent captions share more lexicon objects: Jaccard 0.48-0.55, against 0.38-0.41 for non-adjacent pairs.
- An adjacent second caption adds 0.61-0.76 new objects, a non-adjacent one 0.83-0.92.
- The budget-honest arm therefore draws its second caption at random from positions 2-5. "Caption 2" would
  under-sell a second annotator.

### 1.4 Where the trained models' object errors are (pilot 4: class_freq.py -> class_freq.out)

Test-time true positives, false mentions and misses are binned by how often their class occurs in the model's own
training targets. The 95 % CIs come from an image bootstrap (B=2000).

| model | share of false mentions in classes with >=63 train mentions | share in classes with <15 | P by frequency bin (bins with >=7 mentions) | top false mentions (train mentions) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 s42, short | 77.4 % [71.8, 82.5] | 5 % | 75-84, flat | người (946), đường (333), quầy hàng (203), phụ nữ (444), đàn ông (322) |
| P1 s43, short | 77.5 % [71.4, 83.0] | 4 % | 71-80, flat | same five classes |
| fuse2 s42, short | 86.2 % [82.3, 89.7] | 3 % | 65-80 | người, đàn ông, phụ nữ, áo (591), đường |
| fuse2 s42, detailed | 91.8 % [89.6, 93.9] | 1.5 % | 58-76 | người (1,522), áo (895), phụ nữ (773), đàn ông (589) |

- Misses follow the same pattern: 69-82 % are in classes with at least 63 training mentions.
- Deleting every false mention in classes with fewer than 15 training mentions would raise P1's precision by only
  0.9 points (77.9 -> 78.8).
- P1's precision gap to a human under the parity protocol (75.2 vs 85.3) is therefore not a class-coverage gap.
  It lies in person naming and in low-salience background classes the model has seen hundreds of times.
- More images from the same distribution add mentions of exactly these classes. That is the CPU-level reason to
  expect a small image elasticity.

### 1.4b A within-model proxy for the image elasticity (pilot 4b: class_elasticity.py -> class_elasticity.out)

Idea: doubling the images doubles every class's training count. So the cross-class slope of per-class P (or R) on
log2(training mentions) approximates the points gained per doubling. The fit is weighted, uses classes with >= 7
mentions, and gets 95 % CIs from an image bootstrap.

| model | P slope (pts per doubling) | R slope (pts per doubling) |
|---|---|---|
| P1 s42 | +0.77 [-0.88, +2.26] | +4.2 [+2.9, +5.3] |
| P1 s43 | +0.56 | +4.2 |
| P1 s44 | +0.99 | +4.2 |
| fuse2 short | +2.16 [+0.75, +3.68] | +3.9 |
| fuse2 detailed | +1.62 [+0.34, +3.09] | +5.3 |

The recall slope is not a valid proxy. Frequent classes are frequent because annotators find them salient, so the
slope mostly measures the salience the mention policy learned, not sample size.

The precision slope is less confounded. It bounds the data effect at about 1 P point per doubling for P1 (CI
includes 0) and about 2 for fuse2. Fused targets add low-salience objects, which plausibly need more examples.
- This weakens hypothesis (b) for fuse2: about -2 P at 50 % data is possible, which is below the MDE.
- It keeps hypothesis (b) plausible for P1.

### 1.5 The target sets the operating point (pilot 5: ratio transfer, offline -> trained)

If the trained model inherits its target's operating point, the trained fuse2/P1 P and R ratios should match the
offline ratios of their targets. Image-bootstrap CIs:
- Detailed mode:
  - offline fuse2/p1: P x0.876, R x1.401.
  - trained fuse2-det/P1-det: P x0.868 [0.838, 0.898], R x1.433 [1.369, 1.498].
  - Both offline ratios fall inside the trained CIs.
- Short mode:
  - offline cons/p1: R x1.078.
  - trained fuse2-short/P1-short: R x1.138 [1.089, 1.189].
  - The excess (about +5 % relative) is cross-task transfer from the fused rows. The queued cons-only ladder run
    measures it directly.
- The same rule gives pre-registered predictions for the ladder runs already queued (trained, 5-ref, test):
  - cons-short: P 77.0, R 44.6.
  - unf2-short: P 73.5, R 45.1.
  - multiref-short: P 75.4, R 40.4.
  - rnd2-detailed: P 70.8, R 54.3.
  - pair2-detailed (100 %, first-two-captions version): P 71.1, R 54.3.

## 3. Idea 1 — "What is a second reference worth in images?": a budget-matched, metric-dependent exchange rate for multi-reference SFT

### 3.1 Mechanism

SFT on a target policy pi makes the model reproduce two properties of its targets.
- The mention budget, i.e. how many objects a caption names. Targets per row become trained mentions per caption:
  P1 1.62 -> 1.80, cons 2.03 -> 2.09, fused 3.07 -> 3.15.
- The P/R ratios between policies (section 1.5).

So the number of fused references per target sets the recall operating point, whatever the number of images.
- Images only refine which objects cross the mention threshold.
- On a pretrained 7B whose false mentions sit in classes with hundreds of training mentions (section 1.4), that
  refinement should be small; this is what the grid measures:
  - style is learned within about 150 steps;
  - the prequential loss improves only about 0.05 nats/token per doubling (section 1.2).
- Prediction: a fused second reference on half the images beats one reference on all images in recall.
- Precision either stays within the MDE (7B elasticity of 1 point per doubling or less; LIMA regime) or drops about
  2.5-3 points per halving (if the 7B behaves like the surrogate). The grid measures which.
- In Muennighoff's effective-data form, D' = n_img * (1 + gamma * (k - 1)) for k references per image. The exchange
  constant gamma should be much larger than 1 for R, at most 1 for P, and about 1 for threshold-free ranking
  quality (surrogate: 0.77 for a random second reference).
- gamma = 1 means a second caption is worth exactly one new image (budget-neutral).

### 3.2 Closest prior and delta

Closest prior:
- Hessel et al. 2015 (budget-matched; NIC from scratch; Flickr30k n-grams; "more captions" wins by about 1 CIDEr).
- Chen et al. 2021 (captions per image at a fixed image count; COCO).
- Santurkar et al. ICLR 2023 (CLIP; more pairs beat more synthetic captions per image).
- Superficial alignment: LIMA; Dong et al. 2024; 2509.25568.

Delta:
1. A pretrained 7B VLM with QLoRA SFT, where superficial alignment predicts a different answer than from-scratch
   training.
2. References fused into one coverage target, not added as rows.
3. The effect decomposed into object recall, precision and CHAIR against human-parity ceilings, with n-gram metrics
   reported but not used for decisions (section 1.1).
4. A budget-honest arm that never reads more than 2 captions.
5. Falsifiable predictions written down before training: elasticities, gamma, and ratio-transfer predictions for
   the queued ladder (section 1.5).

I found no paper that reports the image elasticity of object P/R for VLM captioning SFT.

### 3.3 CPU pilots behind the prediction

(i) Error location (section 1.4). Of P1's false mentions:
- 77 % [72, 83] sit in classes with at least 63 training mentions (fuse2: 86 % short, 92 % detailed).
- Under 5 % sit in classes with fewer than 15.
- Precision is flat across frequency bins.

(i-b) Cross-class proxy (section 1.4b): precision slope about +0.8 points per doubling for P1 (CI includes 0) and
+2.2 for fuse2.

(ii) Ratio transfer (section 1.5). The target's offline P/R ratios reappear in the trained models:
- detailed R x1.401 offline vs x1.433 [1.37, 1.50] trained;
- detailed P x0.876 offline vs x0.868 [0.84, 0.90] trained.

(iii) Prequential loss (section 1.2): -0.05 nats/token per doubling, smaller than the 0.074 memorisation drop of
epoch 2.

(iv) Offline budget table (section 1.3). At 3,769 captions, pair2-50 % carries 27 % more object supervision than
P1-100 %, and its targets score F1 63.1 / 65.9 (short / detailed rows) vs P1's 60.6.

(v) Surrogate learning curves (surrogate.py -> surrogate_b16.out, surrogate_b16.json; surrogate_fit.py ->
surrogate_fit_b16.out).
Setup.
- The surrogate learns each policy's per-image object targets from frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 image embeddings. The model
  is a ridge linear-probability model with GCV-chosen lambda; cosine kNN (k = 10) is a robustness check.
- Training sets are random subsets of KTVIC train: 118 to 3,769 images, 8 replicates per size below 100 %.
- Decoding: each test image mentions the classes above one global threshold, chosen so that mentions per caption
  equal the policy's targets per row.
- Scoring is exactly object_prf's: exact match against the union of the 5 test references.
- The surrogate learns grounding from scratch, so its sensitivity to data is an upper bound for the VLM.

Calibration at 100 %. The surrogate reproduces the trained VLM's policy deltas:

| arm minus P1 | surrogate dP / dR / dF1 | trained VLM dP / dR / dF1 |
|---|---|---|
| fuse2-detailed | -10.4 / +19.7 / +11.2 | -10.4 / +18.5 / +9.1 |
| cons-short (vs fuse2-short) | -1.4 / +8.1 / +6.5 | -2.2 / +5.7 / +4.0 |

A linear probe on frozen features and the fine-tuned 7B shift P and R by the same amounts. That is direct evidence
for part (a): the target, not the learner, sets the operating point.

Image elasticity (ridge; points per doubling over 471-3,769 images, +/- 1.96 x replicate SE):

| policy | P | R | F1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | +2.75 +/- 0.52 | +1.23 +/- 0.26 | +1.70 +/- 0.34 |
| cons | +3.12 | +2.00 | |
| fuse2-detailed | +2.52 | +2.32 | |
| pair2r-detailed | +3.09 | +2.19 | |

kNN gives P1 P +1.83 and R +2.50. In this weak learner precision is data-limited, so part (b) is not supported here.
The open question for the GPU grid is how much of that elasticity survives a pretrained 7B. Pilot 4b bounds it at
about +0.8 (P1) and +2.2 (fuse2).

Budget-matched comparison (arm at 1,884 images vs P1 at 3,769; same number of trained captions):

| arm at 1,884 | dP | dR | dF1 | P1 images needed to match the arm's R |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cons-short | -3.9 | +6.3 | +4.4 | x34 (kNN: dR +5.6, dP -6.7, x4.7) |
| pair2-short (richer of 2) | -3.8 | +2.9 | +1.7 | x5.1 |
| pair2r-detailed (the GPU job's budget-honest target) | -6.9 | +10.4 | +6.6 | (vs P1 short) |

- Recall: gamma_R >> 1 (more images cannot buy the recall that references give).
- Precision: gamma_P < 1.
- Threshold-free ranking quality (AUROC of the class scores against the 5-reference union):
  - pair2r at 1,884 images scores 93.98 +/- 0.23 vs P1 at 3,769 scores 94.27, so gamma_AUROC = 0.77. A second
    random reference is worth about 0.8 of a new image for ranking.
  - fuse2 gives gamma 1.48 and unf2 gives 1.17.

So, before any GPU run, the pilot already shows the exchange rate is metric-dependent:
- references buy recall;
- images buy precision;
- for ranking quality the two are roughly interchangeable.
The GPU grid measures the one number the surrogate cannot give: the 7B's precision elasticity, which sets the
budget-matched P and F1 verdict.

### 3.5 Pre-registered decision rules and kill criteria

Settings: short mode unless stated, 5-reference object P/R as in Table IV, paired bootstrap, seed-inflated CIs
printed by exchange.py.

H1 (references set recall).
- Pass: R(fuse2-50 %) - R(P1-100 %, 3 seeds pooled) >= +3.5, and R(pair2-50 %) - R(P1-100 %) >= 0.
- Detailed mode: R(pair2-50 %) >= R(p1_s43-two) + 3.5.
- Kill H1 if fuse2-50 % does not beat P1-100 % in short-mode R by >= 3.5. The detailed-mode version is near
  certain and is not counted as evidence.

H2 (images barely move precision or recall).
- Pass: P and R of P1-25 %, P1-50 % and fuse2-50 % each lie within +/-3 of their 100 % anchor (TOST, margin 3), and
  the fitted |elasticity| is < 1 point per doubling.
- Kill H2 if any 25 % arm loses >= 3 P outside its CI. Precision is then data-limited. Even that is useful: it
  prices the human precision gap (about 10 points) at 2^(10/b) times more images.

Exchange constant.
- Report gamma_R, gamma_P and gamma_F1 with bootstrap CIs from exchange.py.
- Claim "a second reference is worth more than an image" only if the CI of gamma_R excludes 1 while gamma_P <= 1.

Side test (free; depends on the queued ladder). Ratio-transfer predictions:
- cons-short P 77.0 / R 44.6;
- unf2 73.5 / 45.1;
- multiref 75.4 / 40.4;
- rnd2-detailed 70.8 / 54.3.
Each should hold within the MDE (3.5 R / 3.8 P). If two or more miss, part (a) of the mechanism is wrong.

### 3.6 Confidence and value

- Confidence: H1 about 80 %. H2 about 45 %: the surrogate's precision elasticity of about 2.75 per doubling is an
  upper bound, and pilot 4b puts the 7B at about 0.8 (P1) to 2.2 (fuse2).
- Whichever way H2 falls, the result is a metric-dependent exchange rate, and that is the deliverable.
- Novelty: moderate. It is an analysis result with a clean protocol, not a method.
- Value for the paper:
  - It turns "fusion is a data-selection heuristic" into a dataset-design statement for low-resource languages:
    collect 2 captions per image before adding images.
  - It answers the 2x-steps confound with compute-matched arms.
  - It localises the human precision gap: not data-limited if H2 holds.


## 4. Questions 2 and 3: what to run, and what not to

| question | verdict | why | cheapest useful action |
|---|---|---|---|
| LoRA rank or targets (r 8/16/64; attention-only vs all) | do not run | QLoRA App. A.1 found r irrelevant with LoRA on all layers. Zhang et al. (ICLR 2024) found PET-parameter scaling ineffective. LoRA Without Regret found attention-only worse than MLP/all, and we already use all. Zhou et al. (Findings ACL 2024) found r16 = r128 on VL. Expected effect is under 1 point against an MDE of 3.8 | none |
| DoRA / PiSSA / rsLoRA / LoRA+ | do not run | Published VL gains are 0.7-1.9 points at r=128 (DoRA) and 1-2 % (LoRA+). rsLoRA matters only at high rank. "Which LoRA?" (2026) finds no reliable multilingual advantage. None has a CHAIR or object-P/R result on captioning SFT | If SFT compute ever binds: LoRA+ (B-matrix LR x16) for 1 epoch, after the free epoch-1 check below |
| Fewer epochs (1 vs 2) | free check | Epoch 2 mostly memorises: the boundary drop of 0.074 nats/token is larger than a doubling of fresh data. Gekhman et al. find late-fitted "unknown" examples drive hallucination | A6_5(a): evaluate fuse2_s42/checkpoint-943 in both modes (0.75 GPU-h). This is also the fuse2 side of the compute-matched 2x2 with P1-4ep (job 56). Caveat: that checkpoint is un-annealed (LR 5e-5) |
| Test-time vision tokens 512 -> 1,024 | cheap check | Only 271/545 test images change (mean 417 -> 593 tokens). ViCrop finds small details are resolution-limited, but our false mentions are frequent-class naming and salience errors (section 1.4) | A6_5(b): fuse2_s42 with --max-vision-tokens 1024 (about 0.9 GPU-h). Same weights, so the within-model SE is small (about 0.7-0.9 for P, like the tags check) and the MDE is about 2-2.5 |
| Training at 256 tokens (about 35 % shorter sequences) | not now | It saves about 1.1 GPU-h per fuse2 run, but at one seed it can only exclude losses above about 3.8 points | Only if an efficiency section is wanted, with 2 seeds |
| Forced upscaling to about 1,045 tokens (zoom) | not now | Needs a min-pixels flag in evaluate.py; it adds no information to the 274 small images | none |
| Curriculum (single-reference rows first, then fused; or the reverse) | do not run | Sequential SFT forgets (Dong et al., ACL 2024). Captioning curricula give about 1-2 CIDEr to from-scratch models. | Batched evaluation (engineering) | recommend, before the COCO and UIT-ViIC evaluations | Generation runs at batch 1, about 1.5 s/image short and 3.2 s/image detailed. ## 6. What would be novel and what is only good engineering

Novel (moderate; an analysis, not a method):
- The budget-matched exchange rate for SFT of a pretrained VLM.
- Its split into "references set the recall operating point" and "images mainly buy precision", with the 7B's
  own precision elasticity measured rather than assumed.
- Supporting evidence: per-metric image elasticities, the exchange constant gamma, the class-frequency account of
  the precision gap, and the ratio-transfer predictions validated on the ladder.

Methodological, partly known (Bouthillier et al. 2021; Picard 2021 for seeds; sample-variance work on CIDEr):
- The seed-dominated CIDEr finding for LoRA SFT of a VLM captioner. Two of three same-recipe P1 seed pairs are
  "significant" under the image bootstrap.
- The MDE table.
- The prequential learning curve read from training logs.
- Worth one methods paragraph and a footnote on every CIDEr comparison.

Engineering, not novel:
- the epoch-1 checkpoint check and the compute-matched 2x2;
- the 1,024-token check;
- batched evaluation;
- the decision not to sweep LoRA variants or curricula;
- held-out half-B evaluation (plain 2-fold cross-validation).


## 7. Practical consequences for the paper

- Stop citing "fuse2 -5.9 CIDEr vs P1". Use the 3-seed P1 mean (145.8 +/- 3.5). fuse2 is -1.9 against it.
- Make object P/R (seed-stable) the decision metrics.
- Add P1 at 1 epoch / fuse2 at 1 epoch, or the free checkpoint, to answer the 2x-steps confound from both sides
  (P1-4ep already covers one side).
- If H1 and H2 hold, a single sentence carries the dataset-design message: "for low-resource captioning, a second
  caption for half the images is worth more than the other half of the images."
- The UIT-ViIC in-domain runs (59a/59b) could replicate the result with COCO instance gold. They would need extra
  half-data runs (about 5 GPU-h), outside this budget.
