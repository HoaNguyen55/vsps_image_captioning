# A8: label-free self-training on unlabelled images (pilot, literature, designs)

Direction: pseudo-label images that have no Vietnamese caption with our own adapters, keep only what a
gold-free filter accepts, and train a student that beats fuse2 on KTVIC and transfers better.
Date: 2026-10-12. Every number comes from existing artifacts; no GPU was used.
Code and raw outputs are in this folder (section 7). The write tool blocks .md report files from sub-agents,
so this report is .txt.

## 0. Bottom line

### 0.1 Agreement between DIFFERENT systems cannot manufacture labels better than the teacher

"Different systems" here means different prompts, adapters, models, a detector, or a gold-free label model.
k-of-n agreement among them slides along a fixed precision/recall frontier.

- **COCO-5000, judged by human instance labels.** Keep the teacher's mentions that another source confirms:
  - dP +6.5 [+6.1, +7.0]
  - dR -3.8 [-4.1, -3.4]
  - dF1 +0.3 [-0.0, +0.6]
- **KTVIC test, human-parity protocol (1v4).** Same filter: dF1 -6.1 [-7.9, -4.4].
- **Machine vs human ceiling.** The best machine construct stays about 5 F1 and 12 P below a fuse2-style
  target built from two human references: COCO 86.0/73.2, vs at most 83.9/66.5 for any machine consensus.
- **Systematic errors survive voting.** 25.7% of the teacher's false positives survive at least-3-of-5
  agreement. They are mainly "dining table" and "chair", plus a matcher artifact: bare "kéo" (to pull) is
  read as scissors.
- **Label model.** Dawid-Skene ranks items worse than plain vote counting (AUROC 0.779 vs 0.798).

Committee, co-training and detector-agreement designs are therefore killed.

### 0.2 Voting over temperature samples of ONE adapter improves P and R together

This is the only construct that did. Data: 1,500 seen train images, 6 fuse2 samples each.

- **"Vote-then-fuse" (VTF).** VTF keeps the sample sentences whose every object has at least 3 of 6 votes,
  then takes at most 2 by greedy set cover.
  - VTF: P 72.2 [70.9, 73.6] / R 67.1 [65.5, 68.5] / F1 69.6.
  - One sample: F1 63.0.
  - Object-consensus MBR pick: F1 66.2.
  - VTF vs MBR pick: dF1 +3.3 [+2.5, +4.2]. VTF is also shorter (17.6 vs 24.6 words).
- **This is not memorisation of the trained references.** Against the annotators that never entered any
  training target for that image, VTF beats one sample by:
  - dP +7.0 [+5.9, +8.1]
  - dR +5.2 [+3.9, +6.5]
  - dF1 +6.4 [+5.4, +7.4]

  The samples themselves score 18 F1 below the targets they were trained on, and only 3.1% of their sentences
  are verbatim copies of that image's references.
- **Two warnings.**
  - Greedy committees of training seeds on unseen images gain about 0 from voting: +0.3 [-0.5, +1.2].
  - E5 (gold-selected self-samples on seen images, retrained) was null:
    - short dF1 -0.8 [-2.4, +0.7]
    - detailed dF1 +0.2 [-1.2, +1.6]

  The open quantity is VTF vs GREEDY on unseen images. Both designs below are gated on it.

### 0.3 Critical literature fact

UIT-OpenViIC is the natural in-domain pool: 13.1k Vietnam images with 61k human captions. It contains near
duplicates (SSCD >= 0.8) of:
- 401 of the 558 KTVIC test images (71.9%)
- 2,464 of the 3,769 KTVIC train images (65.4%)

Source: ViCLIP-OT, arXiv 2602.22678, App. B.

- Any pool must be de-duplicated.
- Side benefit: about 400 KTVIC test images also have independent, existing human captions. That is a second
  annotator panel for the paper's evaluation.

## 1. Data and protocol

### COCO-5000 (Karpathy test, val2014)

Existing Vietnamese caption sources, one caption per image each (data/results/coco5000, coco5000_vcd):
- zero-shot Qwen2.5-VL-7B, short prompt
- zero-shot, detailed prompt
- zero-shot, detailed prompt with VCD decoding
- old VSPS adapter, short prompt
- old VSPS adapter, detailed prompt

Scoring:
- Mentions: `score_coco_probe.vi_classes`, mapping text to the 80 COCO classes.
- Main gold: instance classes only, from the human boxes. The frozen instances + English-references gold is
  also in `pilot_coco.txt`.
- Human calibration: one English reference (Rohrbach synonyms) scored against the same instance gold.
- P = sum|M&G| / sum|M| (micro); R = per-image mean; F1 = harmonic mean (`object_prf.py` definitions).
- CIs: 95% percentile bootstrap over images, B = 2000, one shared index matrix, so paired deltas use the same
  draws.

### KTVIC test (558 images that no adapter trained on)

- Objects: the KTVIC lexicon (`rescap.chair.objects_in`).
- The 1v4 protocol of `human_parity.py`: each source is scored against the union of 4 of the 5 references,
  averaged over 5 folds.
- Reimplementation check: one held-out human gives 85.3 / 46.9, identical to
  `data/results/human_parity.json`.

### Within-model samples

- File: `/root/ncs-data/e5_round1/samples.jsonl` on n3.
- Contents: fuse2 s42, detailed prompt, T 0.8, top-p 0.95, 6 samples per image, 1,500 train (seen) images.
- No multi-sample file exists for unseen images. The queued cross-fitted RFT and the
  "object-consensus MBR over 6 samples" control will be the first ones.

**Dawid-Skene**
- It misestimates the source reliabilities: estimated P(mention | present) is 0.98-0.99, the true value
  0.60-0.87.
- It ranks items worse than vote count (AUROC 0.779 vs 0.798) and its frontier is dominated. The sources share
  a base model, so conditional independence fails.
- The vote-count AUROC (0.798) equals the hidden-state existence probe (0.807), which was killed for showing
  no selection effect.
- External reference: CapMAS (arXiv 2412.15484) measures 73.5 AUROC for "object in m of 40 samples", 81.4 for
  a per-object yes/no probe and 61.5 for a detector.

**Systematic errors (`audit_classes.py`)**
- 610 of the teacher's 2,376 false positives (25.7%) survive at least-3-of-5 agreement.
- Top survivors: dining table 198, chair 103, scissors 36, laptop 35.
- **Matcher artifact (`audit_keo.py`).** Every one of the teacher's 253 "scissors" mentions comes from bare
  "kéo" (the verb "to pull", or "ngăn kéo" = drawer). 217 are false, and they understate P by:
  - 2.0 points for zero-shot detailed
  - 2.0 points for VCD
  - 0.6 points for VSPS detailed

  This also inflates the paper's COCO CHAIR_i for zero-shot detailed.
- **Fix:** drop bare "kéo" from the scissors entry of `coco80_vi.json`, keeping "cái kéo" and "cây kéo", or
  mark it `_rui_ro`.

**Reading.** The first confirmer buys about 1.7 P per point of R, which is a better rate than shortening the
caption (detailed to short buys 0.57 P per R). F1 does not move. The best machine construct stays 5 F1 and
12 P below a fused-two-human target.

## 3. Literature (full texts read by two sub-agents and me; at least 26 searches; arXiv IDs)

### 3.1 Self-training for captioning and VLMs: small gains; selection beats imitating the mode

**BLIP CapFilt (2201.12086)**
- +1.9 COCO CIDEr at 14M images.
- Nucleus sampling beats beam once filtered.
- A filter that shares the captioner's parameters flags 8% instead of 25% and scores lower (Table 4):
  confirmation bias.
- A new model trained from scratch is about as good as continuing the old one (Table 13).

**UCM (2201.02010)**
- +1.6 CIDEr from 280k unlabelled images.
- A second round adds 0.

**Self-Distillation FSIC (WACV 2021)**
- Committee of 3 captioners trained on re-randomised batch splits (not cross-fitted), with
  confidence-weighted pseudo-captions.
- +2.5 CIDEr at 1% COCO labels.

**MBR and QE finetuning (2309.10966): the closest analogue**
- Self-training on the base model's own beam output HURTS: COMET20 57.79 -> 57.08.
- Self-MBR over 256 samples on 200k unlabelled sentences helps: 59.94, +2.15 over beam.
- It even beats MBR decoding on COMET20 (59.94 vs 59.35), but not on BLEURT or MetricX.

**Noisy Student (1911.04252)**
- A student warm-started from the teacher stalls: 86.4 vs 86.9 trained from scratch.
- Noise matters.
- ReST-EM (2312.06585) restarts from the base model every round.

**Self-labelling for captioning (2305.02297)**
- In-distribution unlabelled images: +12.5 CIDEr with 10 labels.
- Out-of-distribution unlabelled images (CC12M): -0.5.
- Likelihood filtering hurts because it favours short captions.

**Model collapse**
- Gerstgrasser et al. (2404.01413): accumulating synthetic data with the real data keeps error bounded;
  replacing real data does not.
- Shumailov et al. (Nature 2024): keeping 10% original data mostly prevents degradation.
- Recap-DataComp-1B: all-synthetic CLIP training collapses ImageNet accuracy (36.0 vs 69.7).

### 3.2 Consistency and voting signals

**FactTune-MC (2311.08401)**
- DPO from the consistency of atomic claims across samples.
- Llama-2 error rate 0.252 -> 0.154, but correct facts fall 19.03 -> 11.31: the precision-for-coverage trade
  seen in 2.1.

**Atomic Self-Consistency (2405.13131)**
- 50 samples, sentence clusters kept by support count, then an LLM rewrite. Inference only.
- QAMPARI F1 18.07 (USC) -> 19.46.

**IC3 (2302.01328)**
- An LLM summarises 10 samples of one captioner. Inference only. K=10 ≈ K=100.

**Self-Validation (2601.22451, Jan 2026)**
- Filter-then-aggregate over N=3 samples on Qwen2.5-VL-7B: CHAIR_I 9.9 -> 3.1, but F1 74.4 -> 70.6.
- Inference only; aggregation by LVLM rewriting.

**Voting rewards and RL**
- TTRL (2504.16084) and MM-UPT (2505.22453) work for discrete answers. MM-UPT fails on hard data
  (49.47 -> 44.11).
- TTRV (2510.06783): binary majority votes hurt 4 of 5 VLM benchmarks; frequency-weighted votes helped.
- SRT (2505.21444): vote-based RL collapses after about 1,000 steps unless items with under 60% majority are
  dropped.
- RubiCap (2603.09160): majority of K=5 external VLMs as rubric elements, then GRPO. Win rate rises with
  committee size: 58.2 -> 65.2 -> 67.6.

**Bias of self-judging**
- HalDec-Bench (2511.20515): models judge their own captions worse. Gemini-3-Pro: AUROC 54.2 on its own
  captions vs 79.8 on average.

### 3.3 Detector- and agreement-filtered pseudo-captions that already exist (partial scoops)

**SENTINEL (2507.12455)**
- Self-sampled captions; an object is labelled only when two detectors agree; uncertain objects are ignored;
  used for DPO.
- Object HalBench response hallucination: two detectors 6.6, vs 12.3-14.3 with one.
- Qwen2.5-VL-7B: 15.0 -> 4.7.

**CAPTURE self-loop (2405.19092)**
- OWLv2 filter plus LLM merge, then SFT.
- The filter adds little: 56.68 with it vs 56.43 without.

**Other close work**
- HalluciDoctor (2311.13614): cross-MLLM consistency on GPT-written instruction data. Over-filtering kills
  MME: threshold 0.9 gives 739 vs 1207 at 0.5.
- Embodied captioning LD-CPS (2504.08531): LLM consensus over multi-view captions, then fine-tuning.
- Visual Fact Checker (2404.19752): multiple captioners, then detector/VQA checks, then LLM fusion.
  Training-free.
- Image Textualization (2406.07502) and Woodpecker (2310.16045): detector verification.
- OVFact (2507.19262): detector-grounded precision filter.
- RLAIF-V (2405.17220): claim-level yes/no checks by a labeller model.

### 3.4 Scoop verdicts

| Idea | Verdict |
|---|---|
| object-level k-of-n vote over one adapter's own samples as the SFT pseudo-label filter for captioning on unlabelled images | not found |
| deterministic, extractive, LLM-free fusion restricted to voted objects | not found (ASC, IC3, VFC and Self-Validation merge with an LLM or LVLM at inference) |
| committee of adapters trained on disjoint halves, for captioning | open, but the pilot shows little to gain |
| object-majority RL for captioning | open but risky (TTRV, SRT); RubiCap uses external committees |
| self-training for low-resource-language captioning | open |
| detector-verified self-training (English) | scooped (SENTINEL, CAPTURE, OVFact) |

### 3.5 Resources a reviewer will raise

- **UIT-OpenViIC (2305.04166).** 13,100 Vietnam images (train 9,088 / val 2,011 / test 2,001), 61,241 human
  captions of 17.6 words each, open Google-Drive download, "non-commercial research". It contains about 72%
  of KTVIC test and 65% of KTVIC train (above).
- **UIT-EVJVQA.** About 4.9k images; KTVIC is a subset of it (about 550 unused).
- **OpenViVQA.** 11.2k images from the same lab and crawl, so high overlap risk.

  Vietnamese to Open Images classes.

## 4. Designs (at most 2)

### 4.0 Shared mechanism, pre-registered from 2.3 before any unseen-image data is seen

- Sample n=6 detailed captions with T 0.8, top-p 0.95, top-k 0 (the `e5_sample.py` defaults).
- Votes are counted per KTVIC-lexicon object.
- A sentence is admissible only when all its objects have at least k=3 votes.
- Greedy set cover over admissible sentences: at most 2 sentences for the detailed target, at most 1 for
  the short target.
- Fallback when nothing is admissible: the object-consensus MBR pick.
- VTF only ever reuses sentences the teacher wrote; it cannot add content.
- Implementation: `vote_fuse.py`, which has a `_check()`.

### 4.1 D1: VTF self-distillation on a hidden-label pool (UIT-OpenViIC)

**Pool**
- OpenViIC train de-duplicated against KTVIC train+test with `scripts/near_dup.py`. Use a conservative
  threshold, CLIP cosine >= 0.90, CPU on n3. Expect about 6k of the 9,088 images to remain.
- Draw 800 images; their captions stay hidden during training.
- Teacher: the existing fuse2_s42, which never saw these images.
- These are in-distribution images (same crawl as KTVIC); the self-labelling literature reports -0.5 for
  out-of-distribution pools.

**Step 0: label and gate (2.2 GPU-h)**
- Per image (3090): 6 samples (about 6 s, batched) + greedy detailed (about 3 s) + greedy short (about 1 s).
  800 x 10 s = 2.2 h.
- On CPU, compare VTF, greedy, MBR pick and one held-out human against the hidden OpenViIC references:
  1v4 object P/R/F1, paired bootstrap B=2000, plus CIDEr-D as a check independent of the object extractor.
- **KILL** if any of these holds, and report a negative result:
  - VTF minus greedy < +1.5 F1;
  - the CI includes 0;
  - P(VTF) < P(greedy).

**Step 1: three students with matched data and steps (2.4 GPU-h)**
- Each student continues fuse2_s42 (`train_stage2.py --adapter`) for 1 epoch on:
  - 1,600 pseudo rows (detailed VTF k=3 with at most 2 sentences, and short VTF with 1 sentence);
  - plus 1,600 replayed KTVIC fuse2 rows.
- 400 steps x 7.3 s = 0.8 h per arm.
- Arms:
  - V: VTF labels.
  - G: the teacher's greedy captions. Plain self-training / confirmation-bias control; the MBR-finetuning
    literature predicts G <= fuse2.
  - H: OpenViIC's human captions with the fuse2 recipe (consensual + fused 2). Supervised skyline; report
    the fraction of H's gain that V recovers.

**Step 2: evaluation (about 3.4 GPU-h plus 0.45 h for fuse2 baselines)**
- KTVIC test, both prompts: object P/R/F1, CIDEr-D, CHAIR-vi.
- OpenViIC test: 300 images, de-duplicated against KTVIC train, both prompts.
- COCO Karpathy-test: 500 images, short prompt, instance gold. The vote uses the KTVIC lexicon and the judge
  uses COCO-80, so selection and judge do not share an extractor.
- UIT-ViIC test: 231 images, short prompt, instance gold.

**Total: about 8.5 GPU-h.** First extension if V > G: a V student trained from the base model (3.8 h,
step-matched to fuse2), as Noisy Student and ReST-EM recommend.

**Success requires all of:**
- V - fuse2 on KTVIC object-F1 exceeds the seed spread (fuse2 s42 vs s43), paired p < 0.05;
- V does not lose precision on COCO or UIT-ViIC instance gold;
- V > G.

**Confirmation-bias controls**
- Unseen images only (the fix for E5).
- The selection (vote over 6 samples) differs from the student's decoding (greedy), and arm G isolates
  imitation.
- Human data is replayed in every arm (accumulate, never replace), and there is one round only.
- External judges: hidden OpenViIC human references, COCO/UIT-ViIC instance labels, CIDEr.
- k, n and the sentence budget are pre-registered.
- A per-class audit on COCO checks whether voting removed only random errors (2.1 shows systematic ones
  survive).
- No LLM rewriting step.

**Closest prior and delta**
- Closest prior: MBR finetuning (2309.10966), which selects one whole output by a string utility, for NMT.
- ASC, IC3, Self-Validation and VFC fuse with an LLM or LVLM at inference.
- SENTINEL and CAPTURE filter with detectors; our pilot shows detectors are useless on KTVIC objects.
- D1's delta: an object-level vote over one adapter's own samples, realised by extractive set cover, used as
  SFT pseudo-references on unseen images in a low-resource language, gated by hidden human references and
  judged by instance labels.

**Confidence**
- The gate gives a clean answer: high.
- The gate passes: about 50%.
- The student beats fuse2 on KTVIC: about 20%. E5's null result and UCM/FSIC-sized gains (1-3 points) keep
  this low.

### 4.2 D2: cross-fitted VTF on KTVIC train, the label-free twin of the queued cross-fitted RFT

**Inputs**
- The 6 samples the queued fuse2-A adapter (trained on half A) draws on the unseen half B.
- Plus greedy fuse2-A captions on 600 half-B images (about 0.5 GPU-h). This is also the cheapest early read
  of D1's gate quantity: VTF vs greedy on unseen KTVIC images, scored against half B's own references.

**Gate (CPU)**
- Compare VTF vs RFT's gold-selected pick vs one sample vs MBR vs greedy (1v4).
- **KILL** if VTF scores more than 3 F1 below the gold-selected rows, or does not beat greedy.

**Training**
- Only if the gate passes AND the gold cross-fitted RFT itself beats fuse2.
- Needs fuse2-B samples on half A (1,884 x 6 s = 3.1 h).
- One student trained from base on the fuse2 rows plus one VTF detailed row per image: 2,827 steps, 5.7 h.
- Plus evaluation, 0.6 h. Total about 9.9 GPU-h.

**Value:** on identical samples, it measures how much selection needs gold.

**Closest prior and delta.** Tri-training (2-of-3 agreement), DRST and cross-prediction (cross-fitted
estimation) exist, but nobody uses cross-fitted teachers for captioning.

**Confidence that it beats fuse2:** about 10%. High value as an ablation.

### 4.3 Rejected by the pilot or the literature

- **Cross-adapter or co-training agreement:** KTVIC dF1 -6.1; COCO +0.3, not significant.
- **Detector agreement on KTVIC:** R 17.8.
- **Dawid-Skene weighting:** dominated by the vote count.
- **VTF on seen train images, zero labelling cost:** E5 shows retraining on gold-selected seen-image samples
  is null.
- **A COCO pool without the COCO-35L MT baseline.**
- **Object-majority RL (TTRL for captions):** collapse risk (SRT, TTRV); revisit only after D1 succeeds
  offline.

## 5. Caveats

- On KTVIC and OpenViIC, the KTVIC-lexicon extractor both selects (votes) and scores, which risks metric
  targeting. MBR finetuning raises the same caveat for BLEURT. Use CIDEr-D and the COCO-80/instance judge.
- VTF captions are about 25% shorter than fuse2 detailed (17.6 vs 24.0 words). This interacts with the
  Gaussian length penalty of detailed-mode CIDEr-D, so report length. k=2 gives 21.2 words.
- OpenViIC has no explicit licence: release IDs and annotations only. Its captions are longer (17.6 vs 12.1
  words), which matters only for arm H.
- The COCO human calibration matches English references with synonyms and Vietnamese captions with
  `coco80_vi.json`; matcher differences enter the human-vs-machine gap (see the "kéo" artifact).
- The 6-second, 6-sample throughput is an estimate. It comes from 0.87 s per short caption on 3090 4-bit
  (`data/vram`) and 2.06 s per detailed caption on a 4090 (`latency_main_s42.json`). Training uses
  7.3 s/step.

## 6. Side findings for the paper

1. **Second annotator panel.** About 400 KTVIC test images have independent human captions in UIT-OpenViIC
   train. This existing second panel can test whether fuse2's precision gap to humans (about 12 points)
   comes from KTVIC annotators' choice of objects.
2. **The "kéo" artifact.** It understates zero-shot detailed precision on COCO-5000 by about 2.0 points
   (CHAIR_i inflated).
3. **Extra references for training images.** About 65% of KTVIC train images have about 4.7 more human
   captions in OpenViIC: existing extra partial references for the fusion story.
