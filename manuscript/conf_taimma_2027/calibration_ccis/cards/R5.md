## Paper card

**Problem and stated limitation of prior work.** Evaluation suites for LLMs cover high-resource languages. For Mongolian, the paper states that earlier work targets downstream tasks such as text classification and named-entity recognition, and that there is little evaluation of large models. The work covers modern Mongolian in Cyrillic script.

**Main claim.** On a new four-part benchmark, all tested models do better on syntax than on semantics, knowledge scores are moderate (read by the authors as transfer of general knowledge from high-resource languages), and reasoning is weak (best accuracy 29.6%).

**Method / resource.** A "dual capability" scheme: language abilities (syntax, semantics) and cognitive abilities (knowledge, reasoning). Syntax items: sentences of three or more words from one language textbook (digitised by OCR, cleaned by hand), each with three distractors made by shuffling the word order and checked by hand. Semantics items: cloze questions; a word found in the textbook's vocabulary list is removed and three distractors of a compatible part of speech are drawn from the list (verbs are excluded because of inflection). Knowledge items: country facts filtered from WebQSP, plus question-answer pairs generated with the ChatGPT API; distractors are generated by ChatGPT and checked by hand; the items are machine-translated from English and checked by hand. Reasoning items: MGSM arithmetic word problems translated from English and Chinese, compared with the sources by ChatGPT, then corrected and checked by hand.

**Theorem, bound, identification result or provable property.** None.

**Data and splits (sizes).** 569 syntax, 677 semantics and 344 knowledge multiple-choice questions (four options) and 250 reasoning problems with numeric answers; 1,840 items in total. Evaluation only; no development split. The number of human checkers and their agreement are not reported.

**Models and model families.** Open: Qwen2-7B-Instruct, GLM-4-9B-chat, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (run locally, temperature 0, top-p 0.1). Closed: GPT-4-Turbo, DeepSeek V2.5. One fixed English prompt per task.

**Baselines and their years.** No baseline beyond the five models (cited to 2023–2024 references). No human score; the 25% chance level of the multiple-choice parts is not discussed; no scores on the source-language versions of the same items.

**Key numbers (accuracy as printed: syntax / semantics / knowledge / reasoning).** DeepSeek V2.5: 86.29% / 60.12% / 70.35% / 29.6%. GPT-4-Turbo: 90.69% / 72.53% / 80.52% / 26.8%. GLM-4-9B: 66.26% / 32.05% / 65.41% / 7.6%. Qwen2-7B: 72.58% / 54.8% / 66.28% / 6%. Llama-3.1-8B: 54.13% / 28.06% / 59.01% / 5%. The text credits the 72.53% semantics score to Qwen2-7B, while the table gives it to GPT-4-Turbo.

**Ablations, seeds, statistical tests, confidence intervals.** None. One run per model and task; no prompt variants; no confidence intervals and no tests for differences between models or between task types.

**Failure analysis.** None: no error categories, and no analysis of why reasoning is low or of translation errors.

**Code/data release and licence statements.** The dataset is stated to be available in a public repository. No licence is stated, and the reuse terms of the textbook are not discussed. Release of inference code: not stated.

**Positioning against closest prior work.** A short related-work passage lists English and Chinese benchmarks and two Mongolian task corpora; no table; no comparison with a benchmark for another low-resource language.

**Out-of-domain or cross-language checks.** None run. The transfer reading of the knowledge scores is not tested against the same items in English or Chinese.

**Stated limitations.** One content source; multiple-choice format only; a narrow range of reasoning tasks.
