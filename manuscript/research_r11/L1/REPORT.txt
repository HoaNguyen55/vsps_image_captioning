2b. THEORY CHECK (Q2). Same result? YES. Micro-F1 = 2TP/(M+G). Adding mentions with expected precision p raises F iff p > F/2 (F of
   the current set). Lipton's Thm 1 proof compares exactly F1' vs F1'' for one added region; Cor. 1 is its fixed point under
   calibration (theta* = F*/2). G2's "iff b*P > F/2" is the same inequality at any operating point, with b*P = marginal precision.
   - Averaging: exact for micro (pooled) F1. Macro (per-class) F1: one threshold per class. Instance F1: per-image thresholds on tiny
     batches (GFM). object_prf.py is micro-P with image-macro-R: include iff p > P/(1+kappa/|G_i|), kappa = P^2 m/R^2 (C5). This
     equals F/2 only when |G_i| = P*m/R, so G2's rule is approximate for the repo metric (sign agreement 73/77 steps, D-B).
     F_beta generalises to p > F_beta/(1+beta^2), my derivation; Zhao et al. JMLR 14 (2013) derive optimal F thresholds (VERIFY form).
   - Incomplete gold: p and F are measured against the gold used. With k references, unreferenced true objects count as FP, so b and
     the F1-optimal count M* = b*Gbar/(1-b) shrink as references are removed. The rule says what maximises F against THAT gold.
   - Verbosity (E6 corr 0.84, panel gold): b_I 0.899, Gbar 6.0 -> M* ~ 53 >> every system's M, so every frontier mention adds F1.
     The sign is explained. The size of the correlation also reflects scatter: on KTVIC 5-ref gold the all-73 corr is only +0.22,
     because off-style systems sit 20-46 P below the frontier. Under 1-ref gold the sign reverses (D-C).

3. NEW DIRECTIONS INFERRED FROM THE GAP (verdicts on the brief's (a)-(c) included)
D-C. The verbosity reward of raw object F1 is gold-indexed and flips sign under sparse gold   [from Q2; tested here]
  Claim: with frontier TP = a*M^b, micro-F1 peaks at M* = b*Gbar/(1-b) (Lipton Cor. 1 + G2; b -> 1 gives Lipton Thm 3, "name
    everything"). b rises with gold completeness, so F1 rewards verbosity only where M* exceeds the systems' counts, and rewards
    brevity under sparse gold.
  Evidence (object_prf convention, 500 image bootstraps; slope = OLS of F1 on log M across systems):
    KTVIC test, 36 FT systems (M 1.76-3.75): 1 ref -10.65 [-13.87,-7.28] (first-k) / -3.32 [-6.19,-0.48] (random-k); 2 refs (random)
      +3.77 [+0.99,+6.02]; 5 refs +14.15 [+11.67,+16.49]. All 73 systems: -3.94 [-6.10,-1.74] -> +7.61 [+5.74,+9.40].
    COCO-2500, 9 systems (M 0.84-2.14), predictions frozen before the run, 4/4 held: 1 EN ref -4.28 [-5.91,-2.45]; 5 refs +11.08
      [+9.76,+12.43]; instance labels +22.41 [+21.27,+23.48]. sign(slope) = sign(M*_mixed - median M) in 12/12 COCO and 16/20 KTVIC
      variants (3 misses = all-73 sets at k<=2, where off-style systems break one frontier; 1 = FT k=2 first-k, M* 1.87 vs median
      1.83). The micro closed form overestimates M* at sparse gold (10/12, 15/20).
    Side finding for G2: KTVIC's first reference is special. b(k=1) is 0.403 first-k vs 0.536 random-k (COCO 0.478 vs 0.479), so
      Delta b(5-1) is +0.30 first-k vs +0.17 random-k.
  New vs Sec. 2: R10 treats "raw F1 rewards verbosity" as unconditional. Here its sign is predicted from (b, Gbar) and reverses
    between golds of the same images. Count control is needed in BOTH directions.
  Prediction: on UIT-ViIC (refs-lex vs inst-coco80) and XM3600-VI (1-2 refs/img), sign(slope) = sign(M* - median M). Terse and
    verbose systems swap raw-F1 rank between 1-ref and instance gold.
  Test: L1/scripts/check_mstar.py and check_coco.py + G2 loaders (data/results/uitviic, data/uitviic/instance_gold.json); XM3600
    once G2-D outputs exist. Cost: <0.3 CPU-h (+~1.5 GPU-h only for XM3600 outputs). Pillars: C2 (main), C1, C4.
    Novelty 3, risk 2.
D-B. Every object metric declares an implicit confidence target; F1's is F/2, the count-matched residual's is b*P_f   [(b),(c)]
  Claim: raw F1 adds a mention iff its posterior > F/2 (Lipton). F_beta: > F_beta/(1+beta^2), so THRONE's F0.5 asks 0.8*F. The
    frontier residual adds iff posterior > dTP_f/dM ~ b*P_f(m). On KTVIC the residual's bar (0.50-0.62) is about 2x F1's (0.24-0.31),
    so an expected-F1 planner overshoots the residual-optimal count by construction. This is Kalai et al.'s "explicit confidence
    target" (2509.04664) made concrete for object mentions.
  Evidence (check_stop.py on M6 out/m6_alloc.json, dev300, 7 rules x 11 budget steps): sign(dF1) = sign(p_marg - F/2) in 73/77
    steps; sign(d residual) = sign(p_marg - p_frontier) in 74/77. Global vote: raw F1 peaks at m 3.21 (62.45; next step p_marg 0.238 <
    F/2 0.312), exactly the uncapped PLAN-F1 label-free fixed point (m 3.213). Its residual there is -2.21; the residual peaks at m
    1.33-1.91 (+1.85..+2.13; the frontier is extrapolated below 1.76).
  (b) verdict: YES. Decision theory predicts D12 twice over. (i) The plan is top-k_i by its own q, so it equals the q-ranked
    same-count control (300/300 identical, dF1 0.00). (ii) It stops where p = F/2, past the count where p falls below the
    frontier's marginal precision, hence a negative residual. The cap of 3 (-2.49 vs -1.56 uncapped at m 2.63) is outside the theory.
    An EOS policy is a per-image k_i rule over the model's own order, so it can win at matched total count only via allocation.
    E1's LiM-EOS split (count ~4.9 P, per-image stopping 1.9 P, object choice 1.1 P n.s.) fits this.
  (c) verdict: the mapping is off. Corpus allocation = PU/EUM (global threshold; Koyejo micro). Per-image EF1 = ETU/DTA for instance
    F on a one-image batch (GFM): a metric mismatch (C5: -0.2 to -0.7), not a finite-sample effect. Ye et al. predict EUM ~ DTA at
    ~1,300 decisions (C5: 60.1 vs 60.3) and are silent on m* ~ 2.4. The crossing follows from the frontier condition (M6 T4); the
    allocation gain follows from Lipton's batch observation (M6 T2).
  Prediction: on test pools (p1assist_s4{2,3,4} greedy + 6 x T0.5, data/results/upe/test/), a label-free plug-in with threshold F/2
    lands at m >= 2.6 with residual < 0. The same plug-in with threshold = frontier marginal precision lands at m in [1.5, 2.1] with
    residual >= 0. Across released decoders, THRONE-F0.5 favours short decoders more than F1-type scores do.
  Test: plan_f1.py plug_in with the threshold swapped, dev-frozen calibrator; m6_alloc curves for dev. CPU ~0.5 h, 0 GPU.
    Pillars: C1, C5. Novelty 3, risk 2.
D-A. Decision rules cannot buy ranking: CAR's A term is zero for any Bayes act on the control's own score   [(a)]
  Claim: for micro-F (and the mixed metric under independent labels) every Bayes act given marginals q is top-k_i by q per image
    (Lewis 1995; Ye 2012 DTA; Koyejo 2015; Lipton Thm 1). So A = 0 against the own-score control, and A <= 0 when choice is
    restricted to existing captions. A > 0 needs new evidence (a better score), or label dependence through the 1/|G_i| term
    (GFM count-conditional marginals). Known in substance (PRP for F); new only as an evaluation identity and triage rule.
  Evidence: D12 300/300 identical; vs raw-vote control +0.08 [-0.74,+0.91]; UPE Shapley phi_A < 0 in 11/11 rows (-0.10..-0.51,
    mt2_upe_car_shapley.md); knapsack A +0.09 [-0.83,+1.03]; R10_00: "A ~ 0 everywhere measured".
  Prediction: no score-preserving rule (EOS rule, threshold, vote-monotone MBR, F-planner) shows A > 0 with CI > 0. Only
    evidence-adding methods (M1 grounding re-rank; layer-24 probe, AUROC 0.807) can.
  Test: tie-averaged own-score controls (x1_ties.py) for every method. For the loophole, dev300 13-caption pools: compute w =
    E[y/|G_i|] from 5-caption subset unions vs PLAN's independent Poisson-binomial, then dF1 at fixed k_i (predict |dF1| < 0.3).
  Cost: <0.3 CPU-h. Pillars: C1 (CAR), C5; steers GPU to M1. Novelty 2, risk 1.
D-D. Propensity-scored object F1: object importance as missing-label propensity   [B3 x F, B2 x F]
  Claim: reference incompleteness is the XMLC missing-label problem (Jain 2016; Schultheis 2022). A class's naming propensity
    pi_c = P(named by one annotator | present) is object importance (Berg 2012; Spain & Perona). Weighting reference TPs by
    1/(1-(1-pi_c)^k) estimates instance-gold P/R from references alone. If D-C's mechanism is right, the corrected b(k) is flat in k.
  Prediction: on COCO-2500 (pi_c fitted on COCO train/val instances + refs, never on the 2,500), corrected refs-F1 ranks the 9 systems
    closer to instance F1 than raw refs-F1 (Kendall tau gain >= 0.2), and corrected b(1..5) spans < 0.05.
  Test: CPU on data/coco_probe + Karpathy refs; UIT-ViIC instance_gold.json for VI. ~0.5 CPU-h. Pillars C2/C3. Novelty 3, risk 4
    (salience is image-dependent, i.e. labels are missing not at random; only 9 systems).

6. WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY CONCLUSION (kill rules)
 - Gap: a 2018-2026 caption/LVLM paper that chooses object mentions by an F-measure Bayes rule (threshold F/2, GFM or EUM). That would
   make PLAN-F1/C5 and D-B incremental. Checked: arXiv API 35 queries, WebSearch ~20, S2 citers of the F seeds.
 - D-C: on UIT-ViIC or XM3600, the sparsest-gold slope is > 0 with CI > 0 while M*_mixed < median M (or the reverse), in >= 2
   settings. Then drop the sign-flip claim and keep only "b depends on the gold".
 - D-B: on test, the F/2 plug-in lands at m < 2.3 or has residual >= 0, or the frontier-threshold plug-in has residual < 0 with CI
   excluding 0. Then the two-target account fails.
 - D-A: a score-preserving rule has A > 0 (CI > 0) against its own-score tie-averaged control, or dependence-aware w moves F1 by
   > 0.5 at fixed k_i. Then the GFM loophole is real; promote it to a method and drop the identity.
 - D-D: corrected refs-F1 does not raise Kendall tau with instance F1, or corrected b(k) still spans >= 0.1. Then propensity is not
   the mechanism.
