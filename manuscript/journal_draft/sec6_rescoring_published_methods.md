# 6 Experiments I: re-scoring published methods at matched object count

Fazli et al. (2026; arXiv 2609.01888) argue that lower hallucination scores of inference-time methods often reflect "risk suppression" (naming less) rather than better grounding, on the evidence of hallucination and informativeness metrics that move together across model–method configurations; Sandhu et al. (2026; arXiv 2608.24966) make the same point for one method under fixed token budgets. This section measures that concern per image. It applies the controls of Section 3.3(b), registered in advance, to methods whose authors released their captions. We ask one question per comparison: does the method's precision gain over its baseline survive when both captions are cut to the same number of mentioned objects on every image? We report the answer as a specification curve over the three registered controls, because Section 3.7 showed that a single control can be chosen to favour either conclusion. Section 6.4 extends the protocol to training-based methods; that run is pending.

## 6.1 Data and fidelity

We use the released CHAIR evaluation outputs of two groups: OPERA (Huang et al., CVPR 2024 [CITE: arXiv 2311.17911, UNVERIFIED: identifier from memory, check before submission]), which gives greedy, beam-search and OPERA captions for InstructBLIP, LLaVA-1.5, MiniGPT-4 and Shikra; and "Less is More" (Yue et al., 2024; arXiv 2402.14545), which gives captions for LLaVA-1.5-7B, LLaVA-1.5-13B, LLaVA-7B and MiniGPT-v2 with and without its end-of-sequence method, plus VCD and OPERA variants on LLaVA-1.5-7B. Each method is paired with the baseline its authors compared it to, which gives 15 comparisons<!-- src: .session_backup/2026-10-13/r10_cpu/e1_x1pb_verification_v2.json :: overview_15_pairs.n_pairs -->. Every comparison is paired on the same COCO val2014 images (495 to 499 per comparison, after dropping images without instance annotations, as CHAIR does)<!-- src: same :: pairs_15.*.n_img (min, max) -->. Objects are matched to COCO-80 with our re-implementation of the CHAIR synonym list, against the CHAIR gold (instance labels plus the five references).

Before any count-controlled statistic was computed, the re-implementation had to reproduce published CHAIR values (registered fidelity check K1). It reproduces the four published rows we checked to within 0.6 points on every reported column<!-- src: same :: fidelity_K1.{lim_r1,lim_r5,lim_r12,opera_llava15}.{ours,published} (max absolute difference); fidelity_K1.passes_K1 = True -->.

Three readings of the table hold under every control.

**Most of the raw gain is a count effect.** Twelve raw gains are significant; under the two controls that keep the order of mention, two survive. The mean gain falls from +3.41 to +0.62 points with the matched prefix and to +0.65 with the sentence tie-average. The same two comparisons survive both order-preserving controls: OPERA versus greedy decoding on InstructBLIP (+3.88 [+2.49, +5.27] points at matched prefix)<!-- src: same :: pairs_15."opera/instructblip__ours vs opera/instructblip__greedy".dP_MCP_prefix.est, .ci95; .survives_holm_and_sesoi1 = true --> and the end-of-sequence method on MiniGPT-v2 (+2.81 [+0.91, +4.67])<!-- src: same :: pairs_15."lim/r12-ours-cap vs lim/r11-minigptv2".dP_MCP_prefix.est, .ci95; .survives_holm_and_sesoi1 = true -->. In a descriptive decomposition with the baselines' own captions as reference points, count and per-image allocation account for 81.8% of the raw gain under the matched prefix and 80.9% under the sentence tie-average<!-- src: same :: overview_15_pairs.pct_gain_explained_by_count_and_alloc_prefix, pct_gain_explained_by_count_and_alloc_sent_tieavg -->; we do not attach intervals to these shares, because several comparisons share a baseline.

**One gain reverses.** OPERA on Shikra has a raw precision gain over beam search of +3.47 [+0.75, +6.03] points, and a significant loss at matched prefix, −1.23 [−2.42, −0.08]<!-- src: same :: pairs_15."opera/shikra__ours vs opera/shikra__beam5".dP_raw.est/.ci95, .dP_MCP_prefix.est/.ci95; overview_15_pairs.mcp_prefix_sig_neg_unadj = 1 -->. Its captions name 16% fewer objects than the baseline's<!-- src: same :: pairs_15."opera/shikra__ours vs opera/shikra__beam5".M_ratio.est = 0.842 -->: the whole raw gain, and more, comes from naming fewer objects.

**The random-subset control is the outlier, and it is the weakest control.** Under the uniform random subset, 6 of 15 gains survive and the mean gain stays at +2.76 points. This control keeps a random $k_i$ of the longer caption's mentions, so it removes the count difference but not the order effect: when later mentions are less often correct (assumption A2, which the support profile of Section 4.5 confirms on COCO), a random subset of a long caption keeps more late, weak mentions than the shorter caption has, and the longer caption is penalized less than at matched prefix. The gap between the two controls is largest where the count ratio is smallest; for OPERA on Shikra against greedy decoding (count ratio 0.808), the matched-prefix difference is −1.23 [−2.62, +0.08] and the random-subset difference is +5.45 [+3.36, +7.40]<!-- src: same :: pairs_15."opera/shikra__ours vs opera/shikra__greedy".M_ratio.est, .dP_MCP_prefix.est/.ci95, .dP_MCP_rand_exact.est/.ci95 -->. The random-subset control also fails its own two-seed check (two random draws of the same caption set must agree; |ΔP| within the registered bound and an interval covering 0) in 2 of 8 baselines<!-- src: same :: controls_K2_K3.placebos_K2.two_seed_random_subset.*.passes_K2 (6 true of 8) -->. We report it because it was registered, and because a reader who believes mentions are not salience-ordered would choose it.

**Registered stopping rule.** E1 registered that if more than one third of the gains (more than 5 of 15)<!-- src: same :: overview_15_pairs.k4_threshold_gt_one_third --> survived count control, the count-confound claim for English decoding methods would be abandoned (K4). The rule is not triggered under the matched prefix or the sentence tie-average and is triggered under the random subset<!-- src: same :: overview_15_pairs.k4_en_verdict_prefix, k4_en_verdict_sent_tieavg, k4_en_verdict_random_subset -->. Which of these is the verdict depends on which control is primary, and that choice is what the registered seed placebo decides (Section 6.3). Until it has run, we state the result as the curve: between 2 and 6 of 15 published precision gains survive count control.

## 6.5 What this section shows

On public outputs from two groups and seven backbones, the conclusion "method X lowers hallucination" depends on whether count is controlled and on how. Without control, 12 of 15 precision gains are significant; with the order-preserving controls, 2 are. We do not conclude that the other 10 methods do nothing: their outputs name fewer objects, which is a legitimate design choice with a recall cost, and several of them may change object choice by amounts below what 500 images can detect (Section 6.3). We conclude that a raw precision or CHAIR gain does not distinguish "chooses objects better" from "names fewer objects", and that a paper claiming the first should report a pre-declared count-controlled column (Section 10).
