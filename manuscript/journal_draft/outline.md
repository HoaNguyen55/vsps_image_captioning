## Contributions

1. **A gold-indexed account of the verbosity reward, tested out of sample.**
   - *The proposition.* Under exchangeable references and salience-ordered mentions, the frontier exponent $b(k)$ is nondecreasing in the number of references $k$. The sign of micro-F1's verbosity reward changes at most once (from negative to positive) as $k$ grows.
   - *What is cited, and what is ours.* The F1 half-threshold rule is Lipton et al. (2014); the propensity view of human precision versus $k$ is Chen et al. (2015, §4.2). Our part is the monotonicity, the single crossing, the two-channel split, and the tests on fixed outputs.
   - *What it predicts.* The proposition orders the slopes and fixes the large-gold endpoint (CC-007). The negative one-reference sign is an empirical observation, not a theoretical prediction; at $k=1$ the closed form sits near the boundary (sec4 §4.3). It does not predict the number of references at which the sign changes.
   - *Confirmed (registered, XM3600).* The slope is ordered by gold completeness and positive under the verified labels, the large-gold endpoint; two of five registered predictions failed (N1-XM-2 and N1-XM-5, both on the all-references gold) and the kill rule did not fire. *Exploratory (COCO-2500).* The same pattern, with predictions frozen locally (file timestamp 2026-10-14 07:22Z, never committed)<!-- src: manuscript/prereg/2026-10-18_never_committed_frozen_files.json :: files[2] (L1_COCO).mtime_utc = 2026-10-14T07:22:14Z, in_any_git_commit = false -->: 4 of 4 held<!-- src: .session_backup/2026-10-14/r11_litmaps/L1/check_coco_PREDICTIONS.txt (P1–P4); outcomes .session_backup/2026-10-14/r11_verify/c1_b1_b6_reproduction.json :: B1_L1_mstar_and_stopping.check_coco_gold.{refs1_randk,refs5_randk,inst}.slope (−4.28 < +11.08 < +22.41), inst.slope_ci [21.27, 23.48], refs1_randk.Mstar_micro 1.41 ≤ M_range max 2.14, check_coco_sign_agree_mixed = "12/12" -->. COCO-2500's positive five-reference slope is a dataset-specific outcome. On UIT-ViIC (exploratory) the five-reference sign depends on the matcher.
2. **A per-image count-matched control for a confound others have raised.**
   - *The concern.* Fazli et al. (2026; arXiv 2609.01888) report that lower hallucination scores of inference-time methods go with lower object recall and coverage ("risk suppression"). Sandhu et al. (2026; arXiv 2608.24966) show a CHAIR gain that comes with shorter, lower-recall captions. We measure that concern per image at matched object count.
   - *The tools.* Two per-image controls (vote/greedy tie-averaged and POOL-VOTE), a corpus-total count match, and a Shapley decomposition of a score difference into count, allocation and ranking (CAR).
   - *The application.* The data are 15 published comparisons with public outputs<!-- src: .session_backup/2026-10-13/r10_cpu/e1_x1pb_verification_v2.json :: overview_15_pairs.n_pairs -->: 11 decoding comparisons and 4 of a stopping method, the Less-is-More EOS method<!-- src: same :: pairs_15 keys (8 opera/*, lim/r2-vcd, lim/r3-opera, lim/r4-opera-fast; lim/r5, r8, r10, r12 "ours") -->. The number of surviving gains, in a post hoc re-scoring, is reported as a specification curve over controls, 2/15<!-- src: same :: overview_15_pairs.mcp_prefix_survives_holm --> to 6/15<!-- src: same :: overview_15_pairs.mcp_rand_exact_survives_holm -->.
   - *Registration.* The E1 protocol was written after a pilot on the same outputs (committed in 6c647a6 at 07:01Z, after the pilot results of 01:48Z), so E1 is post hoc. Its Phase-A rules and numbers are therefore not counted as registered before their outputs; only later additions with no output yet count, such as the K2 placebo (sec6 §6.3; HELD, PD-15).
   - *What it is.* The count-matched score is a control for this confound, justified by construct validity: it removes the part of a difference that comes from naming fewer objects. It is not offered as a better quality score (item 4).
   - *What we do not claim.* That count is a better unit than word length on English outputs; that exploratory test failed (frozen locally before analysis, committed after its first output).
3. **A reference-exchangeability check.** An adjacent-doubleton statistic and two companion statistics separate independent annotators (COCO, UIT-ViIC) from corpora with a non-random reference order (Flickr30k; partly OpenViIC), and show what breaks in reference-count curves when exchangeability fails.
4. **Human validity, stated narrowly.**
   - *What the human data are for.* The count control rests on construct validity. The human data are used to check that the confound matters to judges, not to show that the control agrees with them better.
   - *Count.* Humans penalize object count on long captions (LongCap-Arena Relevance falls with count, registered), which supports the confound's existence. On short COCO captions they are near count-neutral (a slight preference for more classes on Composite, where P2a held; exploratory).
   - *Comparison conditions.* On short COCO captions, object metrics agree with humans more under complete gold and on count-equal pairs (Polaris PASS and THumB/Composite, all exploratory; Polaris was committed 22 min after its first result, D-W13-6).
   - *The control is not better aligned.* Count-matched precision does not track human judgments better than raw precision: THumB/Composite failed; DOCCI-Critique G3-(ii) HELD on the point estimate, CI includes 0 (exploratory); LongCap-Arena MIXED, Δτ negative, kill fired (registered).
   - *Housekeeping.* CapArena was dropped (no licence). The Polaris/DOCCI preregistration was committed 22 min after the first result and is treated as exploratory (D-W13-6); LongCap-Arena's was committed before its run (7254a39).
5. **Preference data audit.** In public hallucination-preference sets, a large share of chosen/rejected pairs differ in object count, and in several sets the chosen response names fewer objects in most count-unmatched pairs; length matching does not remove this.
