# 9 The object-count channel in hallucination preference data

Preference pairs can carry the count channel of Sections 6 and 7: if the chosen response tends to name fewer objects, a model trained on the pairs can learn to name fewer objects. We measure this in seven public preference sets, without testing trained models.

**Method.** Both responses are matched to COCO-80 with the CHAIR matcher; $\Delta k$ is the chosen minus the rejected class count, and $\Delta$words likewise. Pairs with no COCO-80 class are dropped; a pair is *count-matched* if $\Delta k = 0$. Among unmatched pairs we report the share where the chosen response names fewer classes (0.5 = no direction), with a 95% bootstrap interval, overall and within two words of length.

**What the audit implies, and what it does not.** In four of seven sets, preferring the chosen response means preferring fewer objects in most count-differing pairs, even under length matching. A lower CHAIR after such training may reflect the count channel, better object choice or both; only a count-matched evaluation separates them. The audit does not show that any method's published gain is a count effect; it sees only COCO-80 objects, in released pairs whose labels we did not check.
