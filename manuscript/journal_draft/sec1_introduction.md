# 1 Introduction

Claims that a captioning model or a large vision-language model (LVLM) "hallucinates less" rest mostly on object-level scores such as CHAIR (Rohrbach et al., 2018) and object F1, which compare the mentioned objects with a gold set (Section 2). Like any measurement, these scores can respond to properties of the instrument, not of the model. We ask when an object-level score supports a comparison between two systems, and identify three properties of the measurement that can decide the answer: gold completeness, the number of objects a caption names, and the exchangeability of the references.

**Gold completeness.** A gold made from one reference caption names only the most salient objects; five references name more; instance labels name objects that no caption writer mentions. The direction in which raw F1 rewards verbosity depends on this completeness. **Count.** A caption that names fewer objects tends to have higher precision, because a model names its most reliable objects first, so a method that shortens captions, stops earlier or prunes uncertain mentions improves CHAIR without choosing objects better. The concern is not ours: LeHaCE (Wei et al., 2024) and others control caption length, and Fazli et al. (2026) and others (Section 2) find that lower hallucination rates come with lower object recall ("risk suppression"). What they do not provide is a per-image control that fixes the number of mentioned objects and reports the remaining difference across several controls. That measurement is our contribution; its protocol was written after a pilot on the same outputs, so the re-scoring is post hoc (Section 6.1).

**Reference exchangeability.** The proposition, and any analysis that varies the number of references, assumes that an image's references are exchangeable draws from independent writers. Our diagnostic is the share of objects named by exactly two references that are named by two adjacent ones; under exchangeability it is 0.40. Independent-annotator datasets sit at the null (COCO 0.402; UIT-ViIC 0.384)<!-- src: .session_backup/2026-10-14/r11_verify/c1_b1_b6_reproduction.json :: B2_L2_exchangeability.datasets.coco_en_test_coco80.adjacent_share, uitviic_vi_all_objects_in.adjacent_share -->; a single-writer corpus does not (companion paper). **Motivating case.** These threats surfaced together on Vietnamese captioning (KTVIC), where object-level gains vanished under at least one count control and the references were not exchangeable. ## 1.1 Research questions

- **RQ1 (gold completeness).** Does the completeness of the gold decide the sign of object F1's verbosity reward, and what does theory predict about that sign? (Sections 4 and 7)
- **RQ2 (count).** How many published precision gains survive when both captions name the same number of objects on every image, and how does the answer depend on the control? (Sections 3 and 6)
- **RQ3 (exchangeability).** Are the references of caption corpora exchangeable, and what breaks in reference-count analyses when they are not? (Sections 5 and 7.5)
- **RQ4 (human validity).** Do existing human judgments penalize object count, and does a count-controlled score track them better than the raw score? (Section 8)
- **RQ5 (preference data).** Does the count channel enter through public hallucination-preference data, even when the pairs are matched on length? (Section 9)
