**Gold Completeness and the Verbosity Slope of Object Hallucination Metrics: A Registered Audit**

## Abstract

Object hallucination scores such as CHAIR and object F1 compare named objects with a usually incomplete gold set. This registered audit tests whether their verbosity slope, the slope of object F1 on the log number of named objects, is ordered by gold completeness. On 600 Vietnamese Crossmodal-3600 images the registered ordering held: −8.99 [−10.89, −6.99] with one reference, −2.43 [−4.63, −0.30] with all 2–3 references and +19.32 [+17.43, +21.13] under human-verified Open Images labels (95% image-bootstrap intervals, conditional on the systems scored). A second backbone, Qwen3-VL-8B, reproduced it on 537 of these images, with steps of +5.89 [+4.76, +6.87] and +20.10 [+17.39, +23.05]. On PASCAL-50S (seven public LVLMs) the registered primary replication failed at its label-gold step (50 references against VOC labels: −2.29 [−5.15, +0.61]); the slope rose at every registered level from one to 50 references (secondary), and in a registered companion test one- and five-reference gold gave a lower slope than size-matched random deletions from the 50-reference consensus. Two post hoc checks bound the claim: the positive label-gold sign depends on excluding mentions the labels cannot verify (+1.97 [−0.44, +4.01] with the second backbone when they count as errors), and the Crossmodal-3600 reference-gold signs rest on one verbose system. Nine registered predictions failed, including two of five on Crossmodal-3600, and a count-matched leaderboard failed its fidelity check. In public human judgments, relevance falls with object count, but count-matched precision tracks it worse than raw precision.
