**Gold Completeness and the Verbosity Slope of Object Hallucination Metrics: A Registered Audit**

## Abstract

Object hallucination scores such as CHAIR and object F1 are reference-based quality measures whose reference, a gold object set, is usually incomplete. This registered audit tests whether their verbosity slope, the slope of object F1 on log object count, is ordered by gold completeness. On 600 Vietnamese Crossmodal-3600 images the registered ordering held: −8.99 [−10.89, −6.99] with one reference, −2.43 [−4.63, −0.30] with all 2–3 references and +19.32 [+17.43, +21.13] under human-verified Open Images labels (95% image-bootstrap intervals, conditional on the systems scored). A second backbone (Qwen3-VL-8B) reproduced it on 537 of them (steps +5.89 [+4.76, +6.87] and +20.10 [+17.39, +23.05]). On PASCAL-50S the registered primary replication failed at its label-gold step (50 references against VOC labels: −2.29 [−5.15, +0.61]); a post hoc count (no verdict) found fewer VOC-label than reference-named class instances (1,787 vs 2,162), consistent with that failure. The slope still rose at every registered reference level (secondary), and a registered companion test placed one- and five-reference gold below a size-matched random-deletion null. Two post hoc checks bound the claim: the positive label-gold sign depends on excluding unverifiable mentions (+1.97 [−0.44, +4.01] with the second backbone when they count as errors), and the Crossmodal-3600 reference-gold signs rest on one verbose system. Nine registered predictions failed, including two of five on Crossmodal-3600, and a count-matched leaderboard failed its fidelity check. In public human judgments relevance falls with object count, but count-matched precision tracks it worse than raw precision.
