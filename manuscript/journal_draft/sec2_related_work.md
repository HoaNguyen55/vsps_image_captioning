**Object hallucination metrics.** CHAIR (Rohrbach et al., 2018) scores the share of mentioned COCO classes absent from a gold built from instance labels and reference captions. It is precision-type: a caption that names fewer objects can lower it without choosing objects better. POPE (Li et al., 2023) replaces free-form scoring with yes/no probing and notes that CHAIR depends on the instruction and the caption length. CCEval (Zhai et al., 2023) balances mean caption length and object count across models through prompting, on means only. AMBER (Wang et al., 2023) reports a coverage score next to its hallucination rate, and THRONE (Kaul et al., 2024) uses class-wise precision, recall and an F0.5-score. ALOHa (Petryk et al., 2024) and OpenCHAIR (Ben-Kish et al., 2024) extend the vocabulary beyond COCO-80. Two surveys (Liu et al., 2024; Bai et al., 2024) catalogue these metrics without treating object count as a confound<!-- A5.1: REVIEW_A A:21, A:45 SUPPORTED in manuscript/research_r15/VERIFY/verify.json; the Bai et al. clause follows the verifier's note on A:45 -->. **Length and count as confounds.** LeHaCE (Wei et al., 2024), the closest prior work in method, fits a model-specific curve of hallucination against description length and evaluates every model at a uniform length. Our controls instead match the number of mentioned objects per image exactly, with paired intervals and a decomposition, and are applied to other groups' released outputs. SumGD (Min et al., 2025) reports recall next to CHAIR because shorter captions lower it; "Less is More" (Yue et al., 2024) shortens outputs and accepts the recall loss; OPERA (Huang et al., 2024) fixes token budgets, not object counts. **Thresholds, F-measures and incomplete gold.** Lipton et al. (2014) prove that the F1-optimal decision rule adds an item when its probability of being correct exceeds half the attainable F1. Section 4's sign condition is their result; we cite it. Chen et al. (2015, §4.2) model human caption precision against the number of references with one naming probability per word; our propensity model lets it vary within an image. In extreme multi-label learning with missing labels, Jain et al. (2016) propose propensity-scored losses and metrics that counter the bias toward head labels; F-measures are estimable from positive–unlabeled data only under the selected-completely-at-random (SCAR) assumption (Saunders and Freitas, 2022). Our b-channel is the part of the verbosity effect that SCAR missingness cannot produce. In information retrieval, incomplete relevance judgments bias evaluation measures (Buckley and Voorhees, 2004), and recall-oriented measures vary with pool depth (Lu et al., 2016). **Gold quality and rater equivalence.** Making the gold more complete changes model rankings in ImageNet ReaL (Beyer et al., 2020), and RePOPE (Neuhaus and Hein, 2025) revisits POPE's labels. In detection, LVIS (Gupta et al., 2019) builds non-exhaustive labels into the protocol; COCO-ReM (Singh et al., 2024) refines COCO's instance masks and finds that models predicting sharper masks score higher, while Penquitt et al. (2026) find many missing objects but largely stable rankings. **Caption evaluation against human judgments.** THumB 1.0 (Kasai et al., 2022; expert ratings), the Composite set (Aditya et al., 2015; crowd ratings), Polaris (Wada et al., 2024; 550 evaluators) and DOCCI-Critique (Gordon et al., 2025; sentence-level factuality of long paragraphs) provide human judgments of captions. Berger et al. (2025) survey caption-evaluation metrics and their validity. We use these sets only to test what object metrics agree with, not to propose a learned metric. For long captions, CapArena (Cheng et al., 2025; over 6,000 pairwise battles) and LongCap-Arena (Matsuda et al., 2025) collect human judgments, and CAPability (Liu et al., 2025) and CapMAS (Lee et al., 2025) score precision and coverage separately; none matches the number of mentioned objects between the captions compared.
