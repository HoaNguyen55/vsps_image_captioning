**Object hallucination metrics.** CHAIR (Rohrbach et al., 2018) scores the share of mentioned COCO classes absent from a gold built from instance labels and reference captions. It is precision-type: a caption that names fewer objects can lower it without choosing objects better. POPE (Li et al., 2023) replaces free-form scoring with yes/no probing and notes that CHAIR depends on the instruction and the caption length. CCEval (Zhai et al., 2023) balances mean caption length and object count across models through prompting, on means only. AMBER (Wang et al., 2023) reports a coverage score next to its hallucination rate, and THRONE (Kaul et al., 2024) uses class-wise precision, recall and an F0.5-score. ALOHa (Petryk et al., 2024) and OpenCHAIR (Ben-Kish et al., 2024) extend the vocabulary beyond COCO-80, and CAOS (Datta and Sundararaman, 2025) adds out-of-domain objects whose presence an ensemble of LVLMs verifies. **Thresholds, F-measures and incomplete gold.** Lipton et al. (2014) prove that the F1-optimal decision rule adds an item when its probability of being correct exceeds half the attainable F1. Section 4's sign condition is their result; we cite it. Chen et al. (2015, §4.2) model human caption precision against the number of references with one naming probability per word; our propensity model lets it vary within an image. In extreme multi-label learning with missing labels, Jain et al. (2016) propose propensity-scored losses and metrics that counter the bias toward head labels; F-measures are estimable from positive–unlabeled data only under the selected-completely-at-random (SCAR) assumption (Saunders and Freitas, 2022). **Pool bias in information retrieval.** Gold completeness is the captioning counterpart of a known problem in information retrieval. Incomplete relevance judgments bias evaluation measures (Buckley and Voorhees, 2004), pooled judgments raise the question of how reliable large-scale results are (Zobel, 1998), and recall-oriented measures vary with pool depth (Lu et al., 2016). Pooling also disadvantages runs whose documents were left out of the judged pool (Büttcher et al., 2007). Proposed remedies estimate average precision from incomplete and imperfect judgments (Yilmaz and Aslam, 2006), correct the score of an unpooled run by the bias seen on topics where every run is fully judged (Webber and Park, 2009), or compute measures on condensed lists from which unjudged documents are removed (Sakai, 2007); compared under heavily reduced judgments, the condensed-list metrics proved more robust than bpref (Sakai and Kando, 2008). Our setting differs in three ways. (a) A retrieval run is scored to a depth the protocol fixes, whereas a captioner chooses how many objects it names ($M$), so completeness acts through verbosity. (b) What reference gold misses depends on an object's salience within the image, not only on which systems formed a pool; this is the b-channel (Section 4). (c) The gold sets the sign of F1's verbosity reward, not only the size of the bias (Section 7). Condensed F1 carries the IR correction over to captions; its registered test is mixed (Section 7.6).<!-- cite (CC-088 A-3; positioning per each abstract or title, no result claimed beyond the abstracts): buckley2004retrieval DOI 10.1145/1008992.1009000; zobel1998reliable DOI 10.1145/290941.291014; lu2016effect DOI 10.1007/s10791-016-9282-6; yilmaz2006estimating DOI 10.1145/1183614.1183633; sakai2007alternatives DOI 10.1145/1277741.1277756 (all checked 2026-10-21; Sakai 2007's abstract, via api.openalex.org: Q-measure, nDCG and AveP applied to condensed lists beat bpref, so the primed metrics Q′, nDCG′ and AP′ that Sakai and Kando 2008 find superior to bpref are the condensed-list metrics); buttcher2007reliable DOI 10.1145/1277741.1277755; webber2009score DOI 10.1145/1571941.1572018; sakai2008information DOI 10.1007/s10791-008-9059-7 (each checked 2026-10-22 via api.crossref.org, title matched; abstracts via api.openalex.org) -->

(2026) find many missing objects but largely stable rankings. **Caption evaluation against human judgments.** THumB 1.0 (Kasai et al., 2022; expert ratings), the Composite set (Aditya et al., 2015; crowd ratings), Polaris (Wada et al., 2024; 550 evaluators) and DOCCI-Critique (Gordon et al., 2025; sentence-level factuality of long paragraphs) provide human judgments of captions. Berger et al. (2025) survey caption-evaluation metrics and their validity. We use these sets only to test what object metrics agree with, not to propose a learned metric. For long captions, CapArena (Cheng et al., 2025; over 6,000 pairwise battles) and LongCap-Arena (Matsuda et al., 2025) collect human judgments, and CAPability (Liu et al., 2025) and CapMAS (Lee et al., 2025) score precision and coverage separately; none matches the number of mentioned objects between the captions compared.
