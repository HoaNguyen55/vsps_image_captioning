**Object hallucination metrics.** CHAIR (Rohrbach et al., 2018) scores the share of mentioned COCO classes absent from a gold built from instance labels and reference captions. It is precision-type, so naming fewer objects can lower it. POPE (Li et al., 2023) replaces free-form scoring with yes/no probing; CCEval (Zhai et al., 2023) balances mean caption length and object count across models, on means only; AMBER (Wang et al., 2023) reports a coverage score next to its hallucination rate; THRONE (Kaul et al., 2024) uses class-wise precision, recall and an F0.5-score; and ALOHa (Petryk et al., 2024), OpenCHAIR (Ben-Kish et al., 2024) and CAOS (Datta and Sundararaman, 2025) extend the vocabulary beyond COCO-80. **Length and count as confounds.** LeHaCE (Wei et al., 2024), the closest prior work in method, fits a model-specific curve of hallucination against description length and evaluates every model at a uniform length. Hallucinated objects concentrate late in a caption (LURE; Zhou et al., 2024a), and image reliance fades during generation (M3ID; Favero et al., 2024); the matched prefix of Section 3.3 relies on this ordering. Closest in aim, Fazli et al. (2026) re-evaluate six inference-time mitigation methods and conclude that "current evaluation protocols may overestimate progress by rewarding conservative generation". **F-measures, thresholds and incomplete gold.** Lipton et al. (2014) prove that the F1-optimal decision rule adds an item when its probability of being correct exceeds half the attainable F1; Section 4's sign condition is their result. F-measures are estimable from positive–unlabeled data only under the selected-completely-at-random (SCAR) assumption (Saunders and Freitas, 2022), and species-accumulation curves (Colwell et al., 2012; Chao et al., 2014) supply the expected gold size. In information retrieval, incomplete relevance judgments bias evaluation measures (Buckley and Voorhees, 2004), and condensed lists without unjudged documents are more robust (Sakai, 2007; Sakai and Kando, 2008). **Gold quality.** Making the gold more complete changes model rankings in ImageNet ReaL (Beyer et al., 2020), and RePOPE (Neuhaus and Hein, 2025) revisits POPE's labels. Closest to our question, Feng et al. (2024) argue that technical flaws in object-hallucination metrics make conclusions about detail and hallucination unreliable and propose embedding-based metrics; we keep the object-level metrics and vary their gold. Caption writers choose what to mention (human reporting bias; Misra et al., 2016), and which objects people name has been studied as object importance (Spain and Perona, 2011; Berg et al., 2012). **Position of this paper.** First, the gold is the experimental factor: each caption is matched once and scored against nested reference golds and instance or verified labels (Section 3.1), whereas the gold-quality studies above change classification or probing labels. Second, the count control matches the number of mentioned objects on every image of released captions, where LeHaCE fits length curves and CCEval balances means. Third, every prediction is registered or labelled exploratory or post hoc, failures included (Table 1). Prior work knows that incomplete gold inflates hallucination scores (Rohrbach et al., 2018) and that writers name objects selectively (Misra et al., 2016); this paper adds gold completeness as a controlled factor that can set the sign of F1's verbosity slope, and a size-matched deletion null that quantifies that reporting bias on a 50-reference gold.
