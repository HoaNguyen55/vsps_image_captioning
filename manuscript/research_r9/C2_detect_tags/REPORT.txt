R9 / C2_detect_tags: open-vocabulary detection and multi-label tags as recall evidence
=====================================================================================
Agent C2, 2026-10-13. Repo read-only. CPU pilots only (nice 15, 1 thread, < 1 GB). Scripts and outputs are in
this folder. The literature dumps are in litA/ (taggers and detectors) and litB/ (tag-aided captioning and
hallucination).

0. VERDICT (measured ceilings first)
------------------------------------
- KTVIC: detector or tagger evidence cannot get P, R or F1 to 90. The measured ceilings are far below that.
  - The real Grounding-DINO tags add about 0 F1.
  - A perfect verifier over the existing COCO-80 tags caps short-mode F1 at 62-67.
  - A perfect COCO-80 tagger, rendered with the right Vietnamese word and perfectly verified, caps R at
    64-67 (short) and 74.5 (detailed), and F1 at about 73-74.
  - Humans do worse than the target:
    - An independent 5-caption human panel (UIT-OpenViIC), scored against the KTVIC gold: P/R/F1 45.5/72.4/55.9.
    - One OpenViIC caption: 55.5/37.6/44.8.
    - A held-out KTVIC annotator (CONTEXT): P 85.3 / R 46.9, i.e. F1 about 60.5.
  - The KTVIC gold rewards the KTVIC annotators' salience and wording conventions. It does not reward
    perception, and detectors supply perception only.
- COCO (gold = instances only): caption F1 of 90 is above the human level.
  - One human COCO reference scores P/R/F1 92.6/56.6/70.3 against the instance gold; the union of all 5
    references scores 85.3/74.3/79.5.
  - F1 >= 90 is reachable only as an object INVENTORY: the caption's classes unioned with the output of a
    near-SOTA multi-label tagger.
  - Simulation with the best published COCO operating point (Query2Label, OP 89.2 / OR 84.6): union F1
    87.8-89.0. Reaching >= 90 also needs a verifier that removes about 40% of the tagger's false positives at
    no TP loss; a perfect verifier gives 92.6-93.7.
  - This is the "list every class" gaming the CONTEXT warns about. On KTVIC, one appended inventory clause
    costs 54 syllable-CIDEr-D (165.5 -> 111.0).
- The one design with a defensible novelty and a positive expected sign is copy-guarded tag-conditioned SFT
  (Proposal A, sec. 5). Expected gain: KTVIC F1 +1 to +2.5, COCO F1 +1 to +4.

1. HOW TAGS MAP TO THE TWO METRICS (CPU, existing files)
----------------------------------------------------------
Tags: data/results/tags_gdino.json, from Grounding-DINO-base over the 80 COCO names. They exist for the 558
KTVIC test images only; no train, dev or COCO tags exist.
- 2.39 tags per image. Rendered with the first Vietnamese entry of coco80_vi.json, 2.12 per image are visible to
  the 113-entry KTVIC lexicon (objects_in). 31 images have no visible tag.
- Only 43 of the 80 COCO first entries are visible to the KTVIC lexicon at all; the other 37 COCO classes score
  neither TP nor FP on KTVIC.
- All 80 first entries round-trip through COCO matcher v2 (vi_objects) to their own class, so Vietnamese
  rendering loses nothing on COCO.
Share of KTVIC gold objects (union of the 5 references) that are COCO-80 categories:
- curated strict (person and its hyponyms + 26 classes): 42.2% micro, 44.7% per image;
- reachable by the first Vietnamese entry, i.e. what the current tags can hit: 32.4%;
- any coco80_vi synonym: 51.1%. This one is inflated by artifacts such as bồn cầu -> cầu (bridge) and
  trường kỷ -> trường (school).
- Your 39% lies between the first-entry and curated-strict figures.
Open vocabulary: with this agent's English glosses of the 113 lexicon classes (ram_cover.py), RAM's 4,585-tag
vocabulary contains 110/113 classes. That covers 96.5% of gold objects and 96.2% of fuse2's misses, against
36.8% for strict COCO-80. Coverage is NOT the bottleneck (sec. 2).

Metric: exact object_prf (P micro, R per-image mean, F1). Paired bootstrap B=1000 over images, against each

- The precision-safe filter (add a class only if its cross-fit precision exceeds the base P) adds nothing to
  any trained model.
- Rank-2 union is worse than rank-1.
- Oracle bounds, cocoFirst = M ∪ (gold ∩ first-entry COCO classes):
  - P1 s42 83.9/58.9/69.2; fuse2 s42 short 81.0/62.3/70.5; fuse2 s42 detailed 71.6/71.5/71.6.
  - Curated strict COCO-80: P1 85.2/64.3/73.3; fuse2 short 82.4/66.8/73.8; fuse2 detailed 72.6/74.5/73.5.
- The oracle-verified headroom is mostly wording:
  - 55% of the verified additions are "người";
  - 25-47% re-express an object the caption already names at another granularity (người beside phụ nữ).
  - Real perception headroom is therefore about R +5 in short mode.

2c. What tags in the prompt actually do (pilot3_uptake.py; existing tags_* runs)
Share of visible tags the caption names:
- fuse2 24.1% -> tags_fuse2 24.4%; P1 s43 22.8% -> 25.7%. The trained adapters do not use the tag channel.
- zero-shot 25.0% -> 42.5%, but precision of tag-class mentions falls from 73.9 to 60.8: it copies.
fuse2 short gains P (75.7 -> 77.9), but not from tag evidence:
- precision of tagged mentions moves 85.6 -> 84.4;
- the gain sits in non-COCO mentions (P 73.1 -> 76.3, 1.32 -> 1.26 per image) and untagged COCO mentions
  (69.9 -> 72.7). This is a prompt (typicality) effect, consistent with V5 sec. 7.
- Prediction for R4's G6(a): the placebo-tag control will reproduce most of dP.
Without retraining, a COCO class mention is precise when tagged (P 82-86, SFT models) and less so when untagged
(65-75): absence of a tag is a weak veto.

2d. Detector evidence versus sample self-consistency (pilot4_propensity.py, pilot4b_realise.py)
Setup:
- Candidates = (tags ∪ classes in the 6 existing fuse2_s42 T-samples) minus the greedy classes.
- A logistic propensity model is trained on the other fold.
- Decision rules: add when p > F1/2 (F1-optimal, Lipton et al. 2014) or when p > P (precision-safe).
Object-set level, fuse2 s42 short:
| candidates | AUROC (2 folds) | F1-optimal rule | precision-safe rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| tags only | 0.73 / 0.69 | +0.4 [-0.2,+1.1] | +0.0 |
| samples only | 0.67 / 0.65 | 62.1/67.9/64.9, +6.8 [+5.0,+8.5] (P -13.6) | +0.5 [+0.2,+0.8] |
| tags + samples | 0.78 / 0.73 | 63.2/66.3/64.7, +6.6 (P -12.5) | +0.8 [+0.4,+1.2] (R +1.0, P 0.0) |
Realised as real captions (pick the T-sample that names the added classes, closest to greedy):
- The gains vanish: precision-safe F1 +0.2 / +0.1; F1-optimal F1 -3.7 / -3.3, with syllable CIDEr-D -32 / -29.
- The detector's marginal value over samples, realised: dF1 -0.1 [-0.4,+0.2] (precision-safe) and
  +0.3 [-0.3,+1.0] (F1-optimal).
- Tags carry a little AUROC beyond samples but almost no realisable F1. Realisation, not detection, is the
  bottleneck.

Pattern across the literature:
- Tags without training on them give about 0 (TUNA-, MARINE's prompt-only baseline, this repo's P1 and fuse2).
- Detectors used post hoc or at decoding mostly buy precision. When they add recall it is +3 to +4 (MARINE), or
  a large recall gain that costs CIDEr (CBS -6.7; nocaps -18.5).
- Training WITH tags and WITH masking (ViECap, PS3, TUNA) keeps most of the gain at a small CIDEr cost.

5. PROPOSALS
------------
A. Copy-guarded tag-conditioned SFT, "CG-TagSFT" (recommended; modest)
Mechanism:
- Tag KTVIC train, dev300 and test, the COCO probe and UIT-ViIC with an open-vocabulary tagger over the
  113-class KTVIC lexicon. Use RAM++, or GDINO-T / OWLv2 with the English glosses in ram_cover.py
  (110/113 classes covered). Add COCO-80 names for COCO.
- Train the fuse2 recipe with the tag list in the prompt (evaluate.py --tags format), with:
  (i) 40% per-tag dropout and 50% whole-list dropout (ViECap's optimum; the model must still work without tags);
  (ii) noise-matched distractor tags: absent classes drawn from the tagger's own false-positive confusion on
      train images, at its measured FP rate;
  (iii) an unlikelihood copy-guard: a penalty on the target-side tokens of a distractor class, so copying an
       injected absent tag is punished explicitly.
- Targets stay human (fuse2), so the model learns which detected objects these annotators mention (Misra's
  relevance factor) instead of copying.
Novel element vs the closest prior work:
- ViECap (2307.16525) only drops entities and never injects false ones (zero-shot, CLIP entities).
- TUNA (2406.10839) trains on retrieved tags, with no dropout and no copy penalty.
- HallE-Control (2310.01779) splits ground-truth objects into grounded and omitted, with an inference knob.
- HALVA (2405.18654) and EFUF (2402.09801) contrast hallucinated objects without a tag channel.
- R4's planned 79a (tag-rate 0.5, COCO-80 GDINO tags) is dropout only; R4 itself labels it a baseline
  (OSCAR / VinVL / Tag2Text).
- The new part: (ii) and (iii) together, in a VLM SFT on salience-filtered human targets, with an open-vocabulary
  lexicon tagger. The novelty is moderate (a combination); say so.
Evidence and expected effect:
- The channel is unused without training: uptake 24% -> 24%. Without dropout the model copies: zero-shot uptake
  42.5% at tag precision 60.8; ViECap collapses.
- Ceilings:
  - oracle-verified COCO-80 tags: R +8 / F1 +7 to +8 (short), about +3 (detailed);
  - the real perception share is about R +5 (sec. 2b);
  - an open-vocabulary tagger covers 96% of misses, but salience limits it (tag precision against references
    34%, 54% with the panel).
- Literature: Tag2Text, clean tags, caption-object R +7 and P +3; TUNA, trained vs prompt-only, +3.6 POPE F1.
- Expected:
  - KTVIC short: P +1 to +2, R +1 to +3, F1 +1 to +2.5 (fuse2 58.1 -> about 59-61);
  - KTVIC detailed: F1 +0.5 to +2;
  - COCO: F1 +1 to +4 (59.4 -> at most about 63). Uptake follows KTVIC habits (about 1 object per caption), far
    below the union bound of 79-89.
CIDEr cost:
- Expected -2 to +3 word-level CIDEr. Prompt-only tags measured +2.5 on fuse2 and +1.1 on P1; ViECap keeps
  in-domain CIDEr with masking.
- Report CIDEr, BLEU-4 and CHAIR next to P/R/F1, and add placebo-tag and oracle-tag control rows (R4 jobs 71,
  G6 and G7).
GPU on one 3090:
- tagging about 1 GPU-h;
- SFT seed 42 plus dev and test evals with, without, placebo and oracle tags: about 7 GPU-h (R4 costs 79a at
  5.5);
- seeds 43 and 44: about 11 GPU-h;
- total about 8 GPU-h to the gate, about 19 GPU-h for 3 seeds.
- Code: about 80 lines (distractor sampler in build_gold_sft --tags; unlikelihood term in the train_stage2
  collator/loss).
Kill rule (dev300, seed 42, vs fuse2 s42 without tags), dropping the method if any of these holds:
- R4's G7 fails: dR short < +2.0, or dP < -1.0, or F1 without tags moves outside ±1;
- the placebo-tag control recovers >= 70% of dF1;
- more than 10% of injected distractors are named;
- word-level CIDEr < fuse2 - 3.

B. Presence x relevance evidence completion, "PREC" (COCO-oriented; flagged as metric-shaped)
Mechanism:
- The decision layer is a calibrated P(gold | image, class) = P(present | tagger posterior, VLM yes/no) x
  P(relevant | present, gold type).
- Relevance is learned from each dataset's own TRAIN gold: KTVIC references (salience); COCO-2014-train
  instance labels (relevance about 1, existence only).
- Classes are added by the F1-optimal plug-in rule (p > F1/2, Lipton 1402.1892) or a precision-safe rule.
- Realisation is a prefix-preserving constrained continuation of the greedy caption (one model-written clause),
  not a template.
- For COCO the tagger must be clean of the Karpathy test images:
  - a classifier trained on COCO-2014 train only (ML-Decoder protocol, 82,081 images; Karpathy test ⊂ val2014);
  - or a COCO-free detector (GDINO-T, YOLO-World, DINO-X stage 1).
Novel element:
- Misra (1512.06974) factorises presence x relevance for training classifiers, not for completing captions.
- MARINE, Woodpecker, VFC and HALC model presence only.
- HallE-Control has a knob but no decision rule.
- PREC applies the factorisation per gold type, which explains the measured asymmetry: tags add about 0 on KTVIC
  (realised detector marginal -0.1 to +0.3) and up to +28 to +30 F1 at object-set level on COCO.
Expected:
- KTVIC: 0 to +0.8 F1 at the precision-safe rule (measured object-set +0.8 [+0.4,+1.2]; realised by sample
  selection about +0.1 to +0.2).
- COCO with a Q2L-level tagger: union bound F1 87.8-89.0, P about 86-87. A real verifier lands between 88 and
  the perfect-verifier 92.6-93.7.
CIDEr cost: large and must be reported.
- Inventory clause on KTVIC: -54 syllable CIDEr-D. CBS: -6.7 (4 tags). nocaps: -18.5.
- Measure on UIT-ViIC test, which has Vietnamese references AND COCO instance gold on the same 231 images.
GPU:
- 0 GPU-h for the tagger with a public COCO-2014-train checkpoint, plus about 0.3 GPU-h inference. Training one
  is about 8-10 GPU-h (TResNet-L 448 on 82k images, rough).
- About 1 GPU-h for VLM yes/no verification (2,500 images x about 3 candidates).
- About 0.5 GPU-h for the re-decode.
Kill rule, dropping the method if any of these holds:
- object-set F1 < 88 at P >= 88 on held-out COCO-2014-TRAIN images (never test);
- UIT-ViIC word-level CIDEr drop > 10;
- the realised COCO F1 gain is < 50% of the object-set gain.
If it ships, report it as a "caption + verified inventory" row, never as captioning F1.

6. FLAGS
--------
- All tags are model-generated signals inside the method; none is evidence.
- OpenViIC captions were used only as an evaluation panel (sec. 2a, 2f).
- tags_gdino.json exists only for KTVIC test. Every KTVIC pilot rule is parameter-free or 2-fold cross-fitted on
  test; the descriptive per-class precision table drives no decision. A real run must calibrate on dev300 (tag it
  first).
- Leakage on COCO:
  - the HF grounding-dino-base checkpoint is trained with COCO (README), and about 87% of Karpathy-test (val2014)
    images are in train2017, so its COCO-probe tags would be contaminated;
  - RAM was trained on 113K COCO images (Karpathy train split is likely; check the overlap with Karpathy test);
  - MARINE's DETR has the same issue.
- The COCO numbers in sec. 3 are a SIMULATION: random false-positive classes, two miss models; real tagger
  false positives co-occur with caption false positives, which would make the union's P slightly higher.
- Syllable CIDEr-D is a diagnostic: the paper's numbers are word-level (RDRSegmenter).
- Gaming:
  - the person-word granularity (người plus phụ nữ) carries 55% of the oracle tag headroom on KTVIC;
  - listing COCO classes gives the COCO gains;
  - both raise P/R/F1 without better captions.
