## E6-2. The corrected scope and novelty statement (replaces E-A.3 of ERRATUM_O31_5, l.73 and l.75)

The quoted text below replaces the quoted paragraph of l.73 and the provisional paragraph of l.75. The verified-source list of l.76-80 stays as committed; the sources of the works added here are in `PRIOR_NEW.tsv` and `PRIOR_ART_O31_v4.tsv`. The labels (a), (b) and (c) are kept, because E-B3 (l.234) and the tail of the statement refer to them.

> **The question.** This study asks one question: for supervised fine-tuning of a Vietnamese captioner on verified propositions, and for object existence only, does the fine-tuning do more than make the student say less? The answer is carried by the registered tests of (c) and (b). The measurement (a) is a component check of the verifier that builds the training data; it is reported in the method and results, not as a separate contribution. The study's registered headline (H1 and H2, §6.1) and its claim form (§1) are unchanged; the question frames the novelty statement only.
>
> **What earlier work already shows.** Among the 23 works we read in full, earlier work has already shown five things, and we claim none of them.
> - First, the setting of a training-data filter or verifier changes the fine-tuned model, and its coverage. HalluciDoctor (App. B.2, Table 5) fine-tunes after each of five consistency thresholds, from 0.1 to 0.9, reads the students on CHAIR and MME, and picks the threshold on those benchmarks. KnowAda (Fig. 3; Sec. 5.2) fine-tunes students on captions adapted at five thresholds of a knowledge filter, which removes from human-written or Gemini-written captions the details that the model cannot answer, and on the original captions; their descriptiveness recall rises with the threshold, and, on the supervision side, it reports for four settings of its filter (legend 0.1, 0.33, 0.5 and 0.8, read from the plot; a scale it does not tie to the T of Fig. 3) the distribution of unknown questions per caption, on captions it does not name (Fig. 7; App. A.4), and word counts at one threshold only (Table 2). OVFact (Figs. 4-6; Sec. 4.3) fine-tunes students on data filtered at four ratios and on the full data, with three training runs for its main comparison, and reports their CHAIR and recall; one of its variants selects the training captions on a recall score whose references come from grounding tools. Less is More (Sec. 3.2.1, Table 3) trains a student at each of three filter ratios and reports its recall beside the length of its training data. SENTINEL (Tables 8 and 9) shows the effect of the verifier's setting under preference optimisation.
> - Second, the annotation source of the reward changes a student's object coverage under reinforcement learning (Beyond Saying Less, Table 2: on DOPA labels, Cover 52.0 when trained with MS COCO annotations and 69.6 with the denser DOPA annotations, while on MS COCO labels the order is reversed, 78.8 against 73.7), and reward weights give operating points between hallucination and omission (ClaimDiff-RL, Table 3).
> - Third, a verifier can leave an uncertain middle band unused, as EFUF does with its two CLIP thresholds and SENTINEL with detector disagreement.
> - Fourth, rules and models that build or select training data have been checked against human labels. EFUF (Sec. 3.1-3.2, Table 1; App. A.1) labels by hand every object in 200 captions written by two models, reports how the score of the CLIP rule that selects its training samples differs between hallucinated and correct objects, gives, among the objects that score above its accept threshold, the share that are hallucinated (0.6 % and 1.6 % for the two models), and among those below its reject threshold, the share that are not (2.3 % and 1.7 %), and sets the thresholds from that sample; these are error shares within each score band (Sec. 3.2), and it reports no retention and no false-accept rate at those thresholds. HSA-DPO (Tables 1 and 2) scores the detector that builds its preference data on two labelled benchmarks, one of them built and labelled by hand by its authors (Table 2), beside other detectors on the same items. OVFact (Table 1) reports the share of hand-annotated entities of human captions that the grounding step of its filter score finds, beside ablated versions of that step. HalDec-Bench (Table 2, Fig. 8) scores vision-language models as sentence-level hallucination detectors against human labels, and as filters by the precision of the sentences they keep. Hallucination metrics, judges and preference data have also been checked against human labels (MOCHa, Table 1; ClaimDiff-RL, §4.3; RLAIF-V, Table 3), and the share of correct objects that an inference-time revisor removes has been reported, about 1.6 %, on the data of its main evaluation, with the gold not stated in that appendix (LURE, App. C.2, Table 17).
> - Fifth, trained models have been compared with shortened or length-matched output. Less is More (Fig. 5) sets models trained with its end-of-sentence objective, not its data-filtered students, beside the base model's captions cut to their first R % of words and beside length-penalty decoding, each at several settings, on a hallucination–recall plot, with no matched count and no test; it also reports which of the base model's objects its trained models omit (App. B.3, Table 5) and the correct and hallucinated objects per caption of student and base model (Table 6). KnowAda (Fig. 3; Sec. 4; Table 3) compares its students with students trained on captions trimmed by whole sentences at several degrees, and with a student trained on captions from which information was removed at random instead of the unknown details (KnowAda Random, Sec. 5.4, Table 3); Table 3 lists 55 words for the captions of both students. CCEval (HallE-Control, Sec. 2.1 and App. A.4) keeps the average caption length and object count of the compared models similar by giving them the same prompt, without enforcing it. HALVA (Sec. 4.1, Table 1) reports generation lengths close to its base model's (for example 98 against 100.9).
>
> Multilingual object-hallucination mitigation also exists, at training time for English and 12 other languages (MHR, Sec. IV-B, Table I) and at inference time for seven languages other than English, with English as the reference (CLAIM, Table 1; App. B); Vietnamese is in neither. All three registered lines below are touched by this work; what remains to the study is a set of registered measurements and tests, not a new method (E-A.2 items 5 and 6).
>
> **What this study contributes, for supervised fine-tuning on verified propositions and for object existence only.**
>
> (c) The answer to the question. Three registered tests on the Karpathy test images (5,000 before the registered removals), a set spent by FAIR 2026 and used here in its development-confirm role; each verdict is a registered test on this rehearsal set, never a confirmation (l.157). T-1 tests whether, at a matched number of mentioned objects per image, the student's object precision is higher than the base model's precision on its own first-mentioned objects; the instrument is the count-matched object precision of Objective 3.2 (the companion study's instrument, cited), used as an instrument only, with no claim about the instrument and no raw-versus-matched contrast (§5). T-2 and T-3 test whether the student has both higher object recall and fewer hallucinated objects per caption than two length-controlled versions of the base model: its captions cut to their first j\* sentences, with j\* fixed on development data (one cut point), and its captions under one fixed two-sentence prompt. Where Less is More drew curves over several settings, our tests use one cut point and one prompt; what they add is the per-image matching and the registered paired tests. Beside these tests, and without a test, the paper reports the base model's sentence-cut curve read at the student's recall (O1 of ERRATUM_O31_6), the deletion of unverified sentences from the base model's captions at inference (E-B2), and the split of the student's recall gain into a verifier part and a budget part (E-B6).
>
> (b) The supervision link. Under supervised fine-tuning, the student's object recall at five points: the earlier FAIR recipe (S1), which differs from the other four both in its verifier rule (FAIR's recorded verdicts) and in its budget (B = 9 against the scaled B_i; E-B6); and four students of the O31 recipe at verifier operating points fixed before training by registered rules (OP\*, OP_mid, OP_25 and OP_75). Each point is reported beside the object recall of its training captions, measured before training against the reference captions of the training images. T-4 tests, on the rehearsal set above, whether the student's recall rises in the order of that supervision recall. The two recalls use different gold and different averaging (the supervision recall is a per-image mean on caption-derived gold of KTVIC train; the student's recall is a ratio of sums on COCO instance gold), so T-4 tests order only, not a slope or a ratio. By construction the supervision recall at the five points can move together with the length of the training targets and with where they end (the detailed budget B_i of §4.4 grows with the number of supported entities), and Less is More (Sec. 3.2.2, Table 3) shows that a student's output length and recall can follow how the end of its targets is supervised while their length hardly changes (rows 1 and 6: training-data length 178.3 and 176.8, output length 57.8 and 100.6, recall 64.8 and 68.6); T-4 therefore cannot say whether an order it finds comes from what the supervision contains or from how much it says and where it stops, and the paper says so beside T-4. OP_25, OP_mid and OP_75 have one training run each (seed 42), with the seed variance pooled from the three seeds of S1 and of S2 (ERRATUM_O31_4 E4-7). Reported without a test: the hallucinated-object count at the five points; the transfer ratio on KTVIC test, with caption gold on both sides; and, if the five points are identified, the verifier's retention on the balanced set at each point with a descriptive slope of student recall against it (O2 of ERRATUM_O31_6). Among the works we read in full, none reports, beside each student, the object recall of its training captions against gold, measured before training, or tests the order: Less is More reports the length of each filtered training set (Table 3), KnowAda the distribution of unknown questions per caption at four filter settings (Fig. 7), a count of the details its filter treats as unknown and not a recall against gold, and word counts at one threshold only (Table 2), RLAIF-V the human agreement of the training pairs (Table 3), and OVFact selects on a tool-based recall.
>
> (a) The component check. For the verifier that builds the training data, at the operating point used to train the student (OP\*, fixed at P1 before any training): its retention of present objects and its false-accept rate on absent objects, on COCO-80 class propositions of 231 images (UIT-ViIC test, in its development role), against COCO-2014 instance annotations (near-complete instance gold, §1), on propositions that no trained student produced, with every setting fixed before the first student output. The propositions are the classes the base model mentions (P-a; on P-a the false-accept rate is reported but never read, l.331) and a balanced set P-b of every present class plus three co-occurring and three random absent classes per image, sampled after the adversarial and random settings of POPE (Li et al., 2023, Sec. 5.1); P-b is not the POPE benchmark. The rates are set beside two rules re-implemented after published ones, a two-detector rule after SENTINEL and a CLIP-threshold rule after EFUF, each run only if its settings can be read from its saved source (E-B5), and beside the earlier FAIR rule, run from its recorded code, on the same image–class pairs. Under the saved sources, the rule after SENTINEL is NOT RUN, because the confidence threshold of its second detector is in no saved file, and the rule after EFUF can be run, with a second descriptive row after EFUF's released scorer (E6-7.1, E6-7.4 and E6-7.7 of ERRATUM_O31_6). If both re-implemented rules fail closed, the comparison is with the earlier FAIR rule only and the word "published" is not used. Against the earlier FAIR rule only, the same two rates are measured on the base model's mentions of the rehearsal set (H4 as rehearsed) and, if tier 3 runs, in H4's registered test. The balanced-set rows, the rows of the other rules and the rehearsal of H4 exist only if the seed-42 development stop K1 did not fire; if K1 fires, line (a) reduces to the retention of the O31 verifier on P-a, whose false-accept rate is not read. Scope: only the existence of the 80 COCO classes is measured; the verifier also filters attribute, action and counting propositions in training, which no measurement here covers (E-A.2 item 1). The O31 families are probed in Vietnamese, the detectors and CLIP with English class names. The training images have only caption-derived gold, so retention is measured on other images than those the verifier filtered. The nearest precedent is EFUF (Sec. 3.1-3.2, Table 1), with HSA-DPO, OVFact and HalDec-Bench as above; among the works we read in full, none reports retention and false-accept rate together for the verifier that built fine-tuning data, at its training operating point, on instance gold, beside another verifier rule on the same pairs.
>

### O1. The base model's sentence-cut curve, read at each student's recall (CC-528 O1, J1)

- **Question:** At the same object recall as a student, how many hallucinated objects per caption does the base model have when its own captions are cut by whole sentences?
- **Metric and unit:** the image is the unit; R and h as §5 (R = 100 × ΣTP / Σ|G|, a ratio of sums; h = Σ hallucinated mentions / N captions).
  - **The cut grid.** J = (1, 2, …, 12, full), in this order. The values 1 to 12 are the range of j\* (E-B1, l.143); "full" is S0's uncut caption, added here so that the curve ends at S0 itself. "S0 cut at j" keeps S0's first j sentences under the sentence rule of E-B1 (l.141); a caption with fewer than j sentences is kept whole. Mentions are extracted again from the cut text with the frozen extractor (as l.142).
  - **The curve.** For each j in J, R_j and h_j of S0 cut at j, on the set's image list and gold.
  - **The student.** R_X and h_X of student X on the same list and gold. Where X has three seeds, each image's counts are the mean over the seeds before any sum (G-4); otherwise its single decode is used.
  - **The reference value.** A segment is a pair of consecutive grid entries (u, v) in the order of J. The segment brackets R_X if min(R_u, R_v) ≤ R_X ≤ max(R_u, R_v). On a bracketing segment with R_u ≠ R_v, its value is h_u + (R_X − R_u) × (h_v − h_u) / (R_v − R_u); on one with R_u = R_v, its value is min(h_u, h_v). The reference value h_ref(R_X) is the smallest value over all bracketing segments (the value most favourable to S0). The reported segment is the first in the order of J that attains it.
  - **Out of range.** If no segment brackets R_X, that is if R_X < min_j R_j or R_X > max_j R_j, the row reports "out of range, below" or "out of range, above", with R_X and [min_j R_j, max_j R_j], and no Δh and no interval.
  - **Reported:** Δh_X = h_X − h_ref(R_X), with its 95 % interval; the reported segment; R_X and h_X; the curve, that is (R_j, h_j) with words and distinct classes per caption for every j in J; and the number of S0 captions with more than 12 sentences (for which "full" differs from j = 12).
  - **Interval.** The paired image bootstrap of G-4 (B = 10,000, seed 20261127, the shared index matrix of the set). In each replicate the whole curve, R_X, h_X and h_ref are recomputed from the resampled images. A replicate in which no segment brackets R_X\* is undefined: it is counted, reported and left out of the interval (a rule fixed here, applied as ERRATUM_O31_1 E9 applies its own undefined replicates). The percentile interval is E9's.
- **Data and role:** tier 1 (KTVIC test 558 with the 113-noun CHAIR-vi lexicon on references; UIT-ViIC test 231 with COCO-80 instance gold, G-2) and tier 2 (the Karpathy test list of the registered tier-2 rows, instance gold, E11). Never tier 3.

O1 never causes a decode.

For a student, its decode on that set does not exist, or its arm stopped under K3 or K4 before that decode: that student's row is NOT RUN. Each NOT RUN row is reported with its cause.

### E6-7.2 C-VCD (E-B4)

**Facts (VCD repository at commit d6568ff8; paper 2311.16922v1; the run repository at 071c8dc for the port).**
- The released VCD code adds noise with `add_diffusion_noise` (`vcd_utils/vcd_add_noise.py` l.3-28):
  - 1,000 steps (l.4);
  - β_t = sigmoid(linspace(−6, 6, 1000)) × (0.5e-2 − 1e-5) + 1e-5 (l.7-8), which runs from about 2e-5 to 5e-3;
  - the step index is t itself (l.20-26), and the noise is `torch.randn_like` (l.19);
  - it is applied to the processor's normalised pixel tensor (`experiments/eval/object_hallucination_vqa_llava.py` l.57, l.60).
- Run defaults: α 1 (`object_hallucination_vqa_llava.py` l.117), β 0.1 (l.118) and noise step 500 (l.115). A shell wrapper sets β to 0.2 (`experiments/cd_scripts/llava1.5_pope.bash` l.6).
- The VCD paper: "set α = 1, β = 0.1, and γ = 0.1 unless explicitly stated" (§4.1, saved text l.332), and a noise step of 500 for MME and LLaVA-Bench and 999 for POPE (appendix, saved text l.833).
- The group's port, used by FAIR 2026 (`scripts/baselines.py` sha 1b7bf812, l.80-90, run repository at 071c8dc):
  - β linear from 1e-4 to 0.02 over 1,000 steps;
  - step index t − 1;
  - noise added in pixel space scaled to [0, 1], then clipped and quantised to 8 bits;
  - numpy seed 42 per image.
- Writer's arithmetic from these two formulas: at T = 500, the factor that multiplies the image is √ᾱ = 0.863 under the released schedule and 0.280 under the port's. The port's contrastive image keeps much less of the image.

**Under the committed rules.** l.271: "**C-VCD:** α = 1, β = 0.1 (adaptive plausibility), and diffusion noise step T = 500 of a 1,000-step linear schedule (β_t from 1e-4 to 0.02), with the noise seed 42 per image." l.273: "The noise is the port's `diffusion_noise` … The schedule was not checked against VCD's released code, and the paper states this."
- No saved-source rule governs C-VCD, and the CC-526 sentence does not name it.
- **C-VCD RUNS as committed:** α 1, β 0.1, T 500, the port's linear schedule, seed 42 per image, greedy, batch 1.
- Its α, β and T agree with the released code's run defaults and the paper's §4.1. Its noise does not: it differs from the released code in schedule, index, space and clipping.
- The committed sentence "was not checked" is no longer the fact; E6-7.7 replaces it (C4, option B). No setting of C-VCD changes.

### E6-7.4 VR-EFUF (E-B5) and the CLIP scorer of M5

**Facts (EFUF repository at commit e0798b69; paper 2402.09801v3).**
- CLIP checkpoint `openai/clip-vit-base-patch32` (`insight/clip.py` l.126). Image preprocessing: `CLIPProcessor.from_pretrained` of the same checkpoint, with no override (l.222, l.278), applied to each window crop (l.120).
- Patch size 48 px (`common/args.py` l.90). Window 4 patches on each side (l.91; windows built in `insight/clip.py` l.110-117). Stride 1 patch (the loops of l.112-115).
- Text form "A photo containing " + object + "." (`args.py` l.44; `clip.py` l.46). Score scale: `logits_per_image` of the CLIP model, which includes CLIP's logit scale (`clip.py` l.132).
- Thresholds 23 and 32 (`args.py` l.100, l.103).
- The repository's object score: each patch gets the mean score of the windows that cover it, and the object's score is the mean of its top-4 patches (`clip.py` l.177-185; `--average_top_k` 4, `args.py` l.92). The paper's Eq. 1 takes the maximum over windows (2402.09801v3, §3.1).
- Which of the two forms produced the analysis from which the thresholds were set (App. A.1: "Based on the analysis in Section 3") is stated in neither the paper text nor the code.

**Under the committed rules.** l.347: "The CLIP checkpoint, image preprocessing, patch size, window size, stride, the text form of an object and the score scale are taken from them and recorded with file and line"; l.348: "the maximum over windows, as in the paper's Eq. 1 (saved full text). The repository's `average_top_k` default is not used"; l.349: "thresholds 32 and 23".
- Every value is readable.
- **VR-EFUF RUNS:**
  - CLIP ViT-B/32 with its own processor, 48 px patches, windows of 4 × 4 patches, stride 1 patch;
  - "A photo containing <English class name>.", scored by `logits_per_image`;
  - S = the maximum over windows; SUPPORTED iff S > 32, REJECTED iff S < 23.
- The paper states that the repository aggregates differently (l.348). Because the maximum over windows is never below the repository's top-4 patch mean, VR-EFUF may accept more than EFUF's own code at the same thresholds. E6-7.7 (C5) registers a second descriptive row with the released aggregation.
- The preprocessing values themselves live in the checkpoint's own processor file, which is pinned with the model by revision and per-file sha (E-D.1); ruling CC-531r2 holds that this counts as read in the saved files, because `clip.py` fixes the preprocessing by naming the checkpoint's own processor with no override (l.120, l.222), and that file is part of the pinned model; a setting that a release leaves to the default of a library whose version it does not pin is not read so (E6-7.1).

### E6-7.5 M5's batch size

**Facts (EFUF repository at commit e0798b69).**
- The repository has no single batch-size field. It marks a default of 1 for each of four streams per batch: positive objects (`--train_bs_pos`, `common/args.py` l.137-141), gold captions (`--train_bs_gold`, l.143-147), whole sentences (`--train_bs_sent`, l.149-153) and negative objects (`--train_bs_neg`, l.155).

The paper's App. A.1 gives λ₁ = 0.3 and λ₂ = 0.2, and its objective has no gold-caption term.

**Under the committed rules.** l.693: "The batch size is the EFUF repository default, read in the saved repository files (the EFUF source rule of E-B5) and recorded with file and line; if it cannot be read there, M5 is NOT RUN"; plus the CC-526 sentence. M5's own settings follow the paper (l.685-690: three streams, λ₁ 0.3, λ₂ 0.2).
- The repository's default batch is the derived total of 4, one sample from each of four streams. One of those streams (gold captions) is not part of M5.
- E6-7.7 (C6) fixes the batch as one sample from each of M5's three streams, the marked per-stream default of 1; the CC-526 sentence does not apply, because each stream has one value, marked as the default.
- **M5 RUNS**, if it is kept (E-C), with one sample from each of its three streams per optimiser step and with the targets and loss of E6-7.7.
