2. THE THEORY AS A FORMAL PROPOSITION (paper appendix text) AND WHERE EACH STEP BREAKS
==========================================================================================================
Setting
  - Image x; object vocabulary V; K captions per image; caption j names the set S_j; C_o = sum_j 1[o in S_j].
  - A target policy T maps the captions to m training rows. SFT uses all of them, so the MLE target is their uniform
    mixture.
  - Inclusion function: g_T(c) = E[(1/m) sum_rows 1[o in row] | C_o = c].
    - It is exact for separable policies (single caption, multiref, random unions).
    - It is a first-order summary for consensus and greedy fusion, whose inclusion also depends on the other objects.

Assumptions
  (A1) Given x, S_1..S_K are i.i.d. (exchangeable, conditionally independent annotators), with
       pi_o(x) = P(o in S | x). Hence C_o | x ~ Bin(K, pi_o(x)).
  (A2) Population-optimal learner. The model distribution equals the target mixture, so its mention marginal is
       mu_T(o|x) = sum_c g_T(c) Bin(c; K, pi_o(x)).
  (A3) Set-MAP decoding of a product-form mention distribution. The output names o iff mu_T(o|x) > 1/2.

Prop 1 (implicit threshold)
  - Statement: under A1-A3, with g_T non-decreasing and non-constant, the decoded set is {o : pi_o(x) > tau_T}, where
    mu_T(tau_T) = 1/2.
  - Proof sketch: the binomial family is stochastically increasing in pi, so mu_T is strictly increasing.
  - Values:
    - single caption and multiref (g = c/K): tau = 1/2;
    - union of k i.i.d. captions: mu = 1 - (1 - pi)^k by A1, so tau = 1 - 2^(-1/k);
    - union of all K: tau = 1 - 2^(-1/K) = 0.129.
  - Actual ladder rows (operating_points.py, exact build_gold_sft rows, KTVIC train):

    | policy | tau | g(1) | g(2) | g(3) |
    |---|---|---|---|---|
    | p1 | 0.505 | | | |
    | multiref | 0.500 | | | |
    | unf2 | 0.383 | | | |
    | cons | 0.352 | 0.11 | 0.74 | 0.87 |
    | rnd2 | 0.269 | | | |
    | fuse2 | 0.181 | 0.70 | 0.95 | 0.99 |

Prop 2 (F1-optimal set; Lipton et al. 2014 in Dinkelbach form)
  - Setting: the gold is the union of h fresh annotators, q_o = 1 - (1 - pi_o)^h, and F = 2E[TP] / (E|M| + E|G|)
    (micro P and R).
  - Statement: the optimal set is {o : q_o > F*/2}.
  - Proof: max_M 2E[TP] - F*(E|M| + E|G|) = 0 at the optimum, and this decomposes over objects.
  - Corollary: the matching union size is k* = h ln2 / (-ln(1 - F*/2)).
    - Offline gold (h = 2, F* = 0.683): k* = 3.3.
    - Test gold (h = 5, F* = 0.64): k* = 8.9, i.e. more than the 5 captions that exist.
  - Remark (the repo metric): micro P with macro R (the mean of tp_i/|G_i|) makes the break-even probability
    image-dependent:
    - q*_i = P / (1 + (P/R)^2 (M/N) / |G_i|); verified numerically to 4 decimals;
    - it reduces to F/2 when |G_i| is constant;
    - on KTVIC offline (P 0.63, R 0.75, about 3.2 objects per target) it ranges from about 0.19 (|G| = 1) to 0.40
      (|G| = 4).
  - Not covered here: instance-wise F (Dembczynski et al. 2011, GFM).

Prop 3 (length matching; needs only A1-A2)
  - Statement: a decoder that returns a sample from the model names E|M| = sum_o mu_T(o|x) = E[|row| | x] objects, and
    E[TP_h] = sum_o mu_T q_o.
  - So an ideal sampler sits at the target's own offline operating point (human_parity.py offline).
  - No threshold appears. This is the known null (Eikema & Aziz 2020: samples reproduce data statistics, MAP does not).

Corollary 4 (frontier invariance for an ideal learner)
  - Under A1-A3, monotone policies differ only in tau_T, so they trace one curve.
  - This is Menon et al. 2015 (the optimal classifier thresholds the corrupted class-probability at a shifted threshold;
    AUC is immune to corruption), with the corruption map pi -> mu_T(pi).

Where it breaks, with numbers
  A1 breaks. See 1d: positions are not exchangeable.
    - The 1 - 2^(-1/k) values overstate the effective k for unions.
    - The empirical g(c) avoids the binomial step for g, but mu_T(pi) still assumes Bin(5, pi).
  A3 breaks for the trained captioner.

    | system | ideal-threshold count | length model (same construction on the same test images) | observed |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | P1 | 1.24 (plug-in c >= 3: 1.42) | 1.715-1.743 | 1.783 |
    | fuse2-short (consensus rows) | 1.92-1.97 | 2.188 | 2.091 |
    | fuse2-detailed | 2.91-3.26 | 3.285 | 3.154 |

    - Only the tau ~ 0.5 policies discriminate the two models. There the threshold model misses by 44%; the length
      model by 2-4%.
    - Majority amplification happens at the TEMPLATE level instead:
      - fuse2 detailed targets are two-sentence in 72% of rows (75% on the same test images);
      - outputs average 1.914 sentences, about 91% two-sentence;
      - so thresholding at 1/2 describes the template choice, not the objects.
  Multi-row targets.
    - unf2: objects named in only one of its two rows sit exactly at mu = 1/2, a knife edge where Prop 1 is undefined.
    - multiref: the per-image marginal is exactly c/5. Under A3 it should emit the majority set: 1.19 objects per train
      image, 1.28-1.42 per test image, against 1.63-1.74 for a typical caption. This is the sharpest queued
      discriminator (D1).
  Length and sentence constraints. One-sentence prompts give about 12 words and 1.8-2.1 objects whatever tau is. Count
    follows the targets' object density per sentence.
  F1 decomposition is approximate under macro recall (see the Remark) and flat (1b).
  "Consensus selection and greedy fusion are special cases" holds only with added constraints:
    - one caption, or exactly two with consensus forced;
    - without them, the Bayes rule with uniform q > lambda keeps the union of all captions, not fuse2.

5. NOVELTY
==========================================================================================================
I read the relevant sections of 8 papers in full text (PDFs in lit/). Searches: 4 WebSearch and 9 OpenAlex queries.
The arXiv API returned 429; OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar hit their budgets later. No direct scoop was found of
"caption-level target policy -> inclusion function -> implicit threshold". Every component is textbook.

Closest prior work and the delta that remains
- Chen et al. 2015, COCO Captions (arXiv 1504.00325), Sec. 4.2. Model: p = P(w|o) with no image saliency, independent
  subjects, human P = pk/(pk + (1-p)^k), human R = p, and the Precision-at-Human-Recall (PHR) metric. Delta:
  image-level pi and the mapping from training targets to thresholds.
- Fang et al. 2015 (arXiv 1411.4952). MIL word detectors trained on words in ANY caption (union labels). Outputs are
  calibrated to precision on held-out data and thresholded at a global tau; evaluated with PHR. Delta: caption-level SFT
  targets instead of detectors, and a threshold implied by the target rather than chosen at inference.
- Elkan & Noto 2008. A PU classifier equals c x the true posterior, so the threshold is 0.5c. Menon et al. 2015 (ICML):
  the corrupted class-probability is a monotone transform of the clean one; F-score-optimal classifiers threshold it at
  a shifted threshold; AUC is immune. Prop 1 and Corollary 4 are this result with the corruption map pi -> mu_T(pi).
- Lipton et al. 2014; Narasimhan et al. 2014; Koyejo et al. 2014 (F*/2 and plug-in consistency); Dembczynski et al.
  2011 (GFM, instance-wise F). Delta: only the image-dependent threshold under the repo's hybrid micro-P/macro-R metric
  (Remark), which is minor.
- Wei et al., KDD 2023 (arXiv 2206.07181), "To Aggregate or Not?": theory of aggregated vs separate labels; separate
  labels win when noise is high or annotators few. Also Sheng, Provost & Ipeirotis, KDD 2008. These are the theory
  prior for multiref vs consensus.
- Zheng et al., EMNLP 2018; Nagasawa et al., MAI 2021 (VBCS, WCS); consensus-oriented captioning, IJCAI 2020;
  Phan et al. 2017 (CST). Multi-reference training policies; none relate a target to a threshold or a frontier.
- IC3 (arXiv 2302.01328): beam outputs keep only what references share. A qualitative version of "multiref decodes
  consensus content".
- Eikema & Aziz, COLING 2020; Stahlberg et al., ACL 2022: the mode vs sampling; samples match the data's length
  statistics. Prop 3 and model L are this known null.
- "The Devil is in the EOS" (arXiv 2507.20077, COLM 2025) and "Less is More" (arXiv 2402.14545): caption length vs
  hallucination. The precision-count frontier is their trade-off. The new part is a pre-registered invariance test
  across SFT target policies.

Assessment
- The theory as stated: low novelty (a re-application of Menon/Elkan-Noto plus Lipton plus Chen).
- The empirical frontier-invariance law with a pre-registered ladder test: moderate-low, but cheap and honest.
