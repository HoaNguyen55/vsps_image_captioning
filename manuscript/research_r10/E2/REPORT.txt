R10 / E2 -- Noise-aware object F1 from annotator mention propensity (capture-recapture over the 5 KTVIC refs)
Agent E2, 2026-10-14. CPU only. Files: research_r10/E2/{e2_pilot.py, e2_results.json, e2_ablation.py, e2_ablation.txt}

0. BOTTOM LINE
- Idea: treat the 5 KTVIC references as 5 capture occasions. Fit a closed-population M_h finite-mixture model
  (Pledger 2000) to get (a) mention-worthy objects per image and (b) per-class naming propensity. Treat each
  system as a 6th occasion and extrapolate its hit rate to the objects no reference named. This yields
  P* = (TP + U)/|M|, R* = (TP + U)/N_hat and F1*.
- The model itself works. Fitted on 3 refs, it predicts precision against all 5 refs with MAE 2.3 pts (naive:
  6.7). From 4 refs the MAE is 1.1 (naive: 2.6). It predicts the growth of the reference object set to within
  0.6-2.4%.
- As a replacement metric it FAILS the independent-panel check:
  * P* ranks systems worse than raw P against the panel-implied precision (Spearman 0.65 vs 0.91).
  * F1* beats raw F1 against panel F1 (0.97 vs 0.77), but recall alone does better (0.997). The gain is
    precision compression, not modelling.
- Why: one panel can only see "sampling zeros" (objects KTVIC-style annotators name sometimes). It is blind to
  "guideline zeros" (real objects KTVIC annotators essentially never name). Link (2003) shows this tail is not
  identifiable.
- What survives is an evaluation-methodology analysis. Object "false positives" split three ways:
  sampling-unnamed real / guideline-unnamed real / true hallucination.
  * Fine-tuned systems: about 7-18 / 11-13 / 1-4 points of mentions.
  * Zero-shot detailed: 12 / 25 / 31.
- Value: moderate. It is a clean, general, CPU-only analysis for the PD-2 evaluation pillar. It is NOT a new
  headline metric and does NOT rescue any method from count control.

1. PROBLEM AND HYPOTHESIS
Problem: object P against 5 refs counts every unnamed real object as a hallucination. second_panel.json
already shows the scale of this: a held-out human scores P 85.4 vs 4 refs, and the OpenViIC panel confirms
52-62% of fine-tuned systems' ref-false mentions.
H1 (internal): a propensity model fitted on R'<5 refs predicts each system's precision against all 5 refs
  better than assuming no change. Falsified if model MAE >= naive MAE. Result: NOT falsified.
H2 (external): P*/F1* from KTVIC refs alone agree with the independent OpenViIC panel better than raw P/F1.
  Falsified if the rank correlation with the panel targets is not higher. Result: FALSIFIED for P*. Supported
  for F1* only through precision compression (see 3.4).
H3 (rankings): F1* changes system rankings. Result: yes (Spearman 0.94 over 73 systems), but mainly by
  promoting long, high-recall captions (zero-shot detailed), which the panel says hallucinate most.

2. CLOSEST PRIOR WORK (verified by web search unless marked)
[1] Pledger 2000, Biometrics 56(2):434-442. Finite-mixture ML for closed capture-recapture (M_h, M_th).
    Our model is her M_h with refs as occasions. New: refs as occasions, the system as an extra occasion
    with class-specific rates, and use as a metric correction. Novelty vs [1]: application only.
[2] Link 2003, Biometrics 59(4):1123-1130, doi 10.1111/j.0006-341X.2003.00129.x. N is not identifiable under
    heterogeneous detection. Holzmann, Munk & Zucchini 2006 (Biometrics) give conditions for finite and beta
    mixtures. Our "guideline zeros" are exactly Link's unidentifiable tail. The OpenViIC panel makes them
    measurable here, which is the useful twist.
[3] Hui & Walter 1980, Biometrics 36(1):167-171. Latent-class accuracy of a test with no gold standard. Same
    spirit: evaluate a system against imperfect references with a latent model. New: positive-only "tests"
    (refs never say absent), many exchangeable refs, open object vocabulary.
[4] Dawid & Skene 1979, JRSS-C 28(1):20-28 (doi 10.2307/2346806, NOT verified here). Annotator confusion
    matrices by EM. Our refs have only propensities (no false positives), so DS reduces to [1]. Novelty vs
    DS: low.
[5] Jain, White & Radivojac, AAAI 2017, arXiv:1702.00518, doi 10.1609/aaai.v31i1.10937. They correct
    precision/recall measured on positive-unlabeled data, but need the class prior. New: we identify the
    labelling propensity from 5 independent positive-only labellers, not from a prior. Novelty vs [5]:
    moderate.
[6] Spain & Perona 2011, IJCV 91 (pages 59-76 from memory). "Measuring and Predicting Object Importance":
    importance = probability an observer names the object, fitted with an urn model. Berg et al.,
    CVPR 2012, doi 10.1109/CVPR.2012.6248100, predict what people will describe. Both estimate naming
    propensity in order to predict it. Neither corrects caption metrics for unnamed-but-present objects.
[7] Rohrbach et al., EMNLP 2018, arXiv:1809.02156 (CHAIR). Petryk et al., NAACL 2024 short 30 (ALOHa).
    Both treat a mention outside the references or labels as a hallucination. Neither models reference
    incompleteness.
[8] Keenan, Smeaton & Keogh 2001, JASIST 52(7):570-574 (found on IDEAS; title to confirm). Extrapolates the
    relevant documents a system would get with an infinite pool. This is the IR analogue of our "precision
    at infinite refs".
[9] In-repo prior: scripts/second_panel.py already runs a TWO-panel Lincoln-Petersen/Chapman (N = 8.35
    classes/img). E2 adds the WITHIN-panel, 5-occasion, heterogeneity-aware model, the per-system
    extrapolation, the hold-out validation, and the 3-way decomposition.
Novelty search: 3 queries ("capture-recapture image captioning references", "Dawid-Skene caption evaluation
incomplete references", "capture-recapture hallucination evaluation") found no paper that applies
capture-recapture or Dawid-Skene to caption references or caption metrics. Overall novelty: MODERATE.
The machinery is classical; the application and the sampling-vs-guideline decomposition were not found.

6. RISKS AND PD-2 FIT
Risks:
- Link non-identifiability. N_hat ranges 4.37-5.50/img across estimators. The sampling share in 3.6 moves
  with it.
- Lexical splitting inflates singletons and so N_hat. Test 3a may shrink the sampling share.
- The model has positive bias in V1 (+1.35 at R'=3), so it overstates the sampling share a little.
- The guideline-real term rests on one panel-sensitivity assumption (claim 9).
- Reviewers may see the method as classical. The contribution is the decomposition and the validated
  extrapolation, not the estimator.
- AI-label rule: no model-generated labels are used. Systems are only scored. The rejected "other systems
  as annotators" variant would break the rule and is excluded.
PD-2 fit: this strengthens the EVALUATION/METHODOLOGY pillar.
- It gives one paragraph plus one table: "what object false positives are made of".
- It ties the human 1v4 ceiling, the two-panel capture and the reference-style facts into one model.
- It does NOT help the main open problem (no method beats the count frontier).
