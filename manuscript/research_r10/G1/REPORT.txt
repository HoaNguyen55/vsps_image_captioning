R10 / G1 -- Does the count frontier hold for attributes and relations?
Agent G1, 2026-10-14. Folder: research_r10/G1/  (g1_tuples.py = extractor + fits; g1_counts.npz = per-image counts for
73 systems x 558 images x 7 tuple types; fit_ladder.log, fit_clean.log, fit.log = all outputs quoted below; extract.log)

0. BOTTOM LINE
- Within one caption style, the law holds. On the 36 KTVIC-trained "ladder" outputs (P1, P1-assist, p1mask, fuse2, e5,
  e5dpo, mr5assist, tags, MBR and self-fuse variants), every SPICE-like tuple type follows TP = a*M^b. R2 is 0.87-0.99 and
  the exponents match between short and detailed modes. Objects b = 0.70-0.75, relations 0.61-0.73, counts 0.61-0.67,
  attributes 0.84-0.88 (wide CI). Relation and count slopes are homogeneous across families (Cochran Q p = 0.35 / 0.72).
- Across caption styles, frontier invariance fails. Zero-shot, VCD and the older VSPS-style systems sit below the ladder
  frontier by 19% for objects and 37-45% for attributes and relations, so the deficit grows with tuple arity. It sits in
  conditional precision: when both endpoints are correct, the predicate matches 4-13% of the time vs 35-41% on the ladder,
  and about half of these systems' predicates never occur in any reference. This points to reference-style dependence.
- Not visible in the object metric: MBR and self-fusion outputs are pure count shifts on objects (residual -0.4% / -1.3%),
  yet they fall below the RELATION frontier (p1_s43mbr -17.7% [-32.0, -3.4]; fuse2 selffuse -22.0% [-32.2, -11.3]).
  Count-matched object scoring therefore misses compositional damage.
- Value: an evaluation result for the PD-2 evaluation pillar. It gives one frontier per tuple type and states where
  frontier invariance applies. It is NOT a method that beats the frontier. Novelty: moderate. Cost: CPU only (2 min).

1. PROBLEM AND HYPOTHESES
The pre-registered law (manuscript/prereg/2026-10-12_ladder_predictions.json) is TP/img = 1.3996*(M/1.7826)^0.7468 for
object classes. Question: is "TP grows as a power of mentions, with a system-invariant exponent" a law of caption content in
general? Tuples (same extractor for references and predictions; gold = union over the 5 references):
  attr (object, colour|adjective) | colour (subset) | count (object, 2..10|many) | rel_s (subj, verbs|spatial, obj) |
  rel_l (subj, spatial-or-verb, obj) | rel_u (subj, obj): an unlabeled related pair.
H1 (generality within style): for each type k, every new ladder run lies on that type's frontier, fitted on THIS pilot's
    anchors (P1 3-seed mean + fuse2_s42-detailed), within its 95% CI. Falsified if >= 2 of the new runs miss by > 10%
    relative with the CI excluding 0, or if the joint mean residual's CI excludes 0.
H2 (type-specific exponents): b_rel < b_obj < b_attr. Pooled within-family slopes are rel_s 0.680, obj 0.757 and
    attr 0.878. Falsified if b_rel >= b_obj with a paired bootstrap CI that excludes 0.
H3 (style moves the level, not the exponent; weak-form law TP_k = a_{style,k} * M^{b_k}): within-system dial exponents of
    zero-shot, P1 and fuse2 agree within +-0.10 for relations and attributes, while the zero-shot intercept is >= 30%
    lower. Falsified if |delta b| > 0.15 with a CI that excludes 0.
H4 (style, not hallucination): scored against style-free human relation gold (Visual Genome, COCO transfer images), the
    zero-shot relation precision deficit at matched relation count shrinks by >= 50%. Falsified if it shrinks < 20%.
H5 (compositional damage): UPE-MBR selected captions have a relation frontier residual < 0 (CI excludes 0) while their
    object residual CI contains 0, and the same-count COUNT control does not show that relation deficit.

2. CLOSEST PRIOR WORK (IDs checked by web search on 2026-10-14; details are from abstracts only)
 [1] SPICE, Anderson et al., arXiv 1607.08822, ECCV 2016, DOI 10.1007/978-3-319-46454-1_24. Scene-graph tuple F-score with
     object/attribute/relation/colour/count breakdowns. We reuse its tuple decomposition. New here: cross-system count
     frontiers per tuple type, and their invariance tests. Novelty vs [1]: moderate.
 [2] CAPTURE / DetailCaps, Dong et al., arXiv 2405.19092. Detailed-caption metric that matches objects/attributes/relations
     from a Factual scene-graph parser. Measures the tuples but has no count frontier or matched-count comparison. Moderate.
 [3] CompreCap, Lu et al., arXiv 2412.08614, CVPR 2025. Human directed scene graph; object coverage, attribute accuracy,
     relation scores. Its gold is instance-level, which could serve as a style-free panel for H4. Novelty vs [3]: moderate.
 [4] DeCapBench / DCScore, Ye et al., arXiv 2503.07906, ICLR 2025. Primitive information units, scored for hallucination
     and comprehensiveness, i.e. precision and coverage. No law relating them across systems. Moderate.
 [5] AMBER, Wang et al., arXiv 2311.07397. Existence/attribute/relation hallucination, generative and discriminative.
     Reports rates per system; no frontier and no count control. High (our angle differs).
 [6] R-Bench, Wu et al., arXiv 2406.16449, ICML 2024 (PMLR 235). Relation hallucination via yes/no questions;
     discriminative only. High.
 [7] HallusionBench, Guan et al., arXiv 2310.14566, CVPR 2024. Language hallucination vs visual illusion, with control
     pairs. Not tuple counts. High.
 [8] Feng et al., arXiv 2406.12663, "Do More Details Always Introduce More Hallucinations in LVLM-based Image Captioning?"
     Argues detail-vs-hallucination conclusions rest on flawed metrics; proposes CLIP-P/R/F1. This is the closest concept.
     No functional law, no tuple types, no style analysis. Moderate.
 [9] Lee et al., ICML 2025, PMLR 267:33815-33832, arXiv 2412.15484, "Toward Robust Hyper-Detailed Image Captioning".
     Reliance on self-generated text grows with caption length; factuality and coverage metrics. No cross-system law.
     Moderate.
[10] Yue et al., arXiv 2402.14545, ACL 2024, "Less is More" (EOS decision). Overly detailed training data delays stopping
     and causes hallucination. This is mechanism work, not a frontier. High.
A shallow search found no verified work that fits a cross-system power-law frontier per tuple type, or that shows the
style penalty compounding with tuple arity (see VERIFY 4). This is NOT a "first" claim.
Overall novelty: moderate. The detail-vs-accuracy trade-off is well known. The quantitative per-type frontier, its scope
(within style), and the finding that count-matched object metrics miss relational damage are the new parts.

3. EVIDENCE SO FAR (CPU, existing files only; no model-generated labels used)
Extractor (g1_tuples.py, ~130 lines). It uses only repo lexicons plus pyvi POS tags (installed, about 1 ms per sentence):
- Objects are matched exactly as rescap.chair.objects_in does: same 113-entry lexicon, longest match, hypernym collapse,
  colour-phrase masking. On 40,734 captions, the span-derived classes equal objects_in output 99.94% of the time.
- Attribute: a colour (lexicon COLORS plus bare "xanh"; SYNONYMS canonical form) or an adjective (pyvi tag A) to the
  right of the head noun. "màu", "có màu" and degree adverbs are skipped.
- Count: the quantifier left of the head noun (numerals 2-10, or nhiều/vài/một số -> "many").
- Relation: formed between consecutive objects in a sentence, using the verbs and the last spatial phrase between them
  (SPATIAL_RELATIONS plus variants). Two rules fix attachment: a verb after a worn or held object attaches to the
  wearer, and the fronted locative "trên X có Y" becomes (Y, trên, X).
- Self-check asserts pass (`demo`).
- Whole run: 1 min 47 s, 164 MB RSS, nice 19, one thread.
- Pipeline check: the object anchor exponent comes out b = 0.747 [0.696, 0.802]; the pre-registration has 0.7468.
Gold sparsity (mean per image, union of 5 refs; % of images with any): objects 3.72 (99%), attributes 0.46 (36%),
  colour 0.39 (32%), count 0.60 (49%), rel_s 3.01 (84%). References rarely state attributes, so attribute tests have low power.

3e. Limits.
- The rule extractor is not validated against gold tuples, since no Vietnamese scene-graph gold exists and no annotation
  is allowed. Shared-parser bias cancels between refs and preds only when the styles match.
- Attribute counts on the ladder are tiny (P1 M_attr 0.05-0.08 per image, about 30-45 tuples per system).
- Relations need both endpoints in the 113-class lexicon.

6. RISKS AND FIT WITH PD-2
Risks:
- Extractor validity. Rules plus pyvi, with no gold tuples. A reviewer may call the tuples parser artefacts.
  Mitigations: the same parser runs on refs and preds; A4 swaps the tagger; B uses gold independent of reference style.
- Low power on attributes: about 0.46 gold attributes per image.
- The ladder has only about 8 distinct operating points.
- The cross-style failure may be read as "the law is just a within-style regularity". That reading is the honest scope
  and is worth stating.
- Curvature: the power law may be a local approximation (C tests this).
Fit: this strengthens the EVALUATION and METHODOLOGY pillar.
- The count-matched primary endpoint carries over to attribute and relation claims, using the same machinery.
- Frontier invariance gets an explicit scope (within the KTVIC-trained style) instead of an overclaim.
- Reference-style dependence compounds with tuple arity. This explains the poor KTVIC numbers of zero-shot and legacy
  systems, and ties to the COCO-80 finding.
- The H5 check gives every future selection method a second kill rule at the relation level.
It does not deliver a method that beats the frontier.
