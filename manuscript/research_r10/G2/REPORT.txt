Artifacts (this folder): g2_frontier.py (CPU, 2m40s, 365 MB peak), g2_frontier.json (all numbers), g2_frontier.txt.

0. BOTTOM LINE
- The power-law form TP = a*M^b holds in every setting we have: KTVIC, UIT-ViIC and COCO-2500; reference, instance
  and mixed gold; trained systems, sampling, vote sets and count-only selection. RMSE is 0.6-2 P points per family.
- The exponent is NOT universal. On the same outputs, b goes from 0.40 to 0.99. It is set by the gold, not the
  captioner: on KTVIC b rises monotonically with the number of references in the gold (k=1..5: 0.40 -> 0.70;
  Delta = +0.30 [0.25, 0.35]), and instance labels give the highest b (COCO: 0.85 vs 0.64 for 5 refs).
- Reading: b = marginal precision / mean precision. With incomplete references, most of the precision decay along
  the frontier is unreferenced true objects, not hallucination.
- Invariance is gold- and family-specific. It holds for the KTVIC-fine-tuned ladder on KTVIC refs (32/36 inside
  CI) and on UIT-ViIC refs (6/6). It fails for off-style systems on KTVIC refs (zero-shot -19 to -25 P). It fails
  on instance gold, where it exposes P1's out-of-domain hallucination (-5 P) that UIT-ViIC references hide.
- Decoding: T does not change b (T0.8 - T0.5 = +0.03 [-0.01, +0.07]), but T=0.8 samples sit 3-5 P BELOW the
  frontier. UPE moves along it. Vote sets are S-shaped: mid thresholds sit up to +2.8 P above.
- Value: turns "count-matched evaluation" from a KTVIC observation into a general, gold-indexed measurement law
  for the PD-2 methodology pillar. It also blocks the reviewer trap of reusing b = 0.747 anywhere else.

1. PROBLEM AND HYPOTHESES (falsifiable)
Problem: PD-2's primary endpoint uses a pre-registered frontier TP = 1.3996*(M/1.7826)^0.7468. That frontier was
fit on KTVIC test against the union of the 5 references, from 2 anchors (P1 x3 seeds, fuse2_s42-detailed). Is the
exponent a property of captioners (universal) or of the gold? Does invariance hold off KTVIC?
Identity used throughout: dTP/dM = b*TP/M, so marginal precision p_marg = b*P. Adding a mention raises F1 iff
p_marg > F1/2. In every setting below b*P >> F1/2 (e.g. KTVIC P1: 0.747*78.5 = 58.6 vs 27.3), so F1 rises
monotonically with count over the whole observed range. That is why unmatched F1 rewards verbosity.
H1 (completeness law): on a fixed image set and fixed outputs, b increases monotonically with gold completeness:
   k references (k=1..5) < 5 refs < instance labels. Kill: b(5)-b(1) CI includes 0 on >= 2 of 3 datasets.
H2 (language-free exponent): on the same images and gold, Vietnamese and English caption families have
   |Delta b| <= 0.05. Kill: the paired CI excludes [-0.05, +0.05].
H3 (decoding moves the intercept, not the exponent): the temperature change leaves |Delta b| <= 0.05, and the
   T=0.8 intercept is lower. Kill: |Delta b| CI excludes [-0.05, +0.05].
H4 (family invariance needs a style-matched gold): within a gold, systems sharing the references' style have
   |residual| <= 2 P; systems with another style or domain do not. Kill: zero-shot within 2 P on KTVIC refs, or
   P1 within 2 P of the UIT-ViIC instance frontier.

2. CLOSEST PRIOR WORK (IDs checked by web search on 2026-10-14 unless marked VERIFY)
[1] Rohrbach et al., "Object Hallucination in Image Captioning", EMNLP 2018, arXiv:1809.02156 / ACL D18-1437.
    CHAIR uses instance labels plus references. It has no count-matched comparison and no trade-off law.
    New here: a count frontier per gold, and the finding that the gold's completeness sets its exponent.
[2] Buckland & Gey, "The relationship between recall and precision", JASIS 45(1):12-19, 1994 (RePEc).
    The classic result that precision falls as the retrieved set grows. It is the closest conceptual ancestor:
    count = number retrieved. New here: the power-law form, b = p_marg/P, and the exponent's dependence on
    reference completeness. Novelty vs [2]: moderate.
[3] Kasai et al., "Transparent Human Evaluation for Image Captioning" (THumB), NAACL 2022, arXiv:2111.08940.
    Rubric with precision/recall dimensions; humans beat machines mainly on recall. No fitted trade-off curve.
[4] Zhai et al., HallE-Control / CCEval, arXiv:2310.01779. Controls caption length and object count when
    comparing hallucination. It matches length and count, but fits no frontier law and does not vary the gold.
[5] Lee et al., CapMAS "Toward Robust Hyper-Detailed Image Captioning", arXiv:2412.15484. Listed at PMLR
    v267 (lee25aj), i.e. ICML 2025 (VERIFY venue). Reports factuality and coverage separately; finds that
    hallucination grows with length.
[6] Dong et al., CAPTURE / DetailCaps, arXiv:2405.19092. Object precision/recall against parsed references.
    SC-Captioner (arXiv:2508.06125) criticises its precision as incomplete: real but unreferenced objects are
    penalised. Our k-reference curve quantifies exactly this effect, as an exponent.
[7] VALOR-EVAL arXiv:2404.13874 (faithfulness vs coverage) and THRONE arXiv:2405.05256 (CHAIR_i favours short
    captions). Both state the trade-off; neither has a law, an invariance test or a gold-completeness index.
    VERIFY venues.
Novelty: MODERATE overall. The trade-off and the length confound are well known. The new parts:
  (a) a fitted power law with b = p_marg/P;
  (b) b indexed by gold completeness (k-reference curve, instance limit) on fixed outputs;
  (c) a cross-system invariance test per gold, including where it fails.
  The search found no prior caption-metric paper with (b) or (c). Search depth was limited (6 queries), so this
  is not a "first" claim.

    - T=0.5 single samples: mean residual -0.8 / -1.4 / -1.2 P (3 seeds), individual CIs mostly cover 0.

    - Count-only choice of the min-M / max-M caption from the pool: -2.9 to +0.5 P, i.e. on or under the frontier.

        mid thresholds (>=3, >=4 of 7) reach +2.3 to +2.8 P (CI > 0 for p1assist_s42 and fuse2), but at most
        +1.1 / +1.9 for s43 / s44;
        unanimity (>=7 of 7) falls -5 to -10 P below.
      Consequence: the COUNT control (top-k by vote) is a stricter bar than the training frontier. A selection
      method that ties COUNT is already about 1-2 P above the frontier.

6. RISKS AND FIT WITH PD-2
Risks:
 - Few systems outside KTVIC (6-9) and narrow M ranges. The two-anchor slope is unstable there: COCO anchors
   span M 1.09-1.40, giving b_anc 0.98 vs b_ols 0.85. Mitigation: always report OLS over the family plus
   bootstrap and LOO.
 - A power law is only a local approximation: vote families are S-shaped.
 - The only English points are human captions.
 - EN and VI use different matchers (en_classes vs vi_objects), so cross-lingual intercepts are not comparable;
   only exponents are.
 - The k-subset curve uses file order (fixed in G2-C).
 - UIT-ViIC test has 231 images: wide CIs.
Fit with PD-2:
 - Strengthens the evaluation/methodology pillar directly. The paper can state the frontier as a gold-indexed law,
   with b measuring reference completeness, invariant within style-matched families and diagnostic where it
   breaks.
 - Count-matched comparison must use a frontier refit on the same gold and image set. Never transport b = 0.747.
 - Explains why the COUNT control is the harder endpoint for selection methods (3d).
 - Gives a principled reason the KTVIC frontier "held on every landed run": all landed runs were style-matched.
 - It does not by itself supply a method that beats the frontier.

7. SCORES (1-5)
 Novelty 3: the trade-off is classical (IR, CHAIR, CCEval). The gold-completeness-indexed exponent and per-gold
   invariance tests are new to my search, but incremental.
 Feasibility 5: the core result is already computed on CPU from existing files. Extensions need about 9 GPU-h
   and no new labels.
 Expected impact 3: makes the methodology pillar general and reviewer-proof. It is not a method gain.
 Risk 2: the main findings have tight paired CIs on existing data. The open risk is the English/backbone
   replication (H2/H4).
