====================================================================================================================
0. BOTTOM LINE
====================================================================================================================
- Idea: DPO in which chosen and rejected are the policy's own samples for one image, naming the SAME number k of object
  classes (and within 2 syllables of each other). The winner has more classes in the human gold (KTVIC: union of the
  5 train references; UIT-ViIC: COCO instance labels). With within-(image, k) pairs, the DPO loss is exactly invariant
  to any count-dependent reward c(x, k) (sec. 1), so the model gets no gradient toward saying more or less. It can only
  learn which objects to name at a fixed count. That is the residual the PD-2 count frontier measures.
- Why new (moderate): length control in DPO is loss-side (R-DPO, SimPO, SamPO). The nearest data-side priors are
  substitution and minimal pairs (HALVA, HII-DPO), and they do not control or test count. Precision DPO is known to
  cut coverage (CHAIR-DPO, OPA-DPO). No prior work found matches count by construction or tests against a count frontier.
- Evidence (dev300 pools, held out): 3.68 usable pairs/img on 60% of images. dk = 0 and dsyl -0.03 by construction.
  Only 12% of pairs are hypernym-linked naming variants; up to 88% swap the object. Human labels disagree with self-consistency
  on 30% of pairs. At fixed count: in-sample oracle +6.64 F1 [+4.72, +8.66]; split-annotator +4.64 [+2.69, +6.72];
  vote share only +0.24. The naive alternative (F1-preferred, count-unmatched) picks the caption naming MORE objects
  in 76% of pairs (mean dk +0.69).
- Expected value: realistic dev residual +0.5 to +2.0 F1. P(confirmatory T1 pass) about 25-30%.
  First look: 4.2 GPU-h. All stages: about 33 GPU-h.
  A null result is still a clean, pre-registered negative that closes the training-method question for PD-2.

1. PROBLEM AND HYPOTHESIS
====================================================================================================================
Every landed method moves along the frontier TP = 1.3996 (M/1.7826)^0.7468. Examples: UPE (kill 2), PLAN-F1, fuse2,
and in-house E5-DPO (N2: residual -1.97 [-4.33, +0.18]; vs P1 -1.33 [-3.42, +0.76]; CIDEr -26.7; CJK 4.1%).
Preference data built from object F1 confounds quality with count. Remove the count channel from the DATA.
Invariance (exact, 3 lines): r(x,y) = beta log pi/pi_ref. Replace pi by pi' ∝ pi * exp(c(x,k(y))/beta).
  Then r' = r + c(x,k) - beta log Z'(x). For a pair with k(y_w) = k(y_l), r'_w - r'_l = r_w - r_l, so L_DPO(pi') =
  L_DPO(pi): count-matched pairs carry zero information about the count marginal. (Parametric leakage is still possible;
  guard G1.)
  KTO has no such invariance (absolute per-sample rewards against a batch KL reference point). So DPO is primary;
  KTO is not proposed.
Hypotheses and falsifiable predictions (dev300 first; KTVIC test for the confirmatory look):
  H1 (mechanism): CM-DPO keeps the count: |dm| <= 0.10 vs its start. The unmatched control C1 raises m by >= +0.15 and
     lands within +-1.0 of the frontier (like E5-DPO, UPE and fuse2).
  H2 (efficacy): CM-DPO frontier residual F1 rises: dev d-residual in [+0.5, +2.5], point about +1.0 to +1.5.
  H3 (labels matter): the label-free control C2 (same count/length rule, winner = higher vote share) gives
     d-residual within +-1.0. Reason: vote-share choice at fixed count is +0.24 [-1.11, +1.52] at decode time.
  H4 (truth side): the OpenViIC panel residual moves the same way (>= +0.3). In the COCO arm, instance precision
     rises at |dm| <= 0.10.
  Falsified if: the H2 interval misses, or CM-DPO is not above C2, or m drifts (then it is a count method in disguise).

2. CLOSEST PRIOR WORK (verified on arXiv abs pages / ACL Anthology this session unless marked)
====================================================================================================================
- CHAIR-DPO, 2508.20181 (Compagnoni et al., BMVC 2025). Winner and loser decided by CHAIR (detector gold), no count
  matching. R9 C4/C6 found AMBER Cover 51.7 -> 46.6: a precision win paid for in coverage.
  New here: count held fixed by construction; human gold; frontier test. Novelty vs it: high.
- HA-DPO, 2311.16839 (Zhao et al.; no venue on the abs page). GPT-rewritten non-hallucinated vs hallucinated pairs, with
  a "style-consistent" dataset; count not matched. Novelty vs it: high.
- RLHF-V, 2312.00849 (Yu et al., CVPR 2024). Segment-level human corrections with dense DPO. A correction usually
  deletes or replaces the hallucinated object, so count is not controlled. Human labels are new annotation; we use
  existing labels only. Novelty vs it: moderate.
- POVID, 2402.11411 (Zhou et al.; arXiv, no venue listed). GPT-4V injects hallucinations into the gold answer, plus
  image distortion. Rejected answers typically add content. Novelty vs it: high.
- mDPO, 2406.11839 (Wang et al., EMNLP 2024). Image-conditional preference plus a reward anchor that keeps the chosen
  likelihood up. Orthogonal: its anchor idea is reused here as an SFT anchor. Novelty vs it: high.
- HALVA / DPA, 2405.18654 (Sarkar et al., ICLR 2025). The CLOSEST prior. Negatives replace ground-truth concepts with
  hallucinated ones, "keeping the overall structure intact" (count-preserving for object swaps, but generated, and
  covering attributes and actions too). Phrase-level loss plus a token KL. AMBER Cover 51.0 -> 53.0, CHAIR 7.8 -> 6.6.
  New here: the model's own samples, not generated negatives; human labels; count matching as an explicit rule with a
  vote-matched control and a frontier test. Novelty vs it: moderate.
- Length control: SimPO 2405.14734 (Meng et al., NeurIPS 2024; average log-prob reward); R-DPO 2403.19159 (Park et al.,
  Findings of ACL 2024, DOI 10.18653/v1/2024.findings-acl.297; length regulariser); SamPO 2406.10957 (down-sampled KL).
  All are loss-side fixes for a length confound. Here the confound is object count, removed on the data side.
  Novelty vs them: moderate.
- KTO 2402.01306 (Ethayarajh et al., ICML 2024). Unpaired desirable/undesirable labels; no within-stratum invariance
  (sec. 1). Novelty vs it: n/a (design choice).
- RPO 2404.19733 (Pang et al.; arXiv page lists no venue): DPO + NLL on the chosen answer. Not used here: chosen
  captions are count-biased (mean k 1.95 vs 1.77, sec. 3), so NLL on chosen would push the count up.
- Likelihood displacement 2410.08847 (Razin et al., ICLR 2025): displacement is driven by preferences with similar
  embeddings (CHES). Count-matched one-object swaps are exactly that regime. This is the main risk (sec. 6).
- Also seen (search snippets only, UNVERIFIED): HII-DPO 2602.10425 (identical-prefix minimal pairs);
  OPA-DPO 2501.09695 (on-policy DPO, CVPR 2025 per its project page; lower Cover reported); HallE-Control 2310.01779
  (compares models at roughly matched object count/length in evaluation).
Scoop search: 7 web queries (count-matched / coverage-matched / same-number-of-objects pairs; length-balanced pairs;
iso-coverage evaluation). Verdict: NONE FOUND for pairs matched in object count or for a count-frontier test.
Partial: HALVA (substitution), HII-DPO (minimal pairs), HallE-Control (matched-count evaluation). Overall novelty:
MODERATE. Claim "count-matched preference construction with a frontier test", never "first".

====================================================================================================================
6. RISKS AND FIT WITH PD-2
====================================================================================================================
Risks:
- Likelihood displacement. 97.6% of pairs are one-object swaps of near-identical sentences: the high-CHES regime of
  Razin et al. E5-DPO already showed CJK leakage and -14.8 CIDEr vs its start. Mitigations: the SFT anchor, the CJK and
  CIDEr guards; one fallback at lr 2.5e-6 after a guard kill is allowed if pre-registered.
- Power. The realistic effect is the same size as the CI (+-2 dev, +-2.2 test). T1 needs about +1.5 over p1assist.
  A null is the likely outcome (60-70%).
- Typicality, not truth. The gold is the reference union. At least 12% of pairs are naming variants, and C3 found 56.5%
  of P1 "false positives" are real objects. Truth-side checks: the OpenViIC panel residual and COCO instance labels.
- Parametric count leakage despite invariance. Pairs sit mostly at k = 2 (61%); chosen captions have mean k 1.95 vs
  1.77 in the pool. Guard G1, and never NLL on chosen.
- Memorisation: train images were P1's targets (copy guard; G0 exact copies 0.67% train vs 0.50% dev). One
  off-policy round only. COCO arm: P1 is ~90% precise on COCO, so pairs are scarce; VGR, not this, fixes vocabulary.
Fit: the first TRAINING method built for the PD-2 primary endpoint (matched count) by construction. If T1 and T2
pass, the method pillar gets its first win beyond the frontier. In every outcome the methodology pillar gains the
recipe, the invariance lemma, the vote-matched control and a measured confound (76% "say more"). If it fails, it joins
UPE, PLAN-F1 and E5-DPO as a pre-registered negative, supporting the claim that count explains the object metric.

7. SCORES (1-5)
====================================================================================================================
- Novelty 3: the data-side count control, invariance lemma and frontier test are new in combination. Close priors
  exist (HALVA substitution pairs, length-controlled DPO).
- Feasibility 4: the DPO loop, pi_SFT reference, sampler, dev split and evaluation exist; the builder is drafted and
  tested; the first look is 4.2 GPU-h.
- Expected impact 3: if it passes, it is the only method beyond the count frontier. If not, it is a sharp negative,
  plus a reusable recipe and control.
- Risk 4 (high): the expected effect is about the CI width; DPO on minimal pairs is prone to displacement (E5-DPO
  precedent).
