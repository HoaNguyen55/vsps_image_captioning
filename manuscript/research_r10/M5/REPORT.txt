0. BOTTOM LINE
- Idea: train one QLoRA adapter on existing KTVIC references, each with a count suffix k. k = the reference's number
  of unique lexicon classes, bucketed 1/2/3/4+. Sweep k at test time and compare the curve with the frontier.
- What the CPU checks say: a fixed-k sweep cannot be compared fairly with the frontier. A fixed k spreads objects
  evenly over images, and at the same mean count that costs 1-2.5 F1 against adaptive allocation (dev300).
  With P1's own ranking, even the oracle count only reaches the frontier (residual -0.39). So a curve that lies
  "below the frontier" would mostly measure allocation, not selection. The whole curve can rise only if the adapter
  ranks objects better. One mechanism exists, and it is narrow: the k=1 bucket teaches "name the consensus object"
  (71%), while the first object of a multi-object reference is the consensus object only 34-51% of the time.
- Expected value: H0 (on the frontier; a count shift plus an allocation effect) about 75%. k=1-only ranking gain
  about 15%. Frontier-beating curve about 10%. Cost: 2.4 GPU-h on dev300.
- Salvage, useful whether or not the method works: frontier residual = ranking + allocation + base terms. The
  same-count control (top-k_i by vote) is blind to allocation by construction. Applied to PLAN-F1's dev residual
  (-2.49): ranking -0.95, allocation +0.68, base -2.23. The P1 vote ranker, not the plan, sinks it.
- Novelty: method LOW (noun-count and length control exist); decomposition / matched-allocation method MODERATE.

1. PROBLEM AND HYPOTHESIS
Question: does an adapter told "name k objects" pick better objects at every budget k than the trained frontier
TP = 1.3996 (M/1.7826)^0.7468 and than self-consistency selection at the same per-image count?
Definitions (pre-registered):
- Count M_i = |set(objects_in(caption))|, the same as object_prf.py. Gold = union of the 5 references.
- Frontier = official anchors (manuscript/prereg/2026-10-12_ladder_predictions.json; at() in prereg_ladder_check.py).
- Ranker R = raw vote share over P1's pool (greedy + samples; ties broken by greedy mention order, then name).
For any system S with per-image counts m_i:
  residual_F1(S) = [F1(S) - F1(top-m_i by R)]                 ranking term (the PD-2 same-count control)
                 + [F1(top-m_i by R) - F1(uniform-alloc by R)]  allocation term (same total; counts as even as pool allows)
                 + [F1(uniform-alloc by R) - frontier_F1(m)]    base term (how good R is at that count)
  Check: P1-dev greedy +0.17 = -0.38 + 0.62 - 0.07 (asserted in m5_pilot.py check).
Hypotheses, each falsifiable on dev300 and then on a pre-registered test look:
- P0 (manipulation): realised mean m rises strictly with k. Prediction: m(k=1) ≈ 1.0-1.3, m(k=2) ≈ 1.8-2.2,
  m(k=3) ≈ 2.5-2.9, m(k=4) ≈ 2.9-3.5. k=4 falls short because it has only 568 rows of 4+.
- H0 (frontier invariance holds): the fixed-k sweep lies on or below the frontier at k=2,3, with F1 residual in
  [-3.0, +0.5]. The allocation penalty is the gap. Per-image k_i lands within [-0.5, +1.5] of the frontier.
  Its ranking term vs R is in [-1, +1] at m < 2.3. At m > 2.5 the ranking term is positive only because R's base
  term is weak there; that is not evidence against H0.
- H1k1 (narrow): at k=1 the adapter's precision beats R's top-1 by >= 2 P. Reference: dev R top-1 = 79.3 P
  (13-caption pool) and 80.4 P (7-caption pool).
- H1 (frontier-beating, the claim the direction asks for): for the per-image k_i variant, the ranking term is
  > +1 F1 AND the frontier residual is > +1.5 F1, both with CI > 0 on test (3 seeds).
- Per-image k from uncertainty: k_i = #(pool vote share >= 0.5), clipped to [1,4]. Prediction: +1 to +2 F1 over the
  same sweep at uniform allocation and matched total count, mostly through precision.

2. CLOSEST PRIOR WORK (verified by web search on 2026-10-14 unless marked)
 1. Deng, Ding, Tan, Wu, "Length-Controllable Image Captioning" (LaBERT), ECCV 2020, arXiv 2007.09580,
    DOI 10.1007/978-3-030-58601-0_42. Length-level embedding, non-autoregressive decoder. Controls words, not
    objects; no hallucination-vs-budget frontier. New here: object budget, precision at matched count. Moderate.
 2. Luo & Shakhnarovich, "Controlling Length in Image Captioning", arXiv 2005.14386 (TTIC page: CVPR VQA
    workshop). Length control plus a per-image length predictor; the predictor is the analogue of k_i. New here:
    object count, allocation measured at matched total. Novelty: low-moderate.
 3. Zhu, Wang, Qu, "Macroscopic Control of Text Generation for Image Captioning", arXiv 2101.08000. The control
    signal includes the NUMBER OF NOUNS. CLOSEST: count conditioning is prior art. Abstract reports only
    BLEU/CIDEr/SPICE, no object precision vs count (unverified beyond the abstract). METHOD novelty vs it: low.
 4. Zhai et al., "HallE-Control: Controlling Object Hallucination in Large Multimodal Models", arXiv 2310.01779.
    A continuous switch between grounded and imagined objects in LVLM detailed captions (CCEval: coverage plus
    hallucination). Controls the kind of knowledge, not the count; no matched-count frontier. Moderate.
 5. Yue, Zhang, Jin, "Less is More: Mitigating Multimodal Hallucination from an EOS Decision Perspective",
    ACL 2024, arXiv 2402.14545. The stopping decision (when to emit EOS) drives hallucination. Closest LVLM
    "budget" view, but it is not count-controlled and not compared at matched count. Novelty: moderate.
 6. Zhou et al., LURE, "Analyzing and Mitigating Object Hallucination in LVLMs", ICLR 2024, arXiv 2310.00754.
    Hallucinations concentrate late in captions. Our KTVIC references are not salience-ordered (section 3, C2),
    so a "prefix-k" truncation control is not a salience selector here.
 7. Cornia, Baraldi, Cucchiara, "Show, Control and Tell", CVPR 2019, arXiv 1811.10652. Control by a region set or
    sequence (what to mention). Chen, Jin, Wang, Wu, "Say As You Wish" (ASG), CVPR 2020, arXiv 2003.00387: an
    abstract scene graph sets how many objects, attributes and relations to mention. Both are content and
    structure control without an evaluation at matched count.
 8. Wang et al., "Controllable Image Captioning via Prompting", AAAI 2023, arXiv 2212.01803: learned length
    prompts. Saito et al., "CaptionSmiths", ICCV 2025, arXiv 2507.01409: continuous length and descriptiveness
    control. Kikuchi et al., EMNLP 2016, arXiv 1609.09552: LenEmb and LenInit length control.
 Unverified leads: REVERSE (Wu et al., NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2504.13169); a search snippet says a threshold trades
 coverage against CHAIR, but the abstract does not confirm it. NumCap (ACM, DOI 10.1145/3576927) is a
 number-indexed multi-caption model; details unread.
 Summary: conditioning on a count is NOT new (3, 7). I found no prior work that tests a count-controlled
 captioner against a learner precision-vs-count frontier, or that splits residuals into ranking, allocation and
 base terms. That gap is the only novelty I would claim, and only as "not found", never as "first".

6. RISKS AND FIT WITH PD-2
Risks:
- The count fidelity may be poor. k counts only the 113-entry lexicon, which the model never sees. Hypernym pairs
  (người plus đàn ông, not adjacent) count twice. The cây/cầu matcher bug adds noise.
- k=4 is out of distribution: 568 rows of busy scenes. Forcing 4 objects on simple images invites invention.
  The dev ceiling is 75.5 P at k=4.
- dev300 power: a single residual has CI ≈ ±3.4 F1. Only the paired terms are powered at n=300.
- R is weak above m ≈ 2.3, so "beats the same-count control" at k=3,4 is expected even under H0. The
  decomposition, not dF1 vs R alone, must decide.
- The suffix changes the prompt; style drift and CIDEr loss are possible (hence K2).
Fit with PD-2 (methodology pillar, either outcome):
- (i) Residual decomposition into ranking + allocation + base. It explains why selection methods (UPE, PLAN-F1)
  fail at matched count: a weak base ranker at m > 2.3, not their selection logic.
- (ii) Matched-count comparisons must match the per-image allocation, not just the mean: 1-2.5 F1 at the same
  mean on dev.
- (iii) A new kind of stress test for frontier invariance (explicit count control by one model across m ≈ 1-3.5).
- Positive H1 would be the first method in this project to survive count control. Expect H0.

- Feasibility 5: 2.4 GPU-h dev stage, existing recipe, scripts ready, no repo edits needed.
