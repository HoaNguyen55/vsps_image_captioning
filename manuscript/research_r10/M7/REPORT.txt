0. BOTTOM LINE
- Idea: let the captioner keep objects it is unsure about as calibrated hedges ("có thể có X") instead of
  dropping or asserting them, and score them with a proper rule.
- Main result (a theorem plus a dev300 check): hedging CANNOT escape the count frontier. Under any metric that is
  linear or multilinear in per-mention credit, a hard caption with the same expected count weakly dominates every
  hedged caption. On dev300, hedging is a pure count shift: dF1 vs the same-count vote control is +0.09 [0.00, +0.29].
- Hedging pays only under a strictly proper rule. The per-class Brier score is the proper extension of FP+FN.
  Under it, the Bayes 3-level hedged caption cuts loss by 15% vs the best hard caption (2.24 vs 2.64 per image).
  The gain is calibration of the same signal, not better discrimination.
- New corpus facts: Vietnamese references almost never hedge (KTVIC test 0/2,790; train 0.058%). P1 never hedges
  (0/558). Zero-shot Qwen2.5-VL hedges in 58% of its detailed captions, and those hedges carry signal: 47% of
  hedged mentions are present vs 82% of asserted ones (COCO-2500, human instance labels).
- Value: an evaluation pillar for PD-2 rather than a frontier-beating method. It closes "why not just hedge?",
  adds a gaming-proof metric, and documents that SFT on hedge-free references erases an informative channel.

1. PROBLEM AND HYPOTHESIS
Setting. Each image i has candidate classes o (113-entry lexicon, rescap.chair.objects_in). Gold y_o is 1 when a
KTVIC reference names o. q_o = P(y_o = 1 | model signal). A caption states each o at level c_o in {0, h, 1}
(omit, hedge, assert).
H1 (no escape; theorem, falsifiable empirically through its assumption that q ranks like the votes).
  Take metrics that give a mention credit w_o and score TP_w = sum w_o y_o, M_w = sum w_o (micro P = TP_w/M_w,
  macro R = TP_w/|G|). At a fixed per-image M_w = K, E[TP_w] = sum w_o q_o is maximised by the fractional-knapsack
  solution: the top floor(K) objects by q, plus a fraction of the next one.
  Expected F1 with independent inclusions (a reader who samples a set from the stated levels) is multilinear in
  w, so its maximum over [0,1]^n sits at a vertex, which is a hard caption.
  => A hedge gains recall at exactly the precision cost of a fractional mention. Any "gain" is a count shift.
  Prediction P1: on KTVIC test, the binary F1 of hedged captions (hedges counted as mentions) minus the same-count
  raw-vote control is in [-0.5, +0.5] with a CI covering 0. Linear-credit F1 sits at or below the
  fractional-knapsack hard control.
H2 (where hedging pays). Use the per-class Brier score B = sum_o (c_o - y_o)^2 over the fixed class universe.
  For c in {0,1} it equals FP + FN, so it is the strictly proper extension of today's binary object metric.
  For the level set {0, h, 1} the Bayes act is: hedge iff h/2 < q <= (1+h)/2, assert iff q > (1+h)/2. With h = 0.5
  that is hedge for q in (0.25, 0.75] and assert above 0.75. The expected loss of a hedge is 0.25 whatever q is,
  vs min(q, 1-q) for the best hard choice.
  Prediction P2: the Bayes 3-level caption beats the best hard caption (assert iff q > 0.5) by 10-18% Brier on
  KTVIC test.
  Murphy view: the gain comes from stating levels close to the hit rates (reliability) and from a 3-way split of
  the SAME q. No new discrimination enters, so the ranking, and with it the count frontier, is unchanged.
H3 (semantic validity). "có thể có X" claims uncertainty about PRESENCE. But reference gold and sample votes
  measure SALIENCE (would a captioner mention X?), and 56.5% of P1's reference-"false positives" are real objects
  (CONTEXT section 2, C3 panel).
  Prediction P3: hedges chosen from vote share are present in 25-75% of cases under human instance labels
  (COCO-2500), at least 15 points below asserted mentions. If they are present in more than 75% of cases, the
  hedges mislabel salience as doubt; vote share is then the wrong hedge signal (see kill rule K3).

    Signal (FLAGGED MODEL SIGNAL): P1_dev greedy plus its 12 samples (6 at T0.5, 6 at T0.8).

    - Binary metric: hedging is UPE/PLAN-F1 again, a pure count shift. If the matcher drops hedges, every row
      falls back to greedy (54.57).
    - Linear partial credit: hedged captions are BELOW the fractional-knapsack hard control at the same weighted

6. RISKS AND FIT WITH PD-2
Risks: (a) the Brier gain looks tautological (proper rules reward calibration); the result is the PAIR: gain
under proper rules, zero under count-matched F1. (b) Salience vs presence (H3/K3). (c) CIDEr style tax.
(d) Few zero-shot hedges (229 on COCO). (e) Clause-level hedge scope is approximate; a parser is the upgrade.
(f) Vietnamese hedge strength is not normed; h = 0.5 is fixed a priori, not claimed as the reader's reading.
Fit: this strengthens the EVALUATION/METHODOLOGY pillar.
  (i) A general impossibility result that protects the count-matched endpoint from a whole class of "recall at no
      precision cost" claims, hedging included.
  (ii) A proper, gaming-proof object metric (Brier = FP+FN extended) usable for any system that hedges, e.g. the
       zero-shot detailed baseline, whose 34.7% of hedge-scoped mentions current matchers count as assertions.
  (iii) Resource-style facts: hedge rates in five Vietnamese human corpora; SFT erasure from 58% to 0%.
  It does NOT supply the missing frontier-beating METHOD, and by H1 it cannot.
