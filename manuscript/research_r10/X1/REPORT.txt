===== 0. BOTTOM LINE ==============================================================================================
- Every field where "more output scores higher" already has a control: BLEU's BP, length-controlled AlpacaEval, ROUGE normalised by
  a random system of equal length, faithfulness against a control curve, recall@N proposals. In LVLM hallucination the closest is
  LeHaCE (NeurIPS 2024): a per-model length-hallucination curve, with models compared at one word length.
- Our edge is the unit and the design: OBJECT counts, per-image matching, one pre-registered frontier for all systems, two golds.
  New CPU evidence for the unit: a token-length control still credits MBR with +2.60 F1 (P1) and +1.95 (fuse2); the object-count
  frontier credits -0.14 and -0.76. Across 32 trained runs, log count explains precision better than log length (R2 0.96 vs 0.83).
- Gemini's envelope holds on all 85 runs: max residual +1.58, no residual CI above 0, 32 runs below -20. It is a production frontier
  in the SFA/DEA sense; the residual reads as one-sided "inefficiency".
- M2's tie problem has a textbook fix (tie-aware metrics, McSherry & Najork 2008): use the exact tie-averaged control. At PLAN-F1's
  k_i, ties decide 7.4% of selected slots, and random tie-breaking alone moves the control within a 95% band of [58.74, 59.70].
  With ties averaged, the vote-statistic family spans 59.22-59.60, vs 58.87-59.90 for M2's three deterministic variants.
- Scoop: no verified captioning/LVLM work does per-image object-count matching, frontier residuals, or count-matched re-scoring
  of released outputs (E1). Risk moderate: cite LeHaCE, CCEval, SumGD and VGD and say exactly how we differ.

===== 1. PROBLEM AND HYPOTHESES (falsifiable) =====================================================================
Reviewers will ask: (i) why not control token length, as AlpacaEval-LC and LeHaCE do? (ii) is this just CHAIR_i (per-mention
normalisation)? (iii) is the control well defined? (iv) what exactly is new?
H1 (unit). Object count m is a better control variable than caption length for object P/F1. Predictions: (a) across trained runs,
   R2(P ~ log m) > R2(P ~ log length); (b) for decoders that change object density (MBR, RL), a token-length control leaves most
   of the raw gain and the count control removes it. Falsified if (a) reverses, or if on E1's English outputs length control
   removes as much as count control (|difference| < 0.5 P; pilot P-B).
H2 (normalisation is not control). At constant quality, precision falls with count: P(M) = 78.52 (M/1.78)^-0.253. So CHAIR_i and
   per-100-token rates reward saying less: doubling m on the frontier gives P 78.5 -> 65.9 (non-gold share 21.5% -> 34.1%) with no change in
   quality. Falsified if b ~ 1 on a new dataset (G3 predicts b < 1).
H3 (control definition). With ties averaged, the matched-count control is unique and deterministic, and the family of defensible
   vote statistics spans < 0.5 F1. Falsified if the tie-averaged family spans > 0.5 F1 on test pools.
H4 (envelope). The pre-registered frontier is an upper envelope: no system has a residual CI lower bound > 0, and defective pipelines
   sit far below. Falsified by a well-trained run with residual CI > 0 that no method change explains.

2a. CROSS-DOMAIN CONTROLS, AND WHAT WE TAKE FROM EACH
LLM evaluation (length / verbosity)
 [L1] Dubois et al., Length-Controlled AlpacaEval, arXiv 2404.04475: GLM regression sets the length difference to 0; Arena
      correlation 0.94 -> 0.98. Ours is matching, not regression: assumption-light, per image.
 [L2] Hu et al., Explaining Length Bias in LLM-Based Preference Evaluations, 2407.01085: win rate = desirability + "information
      mass"; AdapAlpaca compares within matched length intervals. Closest analogue of our paired control (tokens vs objects).
 [L3] Singhal et al., Length Correlations in RLHF, 2310.03716: much of the RLHF reward gain is length. Parallel to E1's "~80% of
      published CHAIR precision gains are count".
 [L4] Zheng et al. 2306.05685 (LLM-judge verbosity bias); Saito et al. 2310.10076; Chen et al., MLLM-as-a-Judge, 2402.04788;
      Park et al., R-DPO, 2403.19159; Li et al., Arena-Hard style control, 2406.11939.
 [L5] Zheng et al., Null Models Achieve High Win Rates, 2410.07137: length-controlled metrics can still be gamed. A control must be
      hard to manipulate (cf. M6: the per-image control cannot see allocation).
MT
 [T1] Papineni et al., BLEU, ACL 2002, DOI 10.3115/1073083.1073135: the BP is a one-sided, corpus-level length correction.
      Our frontier corrects both directions, in object units.
 [T2] Murray & Chiang, Correcting Length Bias in NMT, 1808.10006: the bias sits in the search objective too (cf. UPE/MBR).
 [T3] Koehn, Significance Tests for MT Evaluation, EMNLP 2004, ACL W04-3250: paired bootstrap (we use it).
 [T4] Freitag et al., References are not Innocent, 2004.06063: reference style biases metrics (cf. our two golds).
Summarization (coverage vs faithfulness)
 [S1] Sun, Shapira, Dagan & Nenkova 2019, DOI 10.18653/v1/W19-2303: ROUGE F1 is non-linear in length; normalise by a random system
      of the same length; RE-EXAMINES published claims. Precedent for E1.
 [S2] Ladhak et al., Faithful or Extractive?, ACL 2022, DOI 10.18653/v1/2022.acl-long.100: a trade-off curve from control models
      gives "effective faithfulness"; published methods fail to beat the control at matched abstractiveness. Closest ancestor of
      our frontier residual.
 [S3] Nenkova & Passonneau, Pyramid, HLT-NAACL 2004, ACL N04-1019: units weighted by how many references contain them (typicality);
      score normalised by an ideal summary with the SAME number of units (a count-matched ideal). Closest analogue of G3's P_pool.
 [S4] Over, Dang & Harman, DUC in context, IPM 2007, DOI 10.1016/j.ipm.2007.01.019: fixed-length truncation before scoring
      (analogue of E1's matched prefix).
 [S5] Wei et al., Long-form factuality / F1@K, 2403.18802: unit-level recall against a target count K (fixed, not matched).
 [S6] Liu et al., RoSE / ACU, 2212.07981 (ID checked; its length-normalised ACU score: VERIFY).
Detection / IR (operating points)
 [D1] Davis & Goadrich 2006, DOI 10.1145/1143844.1143874; Provost & Fawcett 2001, ROC convex hull, DOI 10.1023/A:1007601015854:
      classifiers below the hull are dominated (analogue of the envelope).
 [D2] Hosang et al., Detection proposals, TPAMI 2016, DOI 10.1109/TPAMI.2015.2465908: recall at a matched NUMBER of proposals per
      image. Nearest count-matched protocol in vision.
 [D3] Yuan et al., Variable number of keyphrases, 1810.05241: F1@k / F1@M / F1@O (cut at the gold count). Set-generation analogue
      of our count regimes.
 [D4] Hoiem et al. 2012, DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-33712-3_25 (AP normalised for numbers of positives); Bolya et al., TIDE, 2008.08115.
 [D5] Lewis, SIGIR 1995, DOI 10.1145/215206.215366; Nan Ye et al. 1206.4625; Lipton et al. 1402.1892: the expected-F-optimal set is
      top-k by marginal probability. This explains why PLAN-F1 "only sets k_i".
 [D6] Yang, SIGIR 2001, DOI 10.1145/383952.383975 (RCut per-instance top-k vs SCut/PCut global thresholds); Arampatzis et al. 2009,
      DOI 10.1145/1571941.1572031 (per-query cutoffs). This is M6's per-image vs global allocation.
Ties, matching, robust controls
 [R1] McSherry & Najork, ECIR 2008, DOI 10.1007/978-3-540-78646-7_38: tie-aware metric = expectation over orderings of tied

 [R2] Abadie & Imbens, Econometrica 2006, DOI 10.1111/j.1468-0262.2006.00655.x (tied matches kept with equal weight: VERIFY wording);

Frontiers and decompositions
 [F1] Aigner, Lovell & Schmidt 1977, SFA, DOI 10.1016/0304-4076(77)90052-5 (composed error = noise - one-sided inefficiency);

2c. WHAT IS NEW, PER ELEMENT (novelty rating)
 (a) Object counts, not tokens: MODERATE-HIGH. LVLM work controls words or tokens (#1, #3, #5, #6) or only reports mean objects (#2).
     Our metric is defined over objects, so the control is in the metric's own unit, as in [D2] and [D3]. Evidence: Sec. 3c.
 (b) Pre-registered power-law frontier: MODERATE. Control curves exist ([S1], [S2], LeHaCE). New: exponent and anchor fixed before
     looking; one frontier for all systems ("invariance"); an upper-envelope (SFA/DEA) reading; G3's split of b into reference
     saturation and system decay D, which LeHaCE's slope conflates.
 (c) Paired same-count controls: MODERATE. Interval matching [L2] and exact matching [R2] exist. New: a control built from the
     method's own sample pool, per image, with an exact tie-averaged expectation (3a) and paired bootstrap [T3].
 (d) Two golds: MODERATE-LOW. CHAIR merges them [G1]; RePOPE and ReaL fix labels. New: the same frontier and residuals under
     reference typicality (KTVIC) AND independent truth (COCO-80, second human panel). fuse2-detailed trails P1 by 12.1 P on KTVIC
     refs (67.21 vs 79.30) but by 0.9 on COCO-80 (88.94 vs 89.84).
 (e) Vietnamese: LOW as method, HIGH as context. Not a "first" claim; write "to our knowledge, no Vietnamese captioning work
     controls for count".

2d. SIBLING FINDINGS AGAINST PRIOR ART
 - E1 (re-scoring released English outputs). Genre: [S1], [S2], Dacrema [R2] (claims vanish under a control). In LVLM hallucination I
   found no per-image count-matched re-scoring (#1-#10). Novelty moderate-high in-field, moderate overall. Caveat: the matched
   prefix inherits [S4]'s truncation bias. Hallucinations come late in captions (LURE 2310.00754, repo-cited), so truncation
   favours the truncated side. Also report TRF and tie-averaged random-subset controls.
 - G3 (P_sys = s1 (M/M1)^-D P_pool). Analogues: the Pyramid ideal at equal count [S3], species accumulation [F3], LeHaCE's slope ~ D.
   New: an exact h-reference pool precision, and b_sys = b_pool - D. Novelty moderate; no scoop found.
 - M6 (allocation). Water-filling at a fixed total matches the micro-F result that one global threshold on marginals is optimal
   (Koyejo et al., NeurIPS 2015: VERIFY), i.e. RCut vs SCut [D6]. New: the evaluation point that the per-image control is blind to
   allocation. CAR is a Kitagawa/Oaxaca decomposition [F2]; telescoping is order-dependent, so Shapley-average the 6 orders before
   ruling on "UPE is (not) a pure count shift".
 - M2 + 3a: [R1] and [R2] say average over ties, then report the family (multiverse) of statistics. Never maximise over arbitrary
   tie-breakers: that selects on noise.
 - Gemini's envelope: SFA/DEA [F1] and the ROC hull [D1]. Report the residual as inefficiency with a noise band (3b).

===== 6. RISKS AND FIT WITH PD-2 ==================================================================================
Risks:
 - LeHaCE is close enough for a reviewer to call (b) incremental. The answer is 3c (same outputs, opposite verdicts); P-B and P-C
   make it a pre-registered result.
 - 3c uses one family of Vietnamese runs. Object density per token depends on model and language, so P-B (English) matters.
 - The frontier is anchored on P1; "envelope" is an empirical property of 85 runs, not a theorem. A method above it is the goal.
 - Matched-prefix / truncation controls (E1) are biased toward the truncated side; tie-averaged random-subset controls are not.
 - Each part's novelty is moderate. The package (object unit + frontier + paired tie-aware control + two golds + released-output
   audit) is what is new. Present it as a protocol with its cross-domain lineage explicit.
Fit: directly strengthens the EVALUATION/METHODOLOGY pillar. 2a = related-work paragraph; 3a = the control definition
("tie-averaged same-count control"); 3b-3c = two figures (85-run residual distribution; length vs count verdicts). It does not supply
a frontier-beating method.
