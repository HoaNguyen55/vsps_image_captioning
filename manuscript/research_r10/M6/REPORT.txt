==========================================================================================================================
0. BOTTOM LINE
==========================================================================================================================
- Theory. At a fixed TOTAL count B, the F1-optimal allocation for micro-P / macro-R is water-filling: keep the B (image, class)
  pairs with the largest s = q + kappa*E[y/|G_i|]. C5's PLAN-F1 rule is this allocation at its own B. Gains need cross-image
  dispersion of the marginal score; with exchangeable images the gain is zero (Jensen).
- Allocation alone does beat the pre-registered frontier at equal total count, but only at LOW counts. dev300, global
  pool-vote allocation: +2.0 F1 at m 1.76, +1.5 at m 2.0, +0.4 at m 2.25; it crosses below the frontier at m* ~ 2.4.
- PLAN-F1 (m 2.63) sat past the crossing. Its cap of 3 also made the allocation near-uniform. Its -2.49 residual splits into:
  cap -0.93, macro term -0.84, calibrated q instead of raw votes -0.54, and a remainder of -0.18.
- Realisable now with no training: choose ONE existing caption per image by a knapsack at greedy's own total count.
  dev300: dF1 +2.08 [+0.05, +4.11], CIDEr-D -2.3 [-8.8, +4.3]. That is UPE's F1, reached at greedy's count.
- The main value is methodological. PD-2's per-image same-count control cannot see allocation (this arm scores +0.09 against it).
  A telescoping Count-Allocation-Ranking (CAR) decomposition shows that UPE is NOT a pure count shift. Novelty: moderate.

1. PROBLEM, THEORY AND FALSIFIABLE PREDICTIONS
==========================================================================================================================
Metric (scripts/object_prf.py): P = sum_i TP_i / sum_i k_i (micro), R = mean_i TP_i/|G_i| (macro), F = 2PR/(P+R).
Total B = sum_i k_i = N*m. Each image i has a ranked candidate list j = 1..n_i with posterior p_ij = P(y_ij = 1).
Define w_ij = E[y_ij / |G_i|] (C5's Poisson-binomial term).

T1 (water-filling).
  At fixed B, dF = (F_P/B) dTP + (F_R/N) dU, with U = sum_i TP_i/|G_i|. Hence dF is proportional to sum (p + kappa*w) over the
  items that change, with kappa = P^2 m / R^2 (exactly C5's kappa).
  The large-N optimum therefore keeps the global top-B items by s_ij = p_ij + kappa*w_ij. Within an image, w is increasing in
  p (prereg 2026-10-13_plan_f1.md, section 3), so k_i* = #{j : s_ij >= tau_B}.
  PLAN-F1 uncapped is this allocation at the B where tau_B = P*. The cap adds a constraint k_i <= 3, and it binds on 70% of
  dev images.
T2 (no dispersion, no gain).
  If images are exchangeable given the evidence (same marginal-score sequence), then sum_{j<=k} s_i(j) is concave in k.
  By Jensen, uniform k_i is optimal. Allocation gains are bounded by how far the evidence separates images AT THE MARGIN.
T3 (exchange formula).
  Take a reference allocation with included set I and excluded set E. The gain is Delta(B) = sum_r (s^E_(r) - s^I_(r))_+,
  with s^E sorted descending and s^I ascending.
  In F1 points, dF ~ 2R^2/(P+R)^2 dP + 2P^2/(P+R)^2 dR. At greedy's point, recall is worth P^2/R^2 = 3.0x precision.
  Check on dev: global vote at greedy's B swaps 61 greedy mentions (41.0% gold) for 62 pool mentions (66.1% gold).
  That is +15.3 TP, so dP ~ +2.9. Observed: +2.84.
T4 (frontier crossing).
  Start from a system on the frontier at m0 (greedy, residual ~ 0). At m = m0, any Delta > 0 puts the allocator above the
  frontier at EQUAL total count.
  For m > m0 the allocator must also add (m - m0)N pool items. Their mean precision c_A(m) must exceed the frontier chord
    c_f = TP0 [(m/m0)^b - 1] / (m - m0)   (b = 0.7468; c_f -> b*P0 at m0: 0.59 official, 0.55 dev-anchored;
    dev-anchored chord to m 2.63 = 0.526).
  The pool has only 54 non-greedy items with vote share >= 0.5 (65% gold). After those, candidates are 50% and then 31% gold.
  So c_A falls below c_f, and the crossing on dev300 is m* ~ 2.4.
  Consequence: allocation alone can beat the frontier only for m below m*. The width of that window is set by pool coverage
  and depth: 65.0% of gold classes are in the 13-caption pool.
Hypotheses and falsifiable predictions (test, pre-registered, nothing tuned on test):
  H1 At each seed's greedy total count, global pool-vote allocation beats greedy at the set level: dF1 >= +1.0, CI > 0.
     (dev, 13 captions: +1.84 [+0.29, +3.34]; 7 captions, T0.5: +1.52 [+0.09, +2.95]).
  H2 In CAR, the allocation term B is > 0 for the global-vote set and for the knapsack captions (dev: +1.27 / +1.42).
     The per-image PD-2 term A is ~ 0 (dev: +0.00 / +0.09).
  H3 Crossing: the set-level residual against the official frontier is > 0 at m0, ~0 at m ~ 2.4, and < 0 at m >= 2.75.
  H4 With 13 captions, the knapsack captions beat greedy at Delta m = 0: dF1 >= +1.0, CI > 0, CIDEr-D >= greedy - 5.
  H5 UPE's test gain re-attributes to count + allocation (dev: C +0.92 [+0.52, +1.32], B +0.62 [-0.38, +1.71]).
     Its ranking term stays ~ 0 (dev +0.14), which agrees with the reported "+0.07 vs count control".

2. CLOSEST PRIOR WORK (V = verified today by web search; C5 = verified in R9 C5 lit/verified.tsv via arXiv/Crossref APIs)
==========================================================================================================================
1. Lipton, Elkan, Narayanaswamy (arXiv PDF header: Naryanaswamy), ECML-PKDD 2014, arXiv:1402.1892 [V, C5].
   - Theirs: calibrated scores give an optimal F1 threshold of F*/2; corpus F1 couples instances through one global threshold.
   - New here: the fixed-budget form for the MIXED micro-P / macro-R metric, the allocation decomposition, and the frontier
     test. The global-threshold structure is theirs. Rating: LOW for the rule.
2. Nan Ye, Chai, Lee, Chieu, ICML 2012, arXiv:1206.4625 [C5]; Dembczynski, Waegeman, Cheng, Huellermeier, NIPS 2011 (GFM)
   [C5]; Jansche, ACL 2007, P07-1093 [C5].
   - Theirs: exact expected-F maximisers for ONE F over one label set, i.e. instance-F when applied per image.
   - New here: a corpus-level budget across images. C5 measured per-image exact EF1 as 0.2-0.7 below the corpus rule. MODERATE.
3. Koyejo, Natarajan, Ravikumar, Dhillon, NIPS 2015, "Consistent Multilabel Classification" [C5].
   - Theirs: micro averaging gives a shared threshold; macro gives per-label thresholds; instance averaging gives per-instance
     thresholds.
   - New here: micro-P mixed with instance-averaged R. The kappa*E[y/|G_i|] term couples the two. MODERATE (C5's closed form).
4. Schultheis, Kotlowski, Wydmuch, Babbar, Borman, Dembczynski, ICLR 2024, arXiv:2401.16594 [V].
   - Theirs: "macro-at-k", with exactly k labels per instance (a PER-INSTANCE budget).
   - New here: a TOTAL budget with free per-instance k_i, and showing that k_i allocation is a separable source of gain.
     MODERATE. This is the closest "budgeted" prior.
5. McDonald, ECIR 2007, DOI 10.1007/978-3-540-71496-5_51 [V]; Lin & Bilmes, NAACL-HLT 2010, ACL N10-1134 [V].
   - Theirs: knapsack, ILP or budgeted-submodular selection of content units under a length budget within one summary.
   - New here: the budget is shared ACROSS images and scored by a corpus micro/macro metric. MODERATE.
6. Arampatzis, Kamps, Robertson, SIGIR 2009, pp. 524-531, "Where to stop reading a ranked list?" [V, title/venue/pages; DOI
   10.1145/1571941.1572031 NOT confirmed]; Bahri, Tay, Zheng, Metzler, Tomkins, SIGIR 2020 "Choppy", arXiv:2004.13012 [V].
   - Theirs: per-query ranked-list truncation for per-query metrics (F1, DCG). This is the analogue of choosing k_i.
   - Not theirs: a shared total budget or corpus-level coupling. MODERATE.
7. Nakov, Guzman, Vogel, COLING 2012, ACL C12-1121 [V].
   - Theirs: sentence-level vs corpus-level BLEU yields different (shorter) outputs; corpus metrics couple length across items.
   - This is a precedent for "corpus metric -> allocation effects", not for objects. LOW-MODERATE.
8. In-house: R9 C5 (closed-form rule), PLAN-F1 prereg and dev_plan_critique.json, UPE-MBR.
   - Here: the first decomposition of their gains into count / allocation / ranking.
Scoop search: 3 WebSearch queries plus 7 verification queries (budget allocation across instances, caption object-count
allocation, global vs per-instance top-k). None found per-image allocation of a fixed TOTAL mention budget for a corpus micro-P / macro-R object metric. Closest
hits: refs 1, 4 and 6. Coverage gaps: no DBLP or Semantic Scholar API, and papers from Sep-Oct 2026 may be missed.
Novelty overall:
  - CAR decomposition plus the crossing condition: MODERATE (an evaluation contribution);
  - knapsack / global-threshold selection: LOW (standard machinery; the count-matched evaluation is the point).

6. RISKS AND FIT WITH PD-2
==========================================================================================================================
Risks.
- Multiplicity and small N. About 6 arms were examined at B = 529 on 300 images; the best p is .028-.044. Test must be
  pre-registered and confirmatory. The effect is small (+1.5 to +2 F1) and the CIs touch 0.
- Sampling depth. The realisable gain needs 12 samples (7-caption pool: +0.78 n.s., CIDEr -6.6). Test CIDEr could fall more
  than on dev.
- Transductive budget. Matching B uses the test batch (no labels). A frozen vote threshold (5/13) is the online form.
- The set-level arm empties 16 vs 9 images (no lexicon object). The knapsack arm avoids this.
- Model signals. q uses model votes (flagged) and a dev-fitted calibrator on human refs (cross-fitted). No AI labels.
- Calibrator transfer. P-M6c applies a P1_dev calibrator to p1assist pools; C5 measured ECE 0.03-0.06 for this transfer.
- Macro artifact? No. The gains are mostly micro precision (dP +2.65 / +2.84 at equal total count), i.e. more correct
  mentions for the same number of mentions.
Fit with PD-2.
- Evaluation pillar (main). CAR refines the primary endpoint: PD-2's per-image same-count control is the A term only, and
  it cannot see allocation. With CAR, UPE's gain is re-attributed (count +0.92, allocation +0.62) instead of being called a
  pure count shift.
- Method pillar. This gives the first candidate method that is count-matched at the TOTAL level and sits above the frontier
  on dev (+2.08). It is cheap and needs no training. The paper must state that allocation is the source of the gain.
- Explains PLAN-F1's failure with a falsifiable law (crossing at m* ~ 2.4), not as noise.
