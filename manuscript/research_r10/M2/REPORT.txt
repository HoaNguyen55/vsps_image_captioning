R10 / M2 -- Learned human mention-worthiness (salience) for content selection
Agent M2, 2026-10-14. CPU-only. Folder: research_r10/M2/ (m2_salience.py, m2_pilot.json, pilot_stdout.txt,
error_analysis.txt). dev300 only; no test labels were read; no OpenViIC text was used.

0. BOTTOM LINE
- Direction is NEGATIVE at CPU level. I learned which classes KTVIC annotators name (class prior, k-of-5 reference
  consensus, first-mention position, co-occurrence) from train-minus-dev300 references only (3,469 images x 5 refs).
  I combined this with the model's sample votes (MODEL SIGNAL) by zero-fit Bayesian transforms and by cross-fitted
  within-image ranking models. On dev300 none of these beats the strongest raw-vote ranking at matched per-image
  counts k_i, in any of 5 count regimes. At PLAN-F1's k_i: best human+vote rule -0.35 F1 [-1.53, +0.87] vs pool-vote.
- Within-image headroom is real (oracle at matched k_i: +9.9 F1). But the boundary errors are image-specific: the
  same classes (người, áo, đàn ông, phụ nữ, đường) are false positives in some images and misses in others. Class-
  level salience cannot reorder them. About 25% of the oracle's helpful swaps are word choice inside one lexical family.
- Useful by-product for PD-2 (methodology): the "raw-vote" matched control is sensitive to tie-breaking. Its variants
  differ by up to 1.03 F1 at the SAME counts (58.87 / 59.52 / 59.90). A learned vote-only calibration "wins"
  +0.92 [+0.18, +1.74] over PLAN-F1's control, yet -0.10 vs the strongest variant. Pre-register the control as the
  best of a fixed vote-ranker family, or any sub-1-F1 "win" is tie-breaking.
- Novelty: low (importance-from-descriptions is Berg et al. 2012 / Spain & Perona 2008-11). Expected value: a
  short negative result plus a control-design rule. Recommend no GPU, except optionally one <=0.5 GPU-h grounded-
  salience check (Sec. 4) with a hard kill rule.

1. PROBLEM AND HYPOTHESIS
Setting. Per dev300 image, the pool is P1_dev s42 greedy + 6 samples at T0.5 + 6 at T0.8 (PLAN-F1's frozen pool).
Candidates are the classes any pool caption names (rescap.chair 113-class lexicon, frozen matcher fd1d0123 via
RESCAP_RAW_TONE=1). Label y = class is in the union of the 5 references. Metric: object_prf micro-P / macro-R / F1.
Primary endpoint: paired DeltaF1 (bootstrap B=5000 over images) vs a same-count control: top-k_i by vote, at
identical k_i per image. PLAN-F1 showed that its calibrated q only sets k_i; within-image order is the only lever left.
H1: a class's human mention-worthiness, learned from train refs, carries within-image ranking information beyond the
  model's votes. Predictions:
  (a) a cross-fitted votes+human ranker beats the strongest vote control at PLAN-F1's k_i: DeltaF1 >= +1.0, CI > 0;
  (b) the gain holds at greedy k_i and fixed k = 2, 3;
  (c) dropping the human features (votes-only learned ranker) loses that gain.
H1 is falsified if (a) fails. RESULT: (a), (b) and (c) all fail (Sec. 3).
H2 (zero-parameter): P1 was SFT'd on ONE caption per image (plan_f1.py docstring: "first caption"), so sample votes
  estimate a single annotator's naming rate. The gold label is a union over 5 annotators. A class-specific
  beta-binomial fitted on the train k-of-5 profile, updated with the votes as pseudo-annotators (n_eff), gives
  P(any of 5 names o). Its within-image order differs from raw vote. Prediction: beats pool-vote at matched k_i.
  RESULT: falsified (-0.47 [-1.48, +0.56] at n_eff=2; no n_eff in {1, 2, 4, 8, 13} wins).

2. CLOSEST PRIOR WORK (verified by web search on 2026-10-14 unless marked)
[1] Berg, Berg, Daume III, Dodge, Goyal, Han, Mensch, Mitchell, Sood, Stratos, Yamaguchi, "Understanding and
    Predicting Importance in Images", CVPR 2012, DOI 10.1109/CVPR.2012.6248100. Defines importance as what people
    choose to describe. Predicts it from compositional, semantic and context factors. M2's class prior and
    co-occurrence are its "semantic/context" factors. New here: tested as a re-ranker on top of LVLM sample votes
    at matched counts; it adds nothing. Novelty vs [1]: low.
[2] Spain & Perona, "Some Objects Are More Equal than Others: Measuring and Predicting Importance", ECCV 2008,
    DOI 10.1007/978-3-540-88682-2_40; journal version "Measuring and Predicting Object Importance", IJCV 91 (2011)
    (IJCV DOI NOT verified). Importance = probability an object is named, measured from many annotators; position
    and size are predictive. M2's k-of-5 consensus is this measure. Novelty vs [2]: low.
[3] Yatskar, Galley, Vanderwende, Zettlemoyer, "See No Evil, Say No Evil: Description Generation from Densely
    Labeled Images", *SEM 2014, DOI 10.3115/v1/S14-1015. Content selection from dense human labels. Novelty: low-mod.
    (M2 has no dense labels; uses LVLM samples as the candidate generator.)
[4] Yun, Peng, Samaras, Zelinsky, T. Berg, "Studying Relationships between Human Gaze, Description, and Computer
    Vision", CVPR 2013, DOI 10.1109/CVPR.2013.101. What is looked at vs what is described. Novelty: moderate (we
    use no gaze).
[5] Jas & Parikh, "Image Specificity", CVPR 2015, arXiv 1502.04569. Description consistency across annotators varies
    per image. M2's beta-binomial over-dispersion is a class-level analogue. Novelty: moderate.
[6] Madhyastha, Wang, Specia, "VIFIDEL", ACL 2019, P19-1654, DOI 10.18653/v1/P19-1654, arXiv 1907.09340. Weights
    objects by how many references mention them, in an evaluation metric. Same signal as M2's "how many of 5 refs",
    used for scoring rather than selection. Novelty: low-mod.
[7] Cornia, Baraldi, Cucchiara, "Show, Control and Tell", CVPR 2019, arXiv 1811.10652. Region-set/sequence-
    controlled captioning: the realiser side of content selection (PLAN-F1's realiser is this idea). Novelty: n/a.
[8] Borgeaud & Emerson, "Leveraging Sentence Similarity in NLG: Improving Beam Search using Range Voting", WNGT 2020,
    arXiv 1908.06288. Outputs voting for each other (consensus over samples). The vote baseline is in this family.
[9] (2026, found by search, CONTENT UNVERIFIED) PatchGate arXiv 2608.21819 (omitted-but-visible objects in frozen
    VLMs); ProCap arXiv 2607.21022 (prominence-ranked object injection, video). Closest recent "seen-but-not-said"
    work; it uses detector/patch evidence, not human mention statistics.
Overall novelty of M2 as proposed: LOW. Possibly new but unpromising: the matched-count, within-image test of human
salience on top of LVLM votes (a negative result), and the "union-of-5 posterior with model samples as pseudo-
annotators" (H2). I found no prior instance of H2; not exhaustively searched.

3c. Within-image pairwise AUC (count-free; ties = 1/2): vote 0.727, pool13 0.726, votes-only learned 0.727,

  So human salience does improve global within-image order, by +0.013 AUC. That gain does not reach the top-k_i
  boundary. Likely reason (NOT tested): it reorders low-vote items (s13 = 1: 389 items, gold rate 0.25), which are
  below k_i anyway.
3d. Why it fails (error_analysis.txt):
  - Votes are already well ordered: gold rate by pool votes s13 of 13 is 0.25 (1), 0.45 (2-3), 0.54 (4-6),
    0.65 (7-9), 0.83 (10-12), 0.88 (13).
  - At k=3, the same classes dominate BOTH error sides: người 29 FP / 24 FN, áo 13/21, đàn ông 11/15,
    quầy hàng 15/10, phụ nữ 20/5, đường 22/5. The errors are image-specific, not class-level.
  - The class residual (gold rate minus vote-bin base rate) is |res| < 0.10 for most of the 30 most frequent pool
    classes. Largest: cầu -0.22 (n=18), báo -0.20 (13), kính +0.22 (10), ô tô +0.20 (10).
  - 24-26% of the oracle's beneficial swaps (k=2: 21/86; k=3: 23/89) stay inside one lexical family: person
    words, đường/con đường/vỉa hè, nhà/toà nhà, ... (my grouping; see Claim 9). That is wording, not content
    selection.
  - Interpretation: SFT on human captions has already internalised class-level mention-worthiness into the votes.
    What remains is image-conditional (which person word fits this image; is the shirt prominent here).
3e. Multiplicity: 16 candidate rankers (+3 controls, +oracle) x 5 regimes, + 1 post-hoc, all on dev300. Nothing passes, so selection bias
  cannot create a false positive here. It only means the near-miss CIs are not inferential.

6. RISKS AND FIT WITH PD-2
Risks:
- The pilot uses one seed (P1_dev s42) and 300 images. CIs are about +/-1 F1, so effects below ~1 F1 cannot be
  resolved. A real +0.5 gain could be hidden. But the point estimates are negative for every human-feature model
  in 4/5 regimes.
- Human statistics come from KTVIC style. Under a different guideline (OpenViIC panel) salience may differ. Untested.
- G-SAL relies on model-made boxes, which are AI-made: usable inside a method (flagged), never as evidence.
Fit with PD-2:
- Strengthens the evaluation/methodology pillar.
  (i) Control-family rule: tie-breaking alone moves matched-count F1 by up to 1.03.
  (ii) An error budget for object F1 on the same pool:
       count (frontier);
       pool coverage (35% of gold never sampled);
       within-pool ranking (oracle +9.9 at matched k_i), about a quarter of it lexical-family choice;
       class-level human salience explains ~0 of the ranking part.
  (iii) A clean negative: "SFT already internalises human mention-worthiness". It complements the PLAN-F1 and UPE
       negatives, which reviewers will ask about.
- It does NOT supply the method that beats the count frontier.
