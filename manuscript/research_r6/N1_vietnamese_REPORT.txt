N1_vietnamese REPORT - Is there a VIETNAMESE-SPECIFIC lever that raises KTVIC CIDEr (short, word-level) and stacks
with CN-RLOO (B1 proposal P1)?   Round R6, 2026-10-12.  Agent N1.
CPU only (nice 15, <= 4 processes, bootstrap B = 2000, paired over images). No GPU, no VLM/LLM loaded (only the
Qwen2.5-VL tokenizer.json vocabulary file was downloaded, for a fertility count). No new labels; no AI-made labels.
Repo untouched. Work dir: research_r6/N1_vietnamese/ (code *.py, outputs out/).
All numbers are on KTVIC TEST predictions/samples and are EFFECT-SIZE ESTIMATES (nothing was tuned on them; the one
tuned knob, Form-MBR's K, was chosen on the KTVIC-TRAIN dev300 split of B2).

==================================================================================================================
0. BOTTOM LINE
==================================================================================================================
- Measured negative. No Vietnamese-specific component that I could build and pilot raises CIDEr over plain
  word-level CIDEr-D. Tested: 17 alternative rewards, 2 decoding constraints, 4 target-style rewrite rules and a
  tokenizer check. All were neutral or harmful, with CIs.
- Vietnamese grammatical variation holds a large ALL-REFERENCE oracle stake. This covers optional classifiers,
  plural quantifiers, aspect and locative morphemes, the existential/locative/nominalised sentence frames, and
  fillers. Fixing only these in P1's captions is worth +26.5 [+23.7, +29.3] CIDEr (3-seed pool). That is about
  half of B1's best-of-6 content oracle (+57).
- None of that stake transfers. Choose the edits on refs A and score on disjoint refs B: −5.4 [−8.1, −2.7].
  Human captions show the same pattern: a human ref against the other four gives +23.3 oracle / −9.4 held-out.
  It is reference noise, not a P1 deficit, and it cannot be learned, normalised or decoded away.
- Segmentation is not where CIDEr is lost:
  - RDRSegmenter splits identical syllable strings the same way 99.5% of the time;
  - P1 and the refs disagree on boundaries in 0.16% of shared spans;
  - the re-segmentation oracle gives +1.7 on all refs and +0.05 held-out.
  Orthography is 0 (KTVIC uses old-style oà/oá and P1 already does). Sino-Vietnamese/native and North/South
  dialect pairs give +0.9 / +0.2. Reduplication is negligible. Qwen BPE fertility does not predict under-generation.
- Where P1 is measurably worse than humans (1v4 protocol, same 4 refs):
  - person-reference granularity (người ↔ người phụ nữ ↔ cô gái ↔ người đàn ông ↔ chàng trai):
    +6.6 [+4.2, +9.1] more oracle headroom than a human caption;
  - plural quantifiers (những vs nhiều / một vài): +3.2 [+1.9, +4.5];
  - mid/long-tail culture vocabulary: recall 28.6% vs 49.0% (mid), 0% vs 48.4% (tail, n = 31).
  P1 inherited the first two from reference position 0. Their transferable corpus-level CIDEr is small: naming
  +2.0 [−0.5, +4.6], quantifiers +0.8 [−0.6, +2.1], long-tail culture +1.0 [−0.1, +2.5].
- For CN-RLOO this means: keep plain word-level CIDEr-D with RDRSegmenter in the reward loop, plus EOS as in B1.
  - The syllable-level reward is worse: −1.6 [−3.1, −0.1] on held-out refs, −0.6 [−1.0, −0.2] on the
    OpenViIC panel.
  - Every form-canonical ("Vietnamese analytic stemming") reward is worse by −1.8 to −4.0 at a 1-nat step.
  - Naming and culture bonuses are neutral to harmful.
  The Vietnamese-specific content (person naming, culture terms, "những") is learnable by the plain reward, and
  only content transfers.
- Proposals: both candidates taken to pilot (section 6) FAIL the CPU gate. I recommend 0 GPU-h for
  Vietnamese-specific components. The reportable Vietnamese contribution is the analysis in sections 3-5: a
  held-out-reference decomposition of CIDEr headroom by Vietnamese phenomenon, with a human control. It explains
  why surface normalisation, segmentation and reward tweaks cannot move KTVIC CIDEr. No prior work doing this was
  found (section 7).

==================================================================================================================
1. SETUP AND VALIDITY
==================================================================================================================
- Scorer: B2's exact pycocoevalcap CIDEr-D replica (common.py) with B2's RDRSegmenter cache (cache/seg.json) and an
  N1 overlay cache (cache_seg.json, VnCoreNLP-1.2 RDRSegmenter via py_vncorenlp, lower()). Every edited string is
  re-segmented by RDRSegmenter before it is scored, except the SEG oracle, which is by design a boundary-only edit.
- Reproduction of data/results/*.json word-level CIDEr: p1_s42 149.79, p1_s43 143.92, p1_s44 143.70,
  fuse2_s42 143.90, fuse2_s44 142.21 (= stored rows).
- Oracle protocol (oracle_run.py; edits.py; namer_build.py):
  - For each phenomenon, an edit set restricted to that phenomenon (section 3).
  - Greedy hill-climb on CIDEr-D, 4 steps (8 for FORM).
  - Every edit must create only bigrams attested >= 2 times in KTVIC TRAIN refs. This grammaticality guard blocks
    metric hacks such as "ngôi ảnh".
  - "oracle(all)": edits chosen and scored on all 5 refs (upper bound of the CIDEr at stake).
  - "held-out": edits chosen on refs A, scored on disjoint refs B (IDF of each set). Averaged over the four
    non-interleaved splits {012→34, 234→01, 01→234, 34→012}. Interleaved splits are inflated by the paraphrase
    chain (B1, B2).
  - Run on 5 systems (P1 seeds 42/43/44, fuse2 seeds 42/44). Pooled CIs average per image over seeds, then
    bootstrap.
- Human control (oracle_1v4.py): hypothesis = human ref k, refs = the other 4 (and P1 against the same 4).
  - oracle(4) = edits chosen and scored on the 4.
  - held-out(2→2) = chosen on 2 of them, scored on the other 2.
- Reward pilots reuse B1's framework: 6 T = 0.8 samples + greedy per test image (p1_s43, fuse2_s42; copies in
  B2_targets_data/samples).
  - The train-side reward is computed on refs T. The judge is TRUE word-level CIDEr-D on disjoint refs E (4
    splits) and on the independent UIT-OpenViIC panel (T = all 5 KTVIC refs; panel captions only scored, never
    copied).
  - Two estimates are reported. BoN7 = best-of-7 pick minus greedy. KL1 = the KL-matched one-step policy
    improvement at 1 nat (B1 pilot 4), the offline proxy for one RL update.

  - Rates per caption, P1 vs test refs:
    - "đang" (aspect): 0.763 vs 0.491; position-0 share of train "đang" 25% vs ~19% per other position.
    - "của" (possessive): 0.057 vs 0.173; position-0 share 8%.

  - Orthography: all NFC. KTVIC uses old-style tone placement (test refs: 140 old-style, 0 new-style). P1 has
    14 old-style and 1 new-style.

==================================================================================================================
4. PER-PHENOMENON FINDINGS
==================================================================================================================
4.1 Classifiers (loại từ)
- RDRSegmenter never joins a classifier to its noun, so the slots are clean.
- Oracle swaps are mixed in direction: chiếc→con 7, con→chiếc 5, ngôi→căn 4, căn→ngôi 2, ∅→cái 6, cái→∅ 5.
- Stake +2.7 oracle, −0.7 held-out. P1's excess over a human: +1.2 (oracle).
- Classifier choice varies across refs of the same image (e.g. ngôi nhà vs căn nhà) and is unpredictable noise.

4.2 Segmentation (a3_seg.py)
- Same syllable string segmented as the same word: train refs 99.46%, test refs 99.23%, P1 99.75%. Rare splits:
  toà_thành, không_gian, cổng_thành, lư_hương.
- Shared P1/ref spans: 6 of 3,704 disagree on internal boundaries.
- Re-segmentation oracle +1.65 / held-out +0.05.
- No case for segmentation-aware training or decoding.

4.3 Naming granularity and synonyms
- NAME_GRAN oracle edits are person terms in both directions, so there is no global bias:
  - ∅→phụ nữ 19 (người → người phụ nữ), người phụ nữ→cô gái 14, ∅→đàn ông 12, cô gái→người phụ nữ 8;
  - người đàn ông→nam thanh niên 8, người đàn ông→chàng trai 4.
- Person profile of captions that mention a person (a11_person.py):

                      bare "người"   gendered   age-graded
    test refs              34%          28%         38.5%
    train position 0       42%          34%         26%
    P1 s42                 43%          28%         29%

- On 211 images where >= 3 refs name the person specifically, P1 stays bare on 14-18%.
- NAME_SYN edits (cửa↔quầy, quầy→sạp, hoa quả→trái cây, mũ→nón) carry +0.5 held-out.
- Word-level train phi (phi_words.py) conflates synonyms with frame complementarity (khung_cảnh vs phụ_nữ), so
  object-level phi with A7 lists (nap.py) was used.

4.4 Culture-specific concepts (a5_culture.py, a5b, a5c; an a-priori lexicon of 8 categories, not labels)
- P1 recall of culture terms that >= 2 refs use: 41.1% (125/304); >= 3 refs: 52.4%. Precision 71.6% (in any ref).
- 1v4 recall (terms named by >= 2 of the other 4; human vs P1):
    all     50.1% vs 44.1%
    head    50.3% vs 47.6%
    mid     49.0% vs 28.6%
    tail    48.4% vs  0.0%  (n = 31)
    general objects (nap E1t)  53.3% vs 49.5%
  So culture terms are as hard as other objects except in the mid/long tail.
- Systematic misses (P1 hits / images where >= 2 refs use the term), each with P1's generic substitute:
  - sạp 0/15 ("quầy hàng")
  - xuồng 0/10 ("thuyền")
  - vỉa hè 2/15
  - ghe 0/3
  - chợ nổi 0/3
  - phố đi bộ 0/4
  - mái tranh 0/3
  - tết 0/2
  Several of these are Southern regional words.
- Images where a shared culture term is missed: CIDEr 122.6 (n = 157), vs 141.8 with no shared culture term
  (n = 307) and 221.4 where P1 names it (n = 94; selection effect).
- Stake: all culture terms +6.5 oracle / +1.3 held-out. Mid/tail only: +1.9 / +1.0. Corpus-level small.

4.5 Sino-Vietnamese vs native, North/South dialect, reduplication (a6_lexvar.py)
- P1 follows the dominant KTVIC choice: phụ nữ ≫ đàn bà, trưng bày ≫ bày, di chuyển ~ đi.
- KTVIC is lexically mixed North/South: quả 482 vs trái 318, hoa quả 117 vs trái cây 150, mũ 134 vs nón 265,
  kính 216 vs kiếng 0. P1 almost always says "quả" (s42 23/0, s43 24/2) while test refs use "trái" 26% of the
  time.
- Within-image alternation in 31 (SV) and 20 (dialect) test images. Even the KTVIC paper's Figure 1 alternates
  thìa/muỗng and bát/tô in one image.
- Swap oracle +0.92 / held-out +0.20.
- Reduplicative words: 4.7% of ref captions, 3.8% of P1's; negligible.

4.6 Word order, frames and template structure
- FRAME +6.9 / −1.6. The 4-gram ratio is the best (0.873), so P1 is not losing on Vietnamese word order.
- The KTVIC chain is largely frame rewriting of the same content (có X xuất hiện ở Y ↔ ở Y có sự xuất hiện của X
  ↔ đây là khung cảnh...). KTVIC paper: 3 participants typed 5 sequential boxes per image, then 2 researchers
  revised.

4.7 Plural marking (a10_plural.py)
- P1 under-uses "những" even against position 0: 0.034 vs 0.118 per caption (positions 1-4: 0.135-0.154). It
  over-uses nhiều (0.27-0.30 vs 0.19-0.21) and một_vài (0.10-0.11 vs ~0.06). This is a model-level bias beyond
  position 0.
- Fixed rules lose: nhiều→những −5.08 [−6.97, −3.33] (3 seeds pooled); một_vài→những +0.28 [−0.47, +1.05].
  Only the context choice matters (QNT held-out +0.8).

4.8 Tokenizer (a9_fertility.py; Qwen2.5-VL tokenizer.json only)
- P1 recall of words that >= 2 refs use, by BPE tokens/syllable, within train-frequency tiers, is non-monotone.
  Train freq 20-100: 21.4 / 24.0 / 27.9 / 28.1% for fertility <= 1 / 1-1.6 / 1.6-2.2 / > 2.2.
- Mean fertility of content words: P1 1.20 vs refs 1.23. sạp and quầy both have fertility 2.0.
- No evidence for a vocabulary change.

6. PROPOSALS (at most 2): both candidates FAIL the CPU gate; recommended GPU-h = 0
==================================================================================================================
V1. ViStem-RLOO: CN-RLOO with a Vietnamese optional-morpheme-canonical CIDEr-D reward.
  Plug-in: replace r = CIDEr-D(c, R) by CIDEr-D(canon(c), canon(R)), or by the mix. Same rollouts, copy mask, EOS
    and KL as CN-RLOO; canonicalisation costs about 0.1 ms per caption on CPU.
  Pilot: section 5.1. KL1 Δ −1.8 to −4.0 (all 8 CIs < 0, both policies); mixes −0.4 to −1.1 (7 of 8 n.s.).
  Closest prior and delta:
  - CIDEr (1411.5726) stems words; CIDEr-D removed stemming to reward correct word forms.
  - METEOR stemming and synonyms. Learned compact reward 2003.10925.
  - chrF++ vs BLEU rewards for low-resource MT RL (2601.12535: +2.47 vs +1.60 chrF++).
  - Delta: the isolating-language analogue (optional classifiers, aspect, locative, plural morphemes and frames)
    inside an RL reward, tested on held-out refs.
  GPU: the same as CN-RLOO (about 10 GPU-h per seed on one 3090); no extra memory.
  Verdict: do not run. No synthetic data.
V2. Vietnamese slot-MBR / Form-MBR decoding on top of the CN-RLOO policy.
  Plug-in: post-hoc. Greedy plus 6 samples; edit only Vietnamese grammatical or person-naming slots by expected
    CIDEr against the samples (or train-retrieved refs).
  Pilot: section 5.3. +0.3 to +0.9, all CIs span 0. Form-MBR fails dev.
  Closest prior and delta:
  - CBDT 2509.12677 (expected utility from retrieved memory; BERTScore; English MSCOCO/nocaps; BLEU 25.6 → 26.0
    over MBR).
  - Devlin et al. 1505.04467 (kNN consensus).
  - MBR captioning (B2 3.6: +2.0, n.s.).

8. CAVEATS
==================================================================================================================
- The oracles are greedy hill-climbs over hand-defined edit sets; the all-ref numbers are upper bounds. The
  held-out numbers depend on the selection rule. Three softer selectors (Form-MBR, slot-MBR, the KL-matched
  reward steps) agree with them.
- Lists (classifiers, quantifiers, SV/dialect pairs, culture lexicon) are a-priori analysis lexicons, not labels.
  They may miss items; an unlisted variant counts as "not that phenomenon".
- Reward pilots use 6 T = 0.8 samples of two SFT policies. The RL'd policy samples at T = 1 and drifts further.
  The pilots bound one update, not a full run.
- 1v4 human held-out is biased negative (chain neighbours). The P1−human ORACLE(4) difference is the cleaner
  statistic.
- OpenViIC panel captions were read for scoring only; none are reproduced here.
