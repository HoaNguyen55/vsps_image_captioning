0. BOTTOM LINE
==========================================================================================================
(a) The only target change the evidence supports is plain multi-reference SFT ("MR5": all 5 KTVIC captions as
    rows, uniform weight). Every consensus-type alternative from the brief (n-gram MBR reference, CST/WXE
    consensus weights, validity-weighted sampling, cross-panel selection, length-calibrated targets, sharper
    "reference temperature") ties with or loses to MR5 on the TRAIN dev split, and the single-target variants
    tie with or lose to the existing P1 target (the FIRST caption).
(b) Reason (new, measured): KTVIC's 5 references are a PARAPHRASE CHAIN. Adjacent positions are near-rewrites
    (mean pairwise CIDEr-D 199-256), non-adjacent ones are not (134-162), on train and on test. In-panel
    consensus therefore mostly detects chain neighbours. The first caption is the canonical, most
    transferable style. P1 has learned that style: its single-reference CIDEr-D is 219.5 against position 0 and
    124-163 against positions 1-4.
(c) Expected gain of MR5 over the P1 3-seed mean (145.8): +3 to +9 CIDEr on KTVIC (point estimate about +5),
    CHAIR_i not worse. On UIT-ViIC, whose references are exchangeable, the gain should be larger (+5 to +15).
    Probability that MR5 alone clears the +8 "breakthrough" bar: about 25%. Method novelty is low (standard
    practice). The reportable novelty is the measured failure of consensus targets on chain-dependent references.
(d) Extra existing data has little n-gram headroom. KTVIC-train captions already contain 99.63% of
    test-reference word tokens and 100% of test object mentions. OpenViIC captions score CIDEr-D 45 against
    KTVIC references, and untagged style-selected OpenViIC captions LOWER the stand-in (-6.3 [-12.1,-1.0] at
    k=10; -2.6 n.s. at k=30). The closest prior for prompt/tag-separated multi-dataset training reports +0.2 CIDEr.
    The one data design with a plausible gain is PanelBridge (section 6, proposal 2). It makes KTVIC-style
    targets for the 8,642 OpenViIC images that have no KTVIC copy, so it USES SYNTHETIC TRAINING TARGETS
    (user veto point). Expected gain +0 to +5, low confidence.
(e) URGENT, outside B2: in train_stage2.py at HEAD, `--loss-mask assistant` is a no-op (section 1). The
    "mask-fix" run on n2 right now (p1mask_s42) is a legacy-mask run.

6. PROPOSALS (at most 2)
==========================================================================================================
P-B2-1  MR5: uniform 5-reference SFT (all KTVIC captions as rows), consistent loss mask
  Mechanism: keeps P1's canonical mode and adds the n-grams that rewrites 1-4 share. In the stand-in, positions
    1-4 rise by 8-16 and position 0 is unchanged. No new hyperparameter (same prompt, lr 1e-4, r16, 2 epochs as P1).
  Novel element: none in method (standard). The reportable part is the KTVIC evidence that MBR, consensus
    weighting, validity sampling and cross-panel selection all lose to uniform MR5 because of reference-chain
    dependence. This kills the "MBR reference target" line.
  Closest prior: Zheng et al. 2018 [4] (first-ref -> all-5: +13.4 CIDEr on a COCO LSTM), Hessel et al. 2015 [5];
    consensus variants [1-3]. Delta: none methodologically.
  Pilot: already queued (gold_multiref on n2). (1) Decide the mask: fix the wiring (section 1) or keep legacy for
    both P1 and MR5. (2) Selection run on the TRAIN dev split: MR5 vs P1, both trained without the 300 dev images
    (~8.9 + 1.8 GPU-h). (3) One seed on full train, scored on test with the per-position single-ref profile.
    Pre-registered: pos2-pos4 each +5 or more, pos0 within -5. Also CHAIR_i, and a paired bootstrap vs P1 s42-44.
  Expected: KTVIC short CIDEr +3 to +9 over 145.8 (point about +5, ~151); B-4 +0.5 to +2; CHAIR_i within +-1.5.
    UIT-ViIC +5 to +15 over a first-caption baseline. Derivation: stand-in relative gain +5.4%/+10.2% (test) and
    +6.6%/+8.0% (dev), discounted 40-60% for a pretrained 7B VLM (my assumption); literature range +2.4 to +13.4.
  GPU-h (n2 measured 0.92 s per row-epoch: P1 = 6,938 s for 3,769 x 2): MR5 9.6 h + 0.3 h eval per KTVIC seed;
    3 seeds 30 h; dev pair 10.7 h; UIT-ViIC MR5 6.9 h/seed x 3 = 21 h. Total about 62 GPU-h; pilot about 11 GPU-h.
  Kill: dev-split MR5 - P1 < +2 CIDEr (or the paired 95% lower bound < 0); or the first test seed < 147.8;
    or CHAIR_i up by more than 2 points.

P-B2-2  PanelBridge: second-panel style pivot to label the 8,642 OpenViIC images with no KTVIC copy
  [FLAG: depends on SYNTHETIC training targets. The targets are model-written text, though their content comes
   from human OpenViIC captions. User veto point.]
  Mechanism: the stand-in says that what moves n-gram metrics is more images carrying the KTVIC style. Four
    times the images gives +22 to +26 stand-in points, and all 5 refs are worth about one doubling. Real
    OpenViIC text cannot supply that (style gap, untagged harm), and its vocabulary adds nothing (99.6% covered).
    PanelBridge learns, on the 3,724 doubly-annotated train images, a text-to-text mapping from the set of
    OpenViIC human captions to a KTVIC-style caption (real human pairs), then applies it to the FREE images.
    Train MR5 rows + bridged rows (2.3x more images). Bridged rows carry an instruction suffix tag that is
    absent at test, as in tagged back-translation. Content is human-attested, which avoids E5's
    self-confirmation, and the style comes from real pairs.
  Novel element: an in-language second caption panel on overlapping images used as a supervised style pivot that
    fuses a caption SET into the target benchmark style, so the other images get targets whose content is human.
  Closest prior: language pivoting [18] (pivot = another language; its pairs come from a separate parallel
    corpus, not the same images); fluency-guided translated captions [19]; tagged BT [13]; CapFilt [20]
    (content from the captioner itself).
  Pilot: gate 0 (CPU, done): 8,642 FREE images after dropping all 601 test and 3,939 train matches. Re-check
    SSCD >= 0.4 vs the 558 test images. Gate 1 (~2.5 GPU-h): train the bridge (text-only LoRA on the Qwen2.5-VL
    LM; input = OpenViIC captions, target = each KTVIC caption) on train minus dev. The bridged captions of the dev
    images must reach dev CIDEr >= P1-dev's image-based CIDEr, else stop. The CPU kNN bridge gives 104.9-117.1
    on dev; P1 is about 146 on test. Gate 2 (~13 GPU-h): MR5+bridge vs MR5 on dev; then test seeds.
  Expected: +0 to +5 CIDEr over MR5 on KTVIC, possibly lower CHAIR_i. Alone it has under a 10% chance of the +8 bar.
    Stacked with MR5 the total is about +4 to +12, maybe 30%. On UIT-ViIC the same design uses English COCO
    captions as the pivot panel (2,695 doubly-annotated images -> the other Karpathy-train images).
  GPU-h: bridge 1.5 + bridge inference 0.5 + P1-dev 1.8 + MR5+bridge dev run 13.3 + 3 test seeds x 14 = about
    59 GPU-h (KTVIC). The UIT-ViIC replication is extra.
  Kill: gate 1 fails; or MR5+bridge - MR5 < +1.5 CIDEr on dev; or CHAIR_i up by more than 1.5.
  Non-synthetic fallback (not a separate proposal): the same rows with the human OpenViIC captions under an
    OpenViIC-style instruction. Expected about 0 [11]; worth running only as the control of P-B2-2.

==========================================================================================================
7. CAVEATS
==========================================================================================================
- The stand-in has no shared visual learning. Its absolute CIDEr (105-126) is far below the VLM (146), and its
  deltas are not VLM deltas: the expected gains apply a discount of my own choosing. It ranks policies; it
  does not measure their size.
- Train leave-out (3.3) is biased by chain adjacency. Use it only for target-level statements.
- The KTVIC "5 refs from 3 participants typed by one person" note explains the chain, but annotator identity is
  not in the data. The position effects are what can be measured.
- Test-set numbers are effect-size estimates. Selection was done on dev only: uniform MR5 vs weighted, subsets,
  and single targets.
