======================================================================================================
0. BOTTOM LINE
======================================================================================================
- RL on CIDEr-D is the most direct metric lever left, and the only RL variant that held up in these pilots.
  Expect about +5 to +9 CIDEr over P1 (3-seed mean 145.8). That is in line with SCST on strong COCO baselines
  (+6.1 to +14.1, median +8.5, for 12 models with CE CIDEr >= 130). The +8 bar is reachable but not likely:
  confidence medium for >= +5, low for >= +8.
- The +57 best-of-6(+greedy) oracle is mostly winner's curse. Picking with references disjoint from the
  scoring references keeps only +0.1 to +15.0 of it (mean about +8). A KL-matched one-step policy improvement
  at 1 nat beats greedy by +8.5 [+1.0, +15.4] (P1) and +7.0 [-0.6, +14.1] (fuse2) on held-out references.
  That is the realistic ceiling for one round of on-policy RL from these SFT policies.
- Reward hacking: the top-reward samples are not degenerate. They win on content: the specific salient object
  or action that the annotators named. Word-level CIDEr-D punishes repetition (-40) and template padding
  (-60). The open loophole is truncation: dropping the last word raises the reward on 29-30% of images.
  The reward therefore needs an EOS token in it (SacreEOS).
- Main new hazard, measured here: regurgitation. On SFT'd TRAIN images, verbatim copies of a reference take
  20.3% of the positive RLOO advantage mass, and <=1-word near-copies take 34.5%. A copy's mean advantage is
  +131.8 against -12.2 for other samples. Copying the SFT target still beats greedy by +19 to +28 CIDEr even
  when it is scored only on references not adjacent to it. Picking held-out references therefore cannot close
  this channel. This is the most likely mechanism behind the E5 RFT/DPO CIDEr drop.
- Killed by the pilots before any GPU use:
  - jackknife-min / leave-closest-reference-out reward
  - reliability-weighted (empirical-Bayes) advantages
  - cross-panel RL on OpenViIC-only images
  - reference-free self-consensus RL (TTRL-style)
  - low-capacity / global-preference RL
  - held-out-reference reward as a copy fix
- Proposals (section 3):
  - P1 CN-RLOO: copy-neutral RLOO on word-level, EOS-aware CIDEr-D. New element: an RL-time advantage mask
    for regurgitated references, with a detector calibrated on images the SFT model never saw.
  - P2 EHX-RLOO: Grounding-DINO tags used only as train-time exploration hints, with the content they uncover
    distilled into the tag-free captioner by importance-weighted policy gradient. Flagged as using
    model-generated training signal.

1a. SCST and its variants
- Rennie et al., Self-critical Sequence Training, arXiv 1612.00563 (FT). REINFORCE with the greedy caption as
  baseline, CIDEr-D reward. Raised the COCO test-server best CIDEr-D from 104.9 to 114.7.
- Luo, A Better Variant of SCST, arXiv 2003.09971 (FT). Uses the mean reward of the other K-1 samples as
  baseline (K=5). Beats the greedy baseline on every architecture, e.g. Transformer 126.6 -> 129.6 and UpDown
  122.7 -> 123.9. Over 5 seeds (UpDown-Long) the gain is +0.6 (127.3 -> 127.9).
- Gao et al., Self-critical n-step training, arXiv 1904.06861. Bayesian SCST, arXiv 2004.02435. Both are
  variance-reduction and baseline variants of the same family.
- Hu, Cavicchioli, Capotondi, A request for clarity over the EOS token in SCST, arXiv 2305.12254 (FT). If EOS
  is omitted from the CIDEr-D reward, models gain "up to +4.1 CIDEr-D using trivial sentence fragments" (bad
  endings). They propose the SacreEOS signature.
- Cornia et al., M2 Transformer, arXiv 1912.08226. SCST with beam candidates and a mean-of-beams baseline.
- dos Santos et al., CIDEr-R, arXiv 2109.13701. With long, variable-length captions, SCST on CIDEr-D learns to
  repeat words to fix length; CIDEr-R is the fix.
- Wang and Chan, Describing like humans, arXiv 1903.12020. CIDEr-RL lowers caption diversity.
- Liang, GRPO for Image Captioning, arXiv 2503.01333 (FT). Weak ResNet50 + 1-layer decoder. COCO: CE 84.3,
  SCST 97.6, GRPO (G=5) 100.0. Flickr8k (6K train images, close to our scale): CE 34.2, SCST 36.6 (+2.4),
  GRPO 38.3 (+4.1). SCST "occasionally crashes".
- Moratelli et al., DiCO, arXiv 2408.14547 (FT, BMVC'24). SCST with CLIP-S/PAC-S rewards hacks the metric
  (e.g. "A rusted rusty rusty rusty rusty"). On a CIDEr-optimised Transformer, DiCO gets 144.2 vs 143.3 for SCST.
- Cho et al., CLIP reward, arXiv 2205.13115. Repetition and grammar hacks under CLIP-S; needs a grammar term.

1c. RL for LVLM captioners, 2024-2026: none of these reports a CIDEr gain
- CapRL, arXiv 2509.22647 (FT; ICLR'26). GRPO; reward = an LLM answering MCQs from the caption; Qwen2.5-VL-3B
  policy; ~75K images. No COCO CIDEr/BLEU/CHAIR. Reward hacking with other judges: verbose captions, or
  collapse to ":description".
- SC-Captioner, arXiv 2508.06125 (FT; ICCV'25). LoRA; policy gradient with KL; scene-graph edit reward;
  6.5K COCO images. Qwen2-VL-7B DOCCI500 B-4 40.92 -> 44.88, COCO-LN500 B-4 34.59 -> 35.05; LLaVA-1.5
  COCO-LN500 B-4 33.81 -> 31.60. No CIDEr.
- BalCapRL, arXiv 2605.07394 (FT). LLM-judge precision, reference-coverage and linguistic rewards; c-GDPO;
  group of 8; length-conditional reward masking (keep the linguistic reward only if 0.5 <= len/len_ref <= 2);
  ~90K images; 8x B200 for 24-46 h. No CIDEr. Hacks: meta-commentary, 3x length, over-short captions.
- CCCaption, arXiv 2602.21655 (FT). GRPO, rollout 5; completeness/correctness rewards from LVLM-generated
  queries; Qwen3-VL-2B. No CIDEr.
- Also checked: CycleCap 2603.18282 (GRPO with an image-reconstruction reward), Re3Cap 2608.21305,
  RubiCap 2603.09160, FoCUS 2609.00709, SimLoss 2609.00591, LoGIC 2507.08610 (BLEU 44 -> 46 from a
  communication game). None optimises or reports CIDEr-D on short captions.

1e. Bounds, noise, hints, memorisation

- Yue et al., arXiv 2504.13837: RLVR improves sampling efficiency inside the base model's support and rarely
  exceeds base pass@k. This motivates using the SFT sample distribution as the bound.
- Policy-gradient estimators: RLOO arXiv 2402.14740; GRPO arXiv 2402.03300; DAPO arXiv 2503.14476;
  Dr. GRPO arXiv 2503.20783 (drop std normalisation).
- Noise-aware advantages: S-GRPO arXiv 2508.05928 and ODRPO arXiv 2605.12667. Both are prior art for

- Pessimism: reward-model ensemble worst-case optimisation, Coste et al. arXiv 2310.02743. Prior for the
  jackknife-min reward; pilot 2 shows it hurts here.
- Test-time RL: TTRL arXiv 2504.16084; spurious-signal amplification DDRL arXiv 2604.21327.
- Hints / off-policy guidance: QuestA arXiv 2507.13266; LUFFY arXiv 2504.14945; ADHint arXiv 2512.13095;

2.7 What reward hacking looks like
  - Top-reward vs greedy captions (examples from the 12 largest oracle gains; model outputs from p1, p1, fuse2):
    - "có nhiều người đang xuất hiện ở trong con phố" (26.8) -> "có những chiếc đèn lồng được treo ở phía trên
      của con đường" (435.0)
    - "có nhiều người đang đứng ở bên lề đường" (12.1) -> "có nhiều người đang xếp hàng trước một quán ăn" (383.0)
    - "cô gái đang ngồi ở cầu thang của một cây cầu" (61.5) -> "có một cô gái đeo kính râm đang ngồi trên bậc thang"
      (389.8)
    => Gains come from naming the specific salient thing; generic greedy content ("nhiều người ... xuất hiện")
       loses.
  - Style statistics (p1): function-word endings 0% in every group.
    - Repeated bigram: refs 1.6%, greedy 1.3%, oracle picks 0.4%, worst picks 3.2%.
    - Length: refs 12.1, greedy 11.9, oracle 12.0 syllables.
    - Starts with "có": refs 55.5%, greedy 95.5%, oracle 86.4%. Refs start with "ở" 20.8%; the model almost never does.
    - distinct-2: greedy 0.170, oracle 0.193, worst 0.270.
    => Inside the SFT support, high reward does not mean degenerate output.
  - Edit probes on the greedy caption: mean delta CIDEr-D, and share of images where the reward goes up.

2.8 Memorisation channel on SFT'd TRAIN images (the hazard that decides the RL design)
  - Verbatim sentence copies of a reference:
    - train (fuse2 detailed rollouts, T=0.8): 3.07%; <=1-word edit 6.63%; captions containing a copied
      sentence 5.53%
    - test, fuse2 detailed greedy: 1.31% / 2.90%
    - test, short samples: 0.87-0.90% / 3.0-3.3%
  - First sentence of train rollouts:
    - mean CIDEr-D 152.0, best of 6 244.5, within-group SD 64.8
      (test comparator: fuse2 detailed greedy first sentence 145.9)
    - exact copies: 5.1% of samples, 17.2% of groups; mean reward 388.5 vs 139.3 for non-copies
    - 15.9% of best-of-6 picks are copies
  - RLOO on these groups:
    - copies take 20.3% of the positive advantage mass; <=1-edit near-copies take 34.5%
    - mean advantage +131.8 (copy) vs -12.2 (non-copy)
  - Copying the SFT target (ref #0) still beats greedy when scored only on other references:
    - p1: +43.6 [33.2, 53.8] on refs{1-4}, +27.6 [17.3, 37.9] on refs{2,3,4}, +27.0 [15.5, 39.2] on refs{3,4}
    - fuse2: +41.1 / +23.3 / +19.1, all CIs above 0
    - train, copy of ref #0: 169.7 / 144.3 / 136.2
    => Human captions are more consensual than model captions under ANY human reference set. A held-out-
       reference reward (the ACF-RLOO idea) does not remove the incentive to regurgitate; the copies have to
       be neutralised directly.

2.9 Do detector-tag-conditioned captions add exploration value? (supports P2)
  - Tag-conditioned greedy (data/results/tags_*-short) equals tag-free greedy on 50.7% / 50.2% of images.
  - It beats tag-free greedy on 27.1% / 24.9% and beats the best of 6 tag-free samples on 18.1% / 15.2%.
  - Adding that one candidate to the pool, with picking and scoring on disjoint references:
    - p1: +0.8 [-0.7, 2.5] (A012->B34), +2.0 [0.3, 3.8] (A234->B01)
    - fuse2: +3.1 [0.7, 5.8], +1.2 [-1.1, 3.6]

2.10 What this bounds
  The SFT policies already sample captions that are much better than greedy. Most of that apparent headroom
  (about 85%) is reference idiosyncrasy. The transferable part is image-specific content choice, worth about
  +8 on disjoint references at a ~1-nat KL budget. Train-time rewards are polluted by regurgitation (a third of
  the positive gradient). The realistic RL target is therefore +5 to +9 CIDEr. +8 needs everything to go right:
  copy neutralisation, EOS, enough KL, and train-to-test generalisation from 3.5K images.
  Caveat: RL would sample at T=1.0, not 0.8, and P1 seed 43 (143.9) is below the 3-seed mean. G0 below
  re-measures these quantities on a dev split.

3. PROPOSALS (at most 2; ranked by expected metric gain x novelty / cost)
======================================================================================================
P1. CN-RLOO: Copy-Neutral RLOO on word-level, EOS-aware CIDEr-D
Mechanism.
- Initialisation: the P1 SFT LoRA (r16) on NF4 4-bit Qwen2.5-VL-7B, 512 vision tokens, short prompt.
  Two PEFT adapters on one base: "ref" (frozen SFT) and "policy" (trainable, initialised from SFT).
- Rollouts: per image, k=8 samples at T=1.0, top_p 1.0, max_new_tokens 40.
- Reward: word-level CIDEr-D (persistent CPU RDRSegmenter worker with a cache; train-split df; sigma 6).
  An explicit EOS token is appended to the candidate and to all 5 references (SacreEOS-style; closes the
  29-30% truncation loophole).
- Copy-neutralisation (the new element): sample c is a copy if a near-copy statistic m(c) exceeds tau.
  - m(c) = max over the image's references of the normalised word edit distance and of single-reference
    BLEU-4.
  - tau = the 99th percentile of m on k=8 rollouts over the 300 dev images (which the SFT model never saw).
    Calibration therefore uses train data only.
  - Copies get advantage 0 and are left out of the leave-one-out baseline.
- Advantage and loss:
  - RLOO advantage, with no per-group std normalisation (Dr. GRPO; pilot 4 shows reweighting groups loses
    0.3-6.5 CIDEr at matched KL).
  - Token-mean policy-gradient loss, PPO clip 0.2 with one inner epoch (on-policy).
  - k3 KL to "ref"; beta chosen on dev from {0.01, 0.03, 0.1}, aiming at 0.5-1.5 nats per caption
    (pilot 5: below 0.5 nat samples do not beat greedy).
- Training and selection:
  - Train on KTVIC train minus 300 dev images (random.Random(0).sample(sorted(train_ids), 300)), 1-2 epochs.
  - Greedy dev CIDEr-D every 500 images; keep the best.
  - Retrain on the full train set with the chosen beta and number of updates.
  - 3 seeds; paired bootstrap against seed-matched P1.
New element: an RL-time advantage mask for regurgitated references, with a detector calibrated on images the
SFT model never saw. Pilot 6 shows that held-out references (the ACF-RLOO idea) and reference selection
cannot remove this channel.
Closest prior and delta:
- SCST 1612.00563, Luo 2003.09971, RLOO 2402.14740: same estimator family, no memorisation handling.
- SacreEOS 2305.12254: EOS diagnosis only.
- Goldfish 2406.10209: SFT-time token dropping, not RL.
- RLHF memorisation study 2406.11715: measures, no fix.
- DAPO 2503.14476: filters zero-variance groups, not copies.
- Delta: neutralise the specific high-advantage regurgitation channel measured here (20-35% of the positive
  gradient), plus the first CIDEr-D RL for a QLoRA 7B LVLM on short Vietnamese captions with a published
  bound analysis.
Pilot (GPU, 2 arms, about 9.5 GPU-h):
- P1_dev SFT on train minus dev (~3 GPU-h; needed for honest tuning anyway).
- G0 (~1.5 GPU-h): k=8 rollouts on 300 SFT'd train images and on the 300 dev images. On CPU: copy rate,
  tau, the T=1.0 reward spread, and best-of-8 gain on disjoint references.
- G1 (~2 x 2.5 GPU-h): RL on 1,000 train images, CN-RLOO vs plain RLOO (same seed and beta), dev eval
  every 250 images.
Memory and time on one RTX 3090 (estimates, to be replaced by G1 measurements):
- Peak memory at k=8: about 12-14 GB.
  - 4-bit base incl. ViT ~6.5 GB; LoRA + Adam ~0.6 GB.
  - Gradient-checkpointed activations for 8 x ~605 tokens ~2-3 GB; response-only logits ~0.1 GB.
  - KV cache ~0.3 GB; allocator ~2 GB.
  - k=4: ~10-11 GB; k=16: ~16-19 GB.
- Time per image at k=8: about 5-7.5 s.
  - Sampling ~2.5-4 s: greedy short measured 0.86 s/img on a 4090; x1.4 for a 3090; bnb-4bit decode
    1.5-2x; batch 8 ~1.2x per step.
  - fwd+bwd ~2-3 s; reference forward ~0.6 s; reward ~0.02 s on CPU.
- Per epoch over 3,469 images: about 4.8-7.2 GPU-h (k=4 about 3.5-4.5; k=16 about 9-13).
- Full plan about 45 GPU-h: 3 + 1.5 + 5 + 3 seeds x ~10 (1.5 epochs) + UIT-ViIC replication ~6.
  A COCO 10K-image replication would add ~15 GPU-h.
Expected gain (KTVIC short, seed-matched, word-level), over P1 (3-seed mean 145.8, so ~151-155):
- CIDEr +5 to +9; B-4 +2 to +5; METEOR +1 to +2; ROUGE-L +1 to +3.
- CHAIR_i -2 to -5 (reference-derived CHAIR moves with CIDEr: the oracle pick took 17.7 to 10.9).
- Basis: COCO SCST median +8.5; pilot transferable +8 (BoN-7, disjoint references) and +7 to +8.5 (1-nat
  step); panel +4.5 to +5.6. Discounted for 3.5K-image generalisation and LoRA capacity.
Kill criterion, at G1 after 1,000 RL images; all three must hold:
- Dev greedy CIDEr-D >= P1_dev + 3.0 with a paired-bootstrap 95% lower bound > 0.
- The exact-copy rate of rollouts on SFT'd images stays <= 2x its step-0 value.
- Function-word endings on dev stay <= 1%.
If only plain RLOO passes, drop the copy mask. If both fail, do one cross-fitted retry (SFT on xfit A, RL
rollouts on B images, and the reverse); if that also fails, the RL lever is dead.
Confidence: medium for >= +5; low for >= +8.
Synthetic training data: no. Only on-policy rollouts scored against existing human references; no generated
text becomes an SFT target.

P2. EHX-RLOO: Evidence-Hinted eXploration (detector tags as train-time hints, distilled into a tag-free captioner)
Mechanism.
- Run Grounding-DINO on KTVIC train images with the same tagger as data/results/tags_gdino.json
  (scripts/tag_images.py), about 1 GPU-h.
- Per image, k=8 rollouts:
  - 4 from the plain short prompt x;
  - 4 from x_h = x + " Các đối tượng có thể có trong ảnh: <tags>." (the repo's tag format).
- Reward: as in P1 (word-level, EOS-aware CIDEr-D, copy-neutral). One RLOO baseline over the pooled group
  of 8, so hinted samples that find the consensus content get positive advantage relative to plain ones.
- Update the tag-free policy pi(.|x) on all 8 samples:
  - plain samples on-policy;
  - hinted samples off-policy, weight w = min(2, pi_theta(c|x) / pi_old(c|x_h)). The numerator comes from
    the training forward; the denominator from generation-time log-probs, so there is no extra pass.
    LUFFY-style policy shaping is the alternative.
  - Plus the on-policy term for pi(.|x_h), so a tagged inference mode improves too.
- Primary evaluation: the plain prompt, with no detector at test time. Secondary row: tagged prompt
  (test tags exist).
New element: open-vocabulary detector evidence used only as a train-time EXPLORATION hint for captioning RL,
with the content it uncovers transferred into a detector-free captioner by importance-weighted policy gradient
inside a pooled hinted+plain group baseline. The reward is untouched, so this is distinct from the killed
evidence-gated PU reward. It follows the round-5 finding that only external evidence moved the frontier.
Closest prior and delta:
- QuestA 2507.13266 (partial-solution hints in math RL; no-hint ability improves) and ADHint 2512.13095.
- LUFFY 2504.14945 (off-policy expert traces in GRPO groups); Selective Expert Guidance 2510.04140.
- Oscar 2004.06165 / VinVL 2101.00529 (tags as inputs at train AND test, with CIDEr optimisation).
- NoisyRollout 2504.13055 (perturbed-image rollouts).
- Delta: the hints are noisy detector outputs, not gold partial answers or expert traces. They exist only at
  training time and are distilled away, and the reward is a continuous reference metric.
Pilot evidence (CPU, test, estimates): the tag-conditioned greedy beats the best of 6 tag-free samples on
15-18% of images. Adding it to the pool adds +0.8 to +3.1 under disjoint-reference selection (2 of 4 CIs
exclude 0; section 2.9).
GPU pilot:
- Tags on 300 dev + 1,000 train images (~0.5 GPU-h).
- G0h (~0.5 GPU-h): 4 plain + 4 hinted samples per dev image. On CPU, check hinted best-of-4 minus plain
  best-of-4 on disjoint references.
- G1h (~2.5 GPU-h): a 1,000-image RL arm, matched to P1's G1.
Memory and time: as P1 at k=8. The tags add about 20 prompt tokens and the extra x_h prefill about 0.3 s per
image, so about 5.5-8 s/image and about 10-11 GPU-h per seed for 1.5 epochs, plus ~1 GPU-h of tagging.
Total about 37 GPU-h: 1 + 0.5 + 2.5 + 3 x 11.
Expected gain: +1 to +3 CIDEr over CN-RLOO at tag-free inference (about +6 to +12 over P1 combined), and
CHAIR_i -1 to -2 more (tags raised P by about +2.2 in round 4).
Kill criterion:
- G0h: hinted best-of-4 minus plain best-of-4 < +2 CIDEr-D, or 95% lower bound <= 0. Stop before RL.
- G1h: plain-prompt dev greedy < CN-RLOO G1 + 1.0 at matched updates. Stop.
Confidence: low.
Synthetic training data: YES, flagged for the user's veto. It trains on rollouts generated under detector-tag
prompts, and the detector outputs are model-generated. No human label is created and nothing model-made is
used for evaluation.

======================================================================================================
4. CAVEATS
======================================================================================================
- Every pilot number is from TEST samples of two SFT policies at T=0.8. They are estimates of headroom, not
  results. G0 re-derives them on a dev split of TRAIN images before any claim.
- The reliability, terciles and KL-step results rest on 6-7 candidates per image. The per-image correlations
  are noisy, though the aggregate effects have bootstrap CIs.
- The memorisation numbers come from fuse2 DETAILED rollouts (first sentence). P1 short rollouts on train
  images were not available. P1 is trained on caption #0 only, so its copy rate may be higher; G0 measures it.
- The time and memory figures are engineering estimates anchored on the repo's 4090 latency file
  (data/results/latency_main_s42.json). G1 must measure them.
- The panel (OpenViIC) captions were only read to score; none are reproduced here.
- The success bar also needs UIT-ViIC, and COCO for language-agnostic methods. Both proposals are
  language-agnostic, so the same recipe must be run with each dataset's own references.
