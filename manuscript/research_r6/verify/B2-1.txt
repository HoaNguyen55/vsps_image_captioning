B2-1 adversarial verification: "MR5, uniform 5-reference SFT (all KTVIC captions as rows), matched loss mask"
2026-10-12. CPU only (nice 15, <= 4 single-thread processes, bootstrap B <= 2000). No GPU, no VLM loaded.
Work dir: verify/B2-1_work/ (code, logs, json). Repo and n2 were read only (n2: ls/cat of queue files, nothing changed).

VERDICT: KILL as an R6 method proposal.
  Novelty: scooped. Multi-reference SFT is the standard recipe, and the proposer concedes "none in method".
  Gain: B2's stand-in gain comes from its decoder. The stand-in picks the pool's MBR-consensus human caption.
        Swap in greedy-type decoders (they reproduce real P1's 96-98% "co" openings) and MR5 minus the P1 target
        on KTVIC is -6.7 to +1.9 CIDEr-D, about 0 on average.
  Power: the proposed gates cannot separate a +5 effect from seed noise.
  Keep at most ONE reconfigured MR5 run, as the standard all-reference baseline/control (section 5), not as a contribution.

2. LENS 3, GAIN REALISM (main refutation)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2a. Decoder swap inside B2's own stand-in. Retrieval is unchanged: TF-IDF of the image's OpenViIC captions.
  - Dev: data/splits/ktvic_dev300.txt (md5 e1af9069, the same file as B2's); 296 queries, database 3,428 images.
  - Test: 555 queries, database 3,724 images.
  Entries are MR5 minus the P1 target (pos0), CIDEr-D x100, with a paired bootstrap 95% CI (B=1000).

  decoder                              dev k=10            dev k=30            test k=10           test k=30
  consensus (B2: MBR over the pool)    +6.0 [-1.8,+13.3]   +7.6 [+1.0,+13.9]   +5.3 [-0.2,+10.9]   +11.5 [+6.5,+17.6]
  trie-greedy (no free parameter)      -2.8 [-11.5,+5.8]   +1.4 [-6.4,+9.2]    +1.9 [-4.1,+8.2]    +1.2 [-5.5,+7.6]
  n-gram greedy (lambda 0.8)           -5.3 [-14.1,+3.7]   -3.4 [-11.8,+5.0]   -6.7 [-13.6,+0.1]   +0.7 [-6.4,+7.3]

  - The consensus row agrees with B2's figures within 1 point (B2: dev +6.9/+8.7, test +5.8/+11.5).
  - trie-greedy: the majority next word among pool captions that share the prefix. This is greedy decoding
    of a model that fits the pool exactly.
  - n-gram greedy: 0.8 x pool + 0.2 x policy-wide stupid-backoff trigram, no repeated trigram.
  - Robustness to lambda (dev, n-gram): lambda 0.5 gives -6.7 / -8.0; lambda 0.95 gives -5.5 / -5.3 (k=10 / k=30).
  - The 7 dev queries with a near-duplicate photo in the database move any delta by at most 1.1.

  Which decoder behaves like the real VLM?
  - Share of outputs starting with "co": real P1 seeds 95.5-97.7%, trie and n-gram 98-100%, consensus 70-86%.
  - Mean words: real P1 10.42-10.50, n-gram 10.5.
  - The consensus decoder selects a central human caption from 5k candidates. Greedy decoding of an SFT model
    does not do that, and that selection is where B2's gain comes from.

  Mechanism on KTVIC:
  - The MR5 mixture mode is still the canonical first-caption template. Share of train captions starting with
    "co": pos0 66.6%, pos1 61.4%, pos2 53.6%, pos3 54.3%, pos4 50.1%.
  - P1 already sits at that mode (96-98% "co").
  - What positions 1-4 add: "o ..." openings (9-18%) and 0.2-1.2 extra words. A greedy decoder will not pick these.

2b. UIT-ViIC, whose references are exchangeable. The decoder swap shrinks the gain but keeps it:
  - consensus: +27.3 / +15.0 (= B2)
  - trie: +14.7 [+4.7, +24.9] / +9.6 [-1.6, +19.6]
  - n-gram: +9.2 [-3.1, +22.0] / +10.2 [-0.2, +20.3]
  So MR5 can help when references are exchangeable. But the UIT-ViIC comparator ("first caption only") is a straw
  baseline, because all-reference cross-entropy training is the standard recipe there.

2c. Evidence from real VLM runs.
  - fuse2's short row is the consensual caption, an MBR-type target. Against P1 s42 it flattened the position
    profile: pos0 -20.6, pos1 -8.3, pos2 -7.7, pos3 +5.6, pos4 +1.2.
  - Yet fuse2 lost 5-reference CIDEr: 143.9 and 142.2, against the legacy P1 mean of 146.3. A flatter profile
    does not by itself raise 5-reference CIDEr.
  - The pre-registration (manuscript/prereg/2026-10-12_ladder_predictions.json) predicts multiref about equal to
    P1 on objects: mentions ratio 0.976, dP +0.5, dR -0.6. Agent A1 predicted recall at least 2 points below P1.

2d. Seed noise. Paired bootstrap over 558 images (B=2000), same recipe, different seed:
  - s42 - s43: +5.88 [+1.03, +10.36]
  - s42 - s44: +6.10 [+1.31, +10.64]
  Both CIs exclude 0 with an identical recipe. p1assist - p1mask: +0.99 [-3.59, +5.43].
  Legacy P1 seeds: 149.8 / 143.9 / 143.7 / 147.7 (mean 146.27, SD 2.97).
  What this means for the proposal's gates:
  - Test gate "first seed >= 147.8": passes a null MR5 with p = 0.30.
  - One-seed dev gate (diff >= +2 and paired lower bound > 0): on 300 images the bound needs diff >= ~6.4.
    It passes a null effect 13% of the time, a true +5 only 41%, and a true +8 only 61%.
  - 80% power with a between-seed t-test needs about 6 seeds per arm for +5, 2-3 for +8, 15 for +3.

2e. Literature effect sizes.
  - The only direct first-reference vs all-references number: Zheng, Ma & Huang 2018 (arXiv:1808.09564, Table 5,
    read). ResNet+LSTM, no pretraining, COCO: 80.70 -> 94.10 (Sample One 85.41). That is a data-starved regime
    60+ points below P1.
  - Consensus-weighting variants on exchangeable references give +1.1 to +3.6:
    VBCS 111.7 -> 114.3; WXE 47.3 -> 49.7 (MSR-VTT); HCO +3.2% relative.
  - No paper reports 1-reference vs all-reference SFT for a pretrained VLM at >= 130 CIDEr, because
    all-reference training is the default.

2f. Leakage and metric gaming.
  - 14 KTVIC test images share an OpenViIC copy with a KTVIC train image. Their train captions score 128.1 against
    the test references (P1 scores 146.9 on them), and P1 copies none of them. Negligible.
  - Verbatim copies of a train caption among test outputs: P1 19.9-22.8% (about 15% are a first caption),
    fuse2 17.4%, E5 16.1%. MR5 at 2 epochs shows each image 10 times, so report copy rate and distinct-n.
  - Length moves +0.2 to +0.4 words in the stand-in. No brevity-penalty or repetition risk.

  => Realistic KTVIC gain for the VLM: about -3 to +3 CIDEr (point 0 to +1), inside seed noise. P(>= +8) < 5%.
     B-4 / METEOR / ROUGE-L within +-1. CHAIR_i about unchanged (pre-registered).

3. LENS 1, NOVELTY: SCOOPED
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Closest prior: Zheng, Ma & Huang, EMNLP 2018 (arXiv:1808.09564). It is exactly first reference -> all
  references (Shuffle) on COCO, +13.4 CIDEr, with pseudo-references adding +3.0 more.
- All-reference cross-entropy is the default COCO recipe. Examples: BLIP fine-tuning (arXiv:2201.12086); GRIT,
  KTVIC's SOTA baseline, trained with 10-epoch XE (arXiv:2401.08100, sec. 5).
- Weighted variants: CST/WXE arXiv:1712.09532; VBCS 2021.maiworkshop-1.6; HCO IJCAI 2020
  (doi:10.24963/ijcai.2020/92); DCR arXiv:2204.03938; Hessel et al. arXiv:1508.02091.
  SMILE (NeurIPS 2023) shows how MLE pushes models toward concise "average" captions.
- The "reportable" finding (chain dependence kills consensus targets) fails on three counts:
  (i) it is an analysis, not the method R6 asks for;
  (ii) it follows from KTVIC's documented protocol;
  (iii) it depends on the decoder. Its policy ranking was measured only with the consensus decoder, and
        section 2a shows that MR5 vs pos0 reverses under greedy decoders.
- Searches. arXiv API (17 queries, emphasis on 2022-2026):
    KTVIC; Vietnamese image captioning; multi-reference caption*; "multiple references" captioning;
    "captions per image" training; MBR caption*; reference selection captioning; GT-caption weighting;
    QLoRA captioning; low-resource captioning VLM; annotator bias in references; multiple captions MLLM
    fine-tuning; self-critical VLM CIDEr; "human consensus" captioning; reference independence of annotators;
    LVLM captioning CIDEr fine-tuning; captioning RL LoRA.
  WebSearch (7 queries):
    1-vs-5 references in VLM fine-tuning; KTVIC VLM results 2025-26; Vietnamese MLLMs on UIT-ViIC
    (Vintern-1B arXiv:2408.12480 has no captioning results); multi-reference MLE and generic captions; SMILE;
    captions per image for BLIP/OFA/GIT; QLoRA Qwen2.5-VL on COCO.
  OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar returned HTTP 429 from this IP.
  Result: no 2022-2026 paper presents uniform multi-reference SFT as a contribution, because it is the default.

4. LENS 2, FEASIBILITY: fits one 3090, but the queued job is not this proposal and the budget is underpowered
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Memory: MR5 rows are as long as P1's, so peak memory equals P1's on a 3090 (QLoRA 4-bit, r16, batch 1 x 8).
- Throughput, from n2's 35_a_p1dev_sft log: 866 steps for 3,469 images x 2 epochs, ~7.3 s per step,
  8 rows per step, i.e. 0.91 s per row-epoch.
  - Full-train MR5 at 2 epochs: 37,690 rows = 9.5 h, plus ~0.6 h eval = ~10 h per seed.
  - MR5 on train minus dev: 8.8 h.
  - P1-dev already exists: B1's 35_a job (seed 42, assistant mask). Reuse it.
- Optimizer steps (linear decay, no warmup):
  - P1 at 2 epochs: 942. MR5 at 2 epochs: 4,711 (5x). The queued job at 1 epoch: 2,356.
  - So "no new hyperparameter" is false. Epochs or steps for 5x the rows must be chosen on dev, and attribution
    needs a step-matched P1 control.
- Queue reality: /root/queue/40_multiref_s42.sh (dated 2026-10-11, before the mask fix) differs from the proposal.
  - It runs --epochs 1 with the LEGACY mask and goes straight to TEST, with no dev gate.
  - Its comparator is the legacy P1 family (mean 146.3), not the method-track baseline p1assist_s42 (148.7).
  - The --loss-mask bug the proposal asks to fix is already fixed at HEAD (559c448). The mask has no detectable
    effect: +0.99 [-3.59, +5.43].
- Budget: the proposal's 62 GPU-h cannot detect +5. A powered version costs about 100 GPU-h:
  6 MR5 seeds (60 h), 5 more P1-assist seeds (11 h), the dev pair (9 h), UIT-ViIC 3+3 seeds (25 h).
  It also competes with B1's RL pilots on n2.
- Implementation risk: low. `build_gold_sft --select all` exists (8d56acc) and the collator is wired.
