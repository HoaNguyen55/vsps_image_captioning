**Vietnamese captioning datasets.** UIT-ViIC (Lam et al., 2020) gives Vietnamese captions for sports images drawn from COCO (Lin et al., 2014), written by independent annotators. Its test images are COCO train2017 images (exposure caveat: Section 3.1)<!-- src: judge ruling CC-024 -->. Its test split has 231 images<!-- src: data/results/uitviic/uitviic_zeroshot-short.json :: n_images -->. KTVIC (Pham et al., 2024) covers the life domain with 4,327 images and 21,635 captions<!-- src: literature: KTVIC paper / FAIR 2026 §IV-A -->. Its annotation interface asks one annotator to enter five captions per image; there are three annotators in total and a revision pass by two researchers (§3.2, Fig. 5). UIT-OpenViIC (Bui et al., 2026; preprint Bui et al., 2023) is a larger open-domain benchmark; its caption-entry protocol is not documented in enough detail to say whether its captions are independent. Its image pool overlaps KTVIC's (Section 3.6), and we use it only through aggregate scores. The Vietnamese part of Crossmodal-3600 (XM3600; Thapliyal et al., 2022) provides an independently written reference set that we use for one cross-corpus punctuation check. Its images come from Open Images (Section 3.1)<!-- src: judge ruling CC-024 -->.<!-- R2 R3-m2: the two exposure caveats here shortened to pointers; the full caveat stays in sec3.1. --> Vietnamese captioning data also exist for narrower domains: the VLSP 2021 VieCap4H challenge on healthcare images (Le et al., 2022), the HDUCap dataset (Hoang et al., 2026) and a scene-text captioning dataset (Nguyen et al., 2026a). van Miltenburg (2024) surveys captioning datasets in languages other than English.

**Vietnamese captioning systems.** Published KTVIC results come from transformer captioners; GRIT (Nguyen et al., 2022) is the reference point most often cited for KTVIC. Two recent systems are directly relevant. HDUCap (Hoang et al., 2026) introduces a Vietnamese dataset and a spatially-aware transformer. ViG (Vu et al., 2026) adapts transformer captioners to low-resource Vietnamese with grounding. We could not obtain the full text of either paper: on 2026-10-15 the publisher page of HDUCap returned HTTP 403, IEEE Xplore returned no content for ViG, and Crossref carries no abstract for either. We therefore do not know their split, word segmentation, CIDEr variant or scale. We cite them, report none of their numbers, and do not use them as result comparators. They become comparators only if their protocol matches ours on all four points<!-- rule: PD-7 Q5 -->. Section 3.4 shows why protocol matters: word segmentation alone moves CIDEr-D by more than the reported gap between our system and GRIT; we did not measure the effect of CIDEr variant or scale.<!-- R2 N-M7 / R1-M4: was "each of these choices moves CIDEr by more than the reported gaps"; only segmentation was measured. -->

**Reference-free scoring.** CLIPScore (Hessel et al., 2021) scores a caption against its image with an image–text model and needs no references. For Vietnamese it would need a Vietnamese image–text model. ViCLIP-OT (Tran et al., 2026) is one, trained on UIT-OpenViIC, whose training images near-duplicate 401 of the 558 KTVIC test images (Section 3.6). Scores it gives on KTVIC test are therefore at risk of leakage, and we never use it as a metric or evaluator on KTVIC test.

**Vision–language models.** Vietnamese VLMs such as Vintern-1B (Doan et al., 2024) and general VLMs such as Qwen2.5-VL (Bai et al., 2025a) write fluent Vietnamese captions zero-shot. Our system fine-tunes Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct with QLoRA (Dettmers et al., 2023) and LoRA (Hu et al., 2021). The supplement also uses Qwen3-VL (Bai et al., 2025b) and InternVL3 (Zhu et al., 2025).

**Reference-based metrics and their reporting.** CIDEr (Vedantam et al., 2015) weights n-grams by inverse document frequency over the reference set and divides by the norm of the n-gram vectors. Section 3.3 shows that this division turns an n-gram with zero document frequency, such as one containing a period the references never use, into a pure penalty. Caglayan et al. (2020) show other failure cases of the standard metrics, including high scores for degenerate system outputs. sacreBLEU (Post, 2018) made BLEU's tokenisation and reference parameters reportable through a version signature. SacreEOS (Hu et al., 2023) did the same for the end-of-sequence convention in self-critical captioning training. Our protocol card (Section 4.2) applies the same idea to Vietnamese captioning: it names the segmenter (RDRSegmenter in VnCoreNLP; Nguyen et al., 2018; Vu et al., 2018), the CIDEr variant, the scale and the punctuation policy.

**Benchmark validity and dataset documentation.** Our audit belongs to work that asks what benchmarks measure (Bowman and Dahl, 2021; Raji et al., 2021) and that asks datasets to document how they were built (Bender and Friedman, 2018; Gebru et al., 2018); the protocol card is the evaluation-side counterpart. Kilickaya et al. (2016) re-evaluated automatic caption metrics against human judgments. The closest analogue of our image-overlap audit (Section 3.6) is the train–test overlap audit of open-domain question-answering datasets by Lewis et al. (2020).<!-- R2 section 4 (verified gaps); identifiers checked 2026-10-21 (Crossref, arXiv API). -->

**Object hallucination and its measurement.** CHAIR (Rohrbach et al., 2018) counts mentioned objects missing from a gold set built from instance labels and references. POPE (Li et al., 2023) probes objects with yes/no questions. Both rely on a lexicon that maps words to classes. For Vietnamese that lexicon has to handle tone-mark placement, classifier nouns and role nouns; Section 3.5 documents three versions of ours. The companion paper (under review) studies how the completeness of the gold and the number of mentioned objects confound these metrics, on English and Vietnamese sets (Section 7.3 lists which). We use its count-matched controls only as tools, in the supplement.

**Independence of references.** Caption metrics treat the references of an image as independent samples of how people describe it. Chen et al. (2015) model human caption agreement on that basis. Rater-equivalence analyses (Resnick et al., 2021) assume independent raters. When a few annotators write most of a dataset, models learn to recognise them, and performance on examples from unseen annotators drops (Geva et al., 2019). Who writes a description also changes its content: across 31 languages, including Vietnamese, describers from closer languages mention more of the same entities (Berger and Ponti, 2025). Reference quality matters for caption evaluation in its own right (Kasai et al., 2022). Two independent caption panels for the same image can therefore legitimately disagree. KTVIC's single-writer design breaks the independence assumption by construction, and we measure how much (Section 3.2).

**Preference and reinforcement tuning for captioning.** Self-critical sequence training (Rennie et al., 2017) and its descendants optimise CIDEr directly. Reward-based methods for complete and faithful captioning add object or evidence rewards (Tang et al., 2026). The FAIR conference version (Nguyen et al., 2026b) reported that 4-bit QLoRA DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) degraded its SFT adapter. The supplement (S6) reports CIDEr-only RL, offline DPO, group-relative RFT and weight-space interpolation (Wortsman et al., 2022) on our system against count-controlled baselines; none is a contribution of this paper.
