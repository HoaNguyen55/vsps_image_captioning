**Vietnamese captioning datasets.** UIT-ViIC (Lam et al., 2020; arXiv 2002.00175) [verified] gives Vietnamese captions for sports images drawn from COCO, written by independent annotators. Its test images are COCO train2017 images, so exposure is possible for any COCO-pretrained backbone<!-- src: judge ruling CC-024 -->. Its test split has 231 images<!-- src: data/results/uitviic/uitviic_zeroshot-short.json :: n_images -->. KTVIC (Pham, Nguyen, Vuong and Ha, 2024; arXiv 2401.08100) [verified] covers the life domain with 4,327 images and 21,635 captions<!-- src: literature: KTVIC paper / FAIR 2026 §IV-A -->. Its annotation interface asks one annotator to enter five captions per image; there are three annotators in total and a revision pass by two researchers (§3.2, Fig. 5). UIT-OpenViIC (Bui, Nguyen and Nguyen, Signal Processing: Image Communication 140:117430, 2026; doi 10.1016/j.image.2025.117430) [verified; R13_OpenViIC_SPIC] is a larger open-domain benchmark. Its image pool overlaps KTVIC's (Section 3.6), and we use it only through aggregate scores. The Vietnamese part of Crossmodal-3600 (XM3600; Thapliyal et al., EMNLP 2022) [VERIFY] provides a third, independently written reference set that we use for cross-corpus checks. Its images come from Open Images, so exposure is possible here too<!-- src: judge ruling CC-024 -->.

**Vietnamese captioning systems.** Published KTVIC results come from transformer captioners; GRIT (Nguyen et al., ECCV 2022) [verified] is the reference point most often cited for KTVIC. Two recent systems are directly relevant. HDUCap (Hoang, Nguyen, Pham and Nguyen, JVCIR 121:104963, 2026; doi 10.1016/j.jvcir.2026.104963) [verified metadata; R13_HDUCap_JVCIR] introduces a Vietnamese dataset and a spatially-aware transformer. ViG (Vu, Ngoc, Dinh and Do, MAPR 2026, pp. 652–657; doi 10.1109/mapr72750.2026.11685699) [verified metadata; R13_ViG_MAPR] adapts transformer captioners to low-resource Vietnamese with grounding. We could not read the full text of either paper: on 2026-10-15 the publisher page of HDUCap returned HTTP 403, IEEE Xplore returned no content for ViG, and Crossref carries no abstract for either. We therefore do not know their split, word segmentation, CIDEr variant or scale. We cite them, report none of their numbers, and do not use them as result comparators. They become comparators only if their protocol matches ours on all four points (PD-7 Q5). TODO: read both in full before 2026-11-01. Section 3.4 shows why protocol matters: each of these choices moves CIDEr by more than the reported gaps between systems.

**A reference-free scorer we do not use.** ViCLIP-OT (Tran et al., 2026; arXiv 2602.22678) [verified; R13_2602_22678] is a Vietnamese vision–language model trained on UIT-OpenViIC (whose training images near-duplicate 401 of the 558 KTVIC test images). It could serve as a reference-free caption scorer, but its training images overlap most of the KTVIC test images, so scores it gives on KTVIC test are at risk of leakage. We never use it as a metric or evaluator on KTVIC test.

Vietnamese VLMs such as Vintern-1B (Doan et al., 2024; arXiv 2408.12480) [verified] and general VLMs such as Qwen2.5-VL (Qwen Team, 2025; arXiv 2502.13923) [verified] write fluent Vietnamese captions zero-shot. Our system fine-tunes Qwen2.5-VL-7B with QLoRA (Dettmers et al., NeurIPS 2023) [verified] and LoRA (Hu et al., 2021; arXiv 2106.09685) [verified]. The second backbone is Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct [VERIFY: technical report identifier].

**Reference-based metrics and their reporting.** CIDEr (Vedantam et al., CVPR 2015) [verified] weights n-grams by inverse document frequency over the reference set and divides by the norm of the n-gram vectors. Section 3.3 shows that this division turns an n-gram with zero document frequency, such as one containing a period the references never use, into a pure penalty. sacreBLEU (Post, 2018; arXiv 1804.08771) [verified] made BLEU's tokenisation and reference parameters reportable through a version signature. SacreEOS (Hu, Cavicchioli and Capotondi, ICIAP 2023; arXiv 2305.12254) [verified] did the same for the end-of-sequence convention in self-critical captioning training. Our protocol card (Section 4.2) applies the same idea to Vietnamese: it names the segmenter (RDRSegmenter in VnCoreNLP; Nguyen et al., LREC 2018; Vu et al., NAACL 2018 demos) [verified], the CIDEr variant, the scale and the punctuation policy.

**Object hallucination and its measurement.** CHAIR (Rohrbach et al., EMNLP 2018) [verified] counts mentioned objects missing from a gold set built from instance labels and references. POPE (Li et al., 2023; arXiv 2305.10355) [verified] probes objects with yes/no questions. Both rely on a lexicon that maps words to classes. For Vietnamese that lexicon has to handle tone-mark placement, classifier nouns and role nouns; Section 3.5 documents three versions of ours. The companion paper (under review) studies how the number of mentioned objects confounds these metrics on English benchmarks. Here we use its count-matched controls only as tools.

**Independence of references.** Caption metrics treat the references of an image as independent samples of how people describe it. Chen et al. (2015; arXiv 1504.00325) [verified] model human caption agreement on that basis. Rater-equivalence analyses (Resnick et al.; arXiv 2106.01254) [verified] assume independent raters. Who writes a description also changes its content: across 31 languages, including Vietnamese, describers from closer languages mention more of the same entities (Berger et al., NAACL 2025; arXiv 2409.16646) [verified; R13_2409_16646]. Two independent caption panels for the same image can therefore legitimately disagree. KTVIC's single-writer design breaks this assumption by construction, and we measure how much (Section 3.2).

**Preference and reinforcement tuning for captioning.** Self-critical sequence training and its descendants optimise CIDEr directly. Reward-based methods for faithful captioning add object or evidence rewards (VES-RFT; CCCaption) [verified]. The FAIR conference version reported that 4-bit QLoRA DPO degraded its SFT adapter. Section 6 tests CIDEr-only RL, offline DPO and group-relative RFT on the response-only P1 system against count-controlled baselines.
