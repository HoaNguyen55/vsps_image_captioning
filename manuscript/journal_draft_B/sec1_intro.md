Public Vietnamese image-captioning benchmarks include UIT-ViIC (Lam et al., 2020), KTVIC (Pham et al., 2024) and UIT-OpenViIC (Bui et al., 2026), with further sets for narrower domains (Section 2). Systems are compared on them mainly by CIDEr. On KTVIC a fine-tuned vision–language model (VLM) now scores far above the reported transformer baseline. Our response-only fine-tune of Qwen2.5-VL-7B reaches a word-level CIDEr-D of 148.23 (SD 0.38 over three seeds)<!-- src: .session_backup/2026-10-15/paperA_keys/cider_3seed_mean.json :: mean ; sd_sample_ddof1 -->; GRIT is reported at 136.0, under a protocol we could not match (Section 3.4)<!-- src: literature: FAIR 2026 Table I (GRIT as reported for KTVIC); protocol not matched -->. Gaps of this size invite a leaderboard reading. This paper asks what a KTVIC CIDEr score, and a gain in it, measures, and how much of a difference between two systems is about the image. Our main finding is positive and specific. On development data with one backbone, more KTVIC training data raises CIDEr-D against KTVIC references by +9.61 [7.18, 12.36] per doubling, and that gain registers mainly on KTVIC's own references rather than on a second caption panel of the same images (Sections 5.4, 5.5).

**A low-resource setting.** In vision–language work, Vietnamese is a low-resource language. Most of the world's languages have little labelled data compared with English (Joshi et al., 2020), and Southeast Asian languages remain thinly covered by public multimodal data (Lovenia et al., 2024). Vietnamese captioning rests on a few benchmarks of a few thousand images each (Lam et al., 2020; Pham et al., 2024; Bui et al., 2026), and captioning data in languages other than English are often small or translated from English (van Miltenburg, 2024). In this setting one benchmark carries most of the evidence for a new system. A gain that would be cross-checked on several datasets in English is here often checked on KTVIC alone. A second set of captions, written separately for the same images, is the cheapest available check, but people from different language communities, and different annotation teams, describe the same images differently (Buettner and Kovashka, 2024; Buettner et al., 2025; Berger and Ponti, 2025). This makes it important to know what a KTVIC score, and a gain in it, measures.<!-- C13 low-resource framing in own words; refs verified 2026-10-29: joshi2020state doi 10.18653/v1/2020.acl-main.560 (Crossref); lovenia2024seacrowd arXiv 2406.10118 (EMNLP 2024); buettner2024quantifying arXiv 2410.02027 (EMNLP 2024 short); buettner2025multimodal arXiv 2504.14359 (IJCNLP-AACL 2025) -->

**What a training-data gain measures.** On 300 development images, each doubling of KTVIC training images raises CIDEr-D by +9.61 [7.18, 12.36] (registered, PASS). This is the three-seed confirmation of an earlier single-seed development result (+9.40), and the pass bar (CI lower bound above +1) was set below it. On the 187 development images that also have UIT-OpenViIC captions, the gain is +10.59 [7.43, 13.81] per doubling against KTVIC references and +0.72 [−0.83, 2.36] against the OpenViIC panel, a gap of +9.87 [6.71, 13.35]. Gains from more KTVIC training captions register mainly on KTVIC's own references. In a two-seed reverse pilot, adapters trained on OpenViIC captions of KTVIC images gain +2.98 [0.94, 4.88] on the OpenViIC panel. Their gain against KTVIC references, +1.29 [−1.23, 3.75], is not distinguishable from zero or from the own-panel slope, and the reverse gap, +1.69 [−0.72, 3.91], is not distinguishable from zero; the registered equivalence bound on that gap is weak, because its margin (4.93) exceeds the reverse own-panel slope. These are panel-specific score associations, not a mechanism: writer, annotators and protocol cannot be separated. They stay at the development tier (one backbone), KTVIC test is closed under our one-look rule, and confirmation is left to a future single registered look on unseen data (Sections 5.4, 5.5, 7.1).<!-- src: number_manifest_B.tsv (B-1 decide b44a76fe P-B1 ; D0 d0_decide 15dd925f primary.b_K ; b_K_ci95 ; b_O ; b_O_ci95 ; g ; g_ci95 ; fixes_for_full_study.P1_SESOI ; B-R1 decide a6627047 primary.b_O_own ; b_O_ci95 ; b_K_other ; b_K_ci95 ; g_R ; g_R_ci95, outcome ASYMMETRIC/O5b ; +9.40 = supplementary_B.md S7 'Training-set size' / PREREG 2026-10-28_b1_scaling l.150) ; merged B+D, PI-1029n ; BD revision 2026-10-29 items 1, 2, 5, 10, 12 -->

**Relation to earlier work.** A conference version (L.M.H. Nguyen et al., 2026) introduced VSPS, which builds supervision for detailed Vietnamese captioning from verified propositions. It reported the P1 gold-SFT control on KTVIC (seed 42) as a baseline, and it already noted that KTVIC CHAIR values are upper bounds because the gold comes from the references, and that P1's precision comes with short, reference-style captions (FAIR §IV-C, §IV-E). **What we do not claim.** We claim no state of the art on KTVIC: we report no published Vietnamese system's score under our protocol as a comparison: the released outputs of two KTVIC baselines enter only the rank correlation of Section 3.4 and the punctuation audit of Section 3.3. The three external systems of Section 3.7 are scored descriptively, each on its own test split, and are compared neither with each other nor with our systems. GRIT's 136.0 comes from a different protocol. We make no claim about human judgments of caption quality, because no new human annotation was collected. Model-generated labels and votes serve only as method components, never as evidence. We do not claim that gains from training on one panel's captions reach the other panel's references, and every two-panel result is on development data.
